# Round 02 · Stable Andrews–Curtis proof

2026-11-19. A set of selected geometric relators with a free quotient of the expected rank can be normalized using only those relators. The entire presentation may lack a geometric realization, and the selected set may contain no primitive relator. This gives a criterion for combining successive lower-rank certificates.

## Normalizing a geometric subtuple

Let $F_g$ be a marked free group of rank $g$. A tuple is geometric if its conjugacy classes have simultaneous disjoint simple closed representatives on the boundary of a marked orientable genus-$g$ handlebody. Let $P=(p_1,\ldots,p_k)$ be geometric, with $0\leq k\leq g$, and write $N=\langle\!\langle P\rangle\!\rangle$ for its normal closure in $F_g$. Assume $F_g/N$ is free of rank $g-k$. In this setting the following hold.

1. Attaching the $k$ 2-handles of any geometric realization of $P$ to a genus-$g$ handlebody produces a genus-$(g-k)$ handlebody.
2. Ordinary Andrews–Curtis moves supported on the $P$ rows take $P$ to a subset of a free basis of $F_g$. Inversions, independent conjugations and products use the $P$ rows alone. The ambient marking stays fixed.

**Proof of the attachment statement.** Let $M$ be the 2-handle addition. The images of the $p_i$ in $H_1(H;\mathbb Q)$ are independent because the abelianization of the quotient has dimension $g-k$. Their disjoint representatives therefore define independent classes on $\partial H$ as well. Cutting an orientable surface along a disjoint family with independent homology classes leaves it connected. Indeed, if the cut surface has more than one component, take the oriented boundary of a component adjacent to a different one across a cut circle. Its image in the original surface is a relation in which at least one of the cut-circle classes has coefficient $\pm1$. Compress the connected cut surface by capping its new boundary circles. This gives the connected boundary of $M$, of genus $n=g-k$.

By van Kampen, $\pi_1(M)\cong F_n$. Doubling the compact orientable manifold $M$ along its boundary gives $\chi(M)=\chi(\partial M)/2=1-n$. Hence $b_2(M)=0$ since $b_1(M)=n$. An embedded sphere in the interior of $M$ must separate. A nonseparating sphere has a closed oriented transversal of algebraic intersection one, contrary to the vanishing of $H_2(M;\mathbb Q)$.

Cap the two sides of a separating sphere by balls and call them $M_0$ and $V$, where $M_0$ has the original boundary, and $V$ is closed. Van Kampen gives $\pi_1(M)=\pi_1(M_0)*\pi_1(V)$, so both factors are free. The same Euler characteristic formula gives $b_1(M_0)=n+b_2(M_0)\geq n$. Since $b_1(M)=n$ and first Betti numbers add in this free product, $b_1(V)=0$. A free group with first Betti number zero is trivial. By Poincaré, $V$ is a 3-sphere, so the original sphere bounds a ball in $M$. Thus $M$ is irreducible. The handlebody criterion for compact orientable irreducible 3-manifolds with free fundamental group and nonempty boundary now gives the claim. [Hempel, Theorem 5.2](https://doi.org/10.1090/chel/349).

**Proof of the move statement.** Shrink $H$ by a boundary collar. Together with the collar and 2-handles on the other side, it gives a Heegaard splitting $M=H\cup C$ in which $C$ is a compression body. For $n=0$, cap the sphere boundary to work in $S^3$ with $C$ a handlebody. The disks on the $C$ side with boundary curves $P$ cut $C$ to $\partial_-C\times I$ for $n>0$, or to a ball for $n=0$. They form a defining disk system. Waldhausen's theorem says this splitting is a stabilization of the boundary-parallel splitting of the handlebody, or the usual standard splitting when $n=0$. The genus difference is $k$, so the standard picture has $k$ defining disks on the $C$ side with dual disks on the $H$ side. In this picture their boundary classes represent $k$ members of a basis of $\pi_1(H)$. One can read them using the dual meridian disks in the stabilizing summands and a full meridian system in the lower-genus handlebody. Transport this defining system to the given splitting. [Waldhausen, §4.3 and Theorem 3.1](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/download/1782178/2313707/Waldhausen_020.pdf).

Bonahon's disk-slide theorem connects the original defining system in the same compression body to the transported one. A slide replaces one boundary loop by its product with a conjugate of a different boundary loop, up to choice of orientation and basepoint. Conjugate or invert the donor temporarily, multiply the recipient, and restore the donor. Isotopies give the same conjugacy classes. These are ordinary moves on $P$ in the original marked $H$, so the resulting partial basis is reached without changing the ambient generators. No other relators are used, even if the slide arcs meet their curves, since only the selected curves enter this argument. [Bonahon, Appendix B](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.1448/), [Lackenby, proof of Theorem 6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06122v1).

## Descent and a certificate assembled in blocks

If a full tuple $(P,U)$ is geometric, attach the $P$ handles in a simultaneous geometric realization. The curves for $U$ lie outside their attaching annuli, so they remain disjoint on $\partial M$ and represent the images of $U$ in $F_g/N$. The attachment statement therefore gives a geometric projected tuple. For a balanced trivial input, its projected tuple is also balanced and trivial. Individual selected rows can all be nonprimitive.

Conversely, to certify **ordinary triviality** of a balanced trivial tuple $(P,U)$ it suffices to give a geometric $P$ in the ambient rank, a free quotient of rank $g-|P|$, and an ordinary trivialization of the quotient tuple $\bar U$. The lower-rank certificate may come from geometry or use a further block. To see this, first normalize $P$ to a partial basis $B$, leaving $U$ literally fixed. Extend $B$ to a basis $(B,C)$ of $F_g$, so the image of $C$ is a basis of the quotient. Append quotient Nielsen moves to end the lower-rank certificate at that particular basis. Lift its relator moves to $U$ in $F_g$ using arbitrary preimages of conjugators. The lifted endpoint differs coordinatewise from $C$ by elements of $N=\langle\!\langle B\rangle\!\rangle$. Clear each difference by multiplying by individual conjugates of the $B$ rows and restoring the donors. The resulting full basis is Nielsen equivalent to the marked standard basis. The full tuple is allowed to be nongeometric throughout this test.

For a stable lower-rank certificate, first use peak-rank normalization to turn it into an ordinary certificate in a fixed padded rank. Append the same isolated pairs in the original ambient group and apply the argument there. At the marked standard basis, delete the added pairs. This gives stable triviality of $(P,U)$. [Peak-rank normalization](round-01-stable-disproof.md#fixed-rank-normalization).

A useful free-quotient witness for a padded presentation is an explicit retraction $\rho:F(X,Z)\to F(X)$, given by $z_i\mapsto a_i(X)$, whose kernel is the normal closure of the selected rows. To certify that equality, evaluate the selected rows under $\rho$ and give normal-closure factorizations of each $z_i a_i(X)^{-1}$ using those rows. This avoids having to recognize an arbitrary finite presentation of a free group. When such a $P$ is found on an ordinary path in the padded rank, the block criterion is a stable-AC search target. The two tests can use different geometric realizations. They require a certified free quotient and actual ordinary paths where moves are claimed.

## Two controls

Use uppercase letters for inverse letters. The first control exhibits a loss of geometricity with an unchanged projection. Let $c=xy$ and take $(x,y)$. Conjugate $x$ by $c$, right-multiply the second row by that donor, and restore $x$. The result is $(x,yxyxYX)$. Killing $x$ projects both endpoints to $(y)$. The ambient automorphism $y\mapsto yX$ and $x\mapsto x$ sends the final pair to $(x,yyxYX)$, which has the simple Whitehead graph $K_4$. Both inverse vertex pairs have degree and minimum separating cut three, proving Whitehead minimality. The tuple has determinant one and minimum cyclic length six, so it is disk-busting. A nontrivial splitting of $F_2$ would instead make the two classes powers of separate basis elements, forcing total minimal length two. The independent planar-link replay checks a one-equation contradiction in the synchronized rotations of this minimal $K_4$. Zieschang's minimal-diagram theorem excludes geometricity in every marking. Thus a single product by a conjugated pivot can change geometry even when the lower-rank projection is constant. [Rank-two geometricity criterion and parity convention](round-01-stable-proof.md), [Manning, Theorem 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.0724).

For a control without any primitive row, put $P=(xxYYY,xxxYYYY)$ in $F(x,y,z)$ and let $c=xZXYz$. Set $s=zcp_1c^{-1}=zxZXYzxxYYYZyxzX$. The full tuple is $(P,s)$. The earlier paired-rotation witness gives a geometric realization of $P$ in the genus-two summand, and its 35-move ordinary path normalizes $P$ to $(x,y)$. This path also runs in $F(x,y,z)$ with $s$ fixed. Killing the block gives $F(z)$ with projected relator $z$. After the two donor rows become $(x,y)$, the five letters of $p_1^{-1}$, conjugated by $c$, clear $cp_1c^{-1}$ from the third row. This realizes the block certificate directly. [Earlier planar witness](../certificates/w_geometric_nonprimitive_planar.json), [earlier ordinary path](../certificates/w_geometric_nonprimitive_full.json).

The Whitehead cut minima of the individual words $p_1,p_2,s$ in $F(x,y,z)$ are $(2,3,0),(3,4,0),(6,5,5)$, respectively, in generator order $(x,y,z)$. Each entry equals the corresponding vertex degree, so all three words are cyclically length-minimal in their automorphism orbits. Their lengths are greater than one, excluding a primitive coordinate. The Whitehead graph of $s$ contains $K_{3,3}$ with sides $(X,x,y)$ and $(Y,Z,z)$. Minimality and nonplanarity exclude any geometric realization of $s$, hence of the full tuple. Thus block normalization applies beyond the full-tuple geometricity criterion and beyond elimination of a displayed primitive relator. [Manning, Theorems 2.3 and 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.0724).

## Interaction with the other tracks

The ordinary proof track's moving triangular frames use selected relators whose quotient is a known free group. The attachment theorem can project a geometric endpoint even if an alternative set of selected rows has no sequence of exposed primitive pivots. A peak-rank stable solver can also score row blocks for a geometric realization and a certified free quotient, then try to solve the smaller projected tuple. For initial AK(3), any marked block extension projecting back to its unchanged initial tuple still fails the full geometricity test by descent. The existence of a reachable padded tuple with a successful block certificate for AK(3) remains open.
