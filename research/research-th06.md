# Research TH06: arbitrary-conjugator AC geometry of nilpotent, metabelian and pro-p quotients — a universal 8-move bound, and what is left for conjugator length

Date: 2026-11-09 (Los Angeles). Independent research agent TH06 (round 5, theory round).

**Status (draft, being written incrementally):** theory results with complete proofs. **No AC trivialization of AK(3), no proof of non-triviality, no counterexample, nothing on the full conjecture.** Every statement is labelled *proved here*, *externally reported*, *experiment* or *open*. No novelty claim is made.

Headline (proved here): if conjugation of one coordinate by an **arbitrary** group element is counted as one move, then in **every nilpotent group** (hence every finite p-group, every N_c = F/γ_{c+1}, every P_c^{(p)}), in every 2-generator metabelian group, and in the free pro-p group, every generating pair with the standard abelianization is at distance **at most 8** from the standard pair, by one fixed scheme of 4 conjugations and 4 multiplications. So the unbounded quantity in E's Corollary 4 is *only* the word length of conjugators, never the number of generalized moves; the pro-p completion "sees" nothing but the exponent matrix. In contrast (proved here), if only conjugations by *powers* x^k, y^k (and multiplications by powers) are counted as single moves, the nilpotent chain remains faithful: bounded distances along N_c still force AC-triviality.

## 1. Conventions

F = F(x,y), X = x⁻¹, Y = y⁻¹; g^h = h⁻¹gh, [g,h] = g⁻¹h⁻¹gh. For a group Q, γ_1(Q) = Q, γ_{m+1}(Q) = [γ_m(Q), Q]. N_c = F/γ_{c+1}(F); P_c^{(p)} is E's finite p-group (image of F in the units of 𝔽_p⟨X,Y⟩/(degree > c)).

A *pair* of Q is an element of Q × Q. **Generalized instructions** (the move set M*): the 7 "Nielsen" instructions of E (invert u_0 or u_1; swap; u_0 ← u_0u_1^{±1}; u_1 ← u_1u_0^{±1}) and, for every g ∈ Q, the conjugations u_0 ← u_0^g and u_1 ← u_1^g, **each counted as one move**. d*_Q(P, P′) is the M*-distance. For a quotient π: F → Q with a = π(x), b = π(y), d*_Q(U) := d*_Q(π(U), (a,b)).

Every M*-move in F is a composite of ordinary AC moves, and E's 15 instructions are M*-moves, so (E's Fact 0) d*_F(U) < ∞ iff U is ordinary-AC-trivial, and d*_Q ≤ d_Q (E's letter distance) for every Q. M*-moves commute with homomorphisms *onto* Q (a conjugator in Q lifts), so d*_Q ≤ d*_{Q′} when Q is a quotient of Q′.

ℓ(U) := d_{ℤ²}(U) = d_{N_1}(U), the number of Nielsen instructions needed to bring the exponent matrix M_U ∈ GL₂(ℤ) to the identity (conjugations act trivially on ℤ²). E's table gives ℓ(AK(1)) = 3, ℓ(AK(2)) = 4, ℓ(AK(3)) = 5, ℓ(AK(4)) = 6.

## 2. Theorem 1: the universal 8-move bound (proved here)

**Lemma 1.1 (the two-conjugation lemma).** Let Q be a nilpotent group, and p, q ∈ Q such that Q = ⟨p, q⟩γ_2(Q) (equivalently Q = ⟨p,q⟩). Then for every e ∈ γ_2(Q) there are g, g′ ∈ Q with

    (p^g · q)^{g′} · q⁻¹ = p·e.

*Proof.* Write L_m = γ_m(Q)/γ_{m+1}(Q) (additively). Standard facts, valid in every group: (i) the commutator induces a bi-additive map Q/γ_2 × L_m → L_{m+1}, written [ā, h̄]; (ii) L_{m+1} is generated by the elements [ā, h̄]. Since Q/γ_2 is generated by p̄, q̄, bi-additivity gives

    (*)  L_{m+1} = [p̄, L_m] + [q̄, L_m]   (every element is [p̄, α] + [q̄, β] with α, β ∈ L_m).

Let c be the class of Q. We prove by induction on m = 1, …, c the statement S(m): there are g, g′ with (p^g q)^{g′} q⁻¹ = p·e·δ for some δ ∈ γ_{m+1}(Q). S(1) holds with g = g′ = 1 (then δ = e⁻¹ ∈ γ_2), and S(c) is the lemma because γ_{c+1}(Q) = 1.

Assume S(m), m < c, with g, g′, δ. Let h, h′ ∈ γ_m(Q), to be chosen, and put w = p^g q. Then p^{gh} = p^g·[p^g, h], hence p^{gh}q = w·[p^g,h]^q, and

    (p^{gh} q)^{g′h′} = (w^{g′})^{h′} · [p^g,h]^{q g′ h′} = w^{g′} · [w^{g′}, h′] · [p^g,h]^{q g′ h′}.

Both commutators lie in γ_{m+1}(Q), which is central modulo γ_{m+2}(Q). By (i), [p^g,h]^{qg′h′} ≡ [p,h] and [w^{g′},h′] ≡ [pq,h′] modulo γ_{m+2}, because p^g ≡ p and w^{g′} ≡ pq modulo γ_2. Therefore

    (p^{gh} q)^{g′h′} q⁻¹ ≡ w^{g′} q⁻¹ · [p,h]·[pq,h′] = p·e·δ·[p,h]·[pq,h′]   (mod γ_{m+2}(Q)).

In L_{m+1} the class of [p,h][pq,h′] is [p̄, h̄ + h̄′] + [q̄, h̄′]. By (*) write −δ̄ = [p̄, α] + [q̄, β]; choose h′ ∈ γ_m with h̄′ = β and h ∈ γ_m with h̄ = α − β. Then the new error δ·[p,h][pq,h′] lies in γ_{m+2}(Q), which is S(m+1) for the conjugators gh, g′h′. ∎

**Theorem 1.** Let Q be a nilpotent group generated by a, b, and let e_0, e_1 ∈ γ_2(Q) be arbitrary. Then there are g_1, g_2, g_3, g_4 ∈ Q such that the following 8 generalized instructions transform (a, b) into (a·e_0, b·e_1):

    u_0 ← u_0^{g_1};  u_0 ← u_0u_1;  u_0 ← u_0^{g_2};  u_0 ← u_0u_1⁻¹;
    u_1 ← u_1^{g_3};  u_1 ← u_1u_0;  u_1 ← u_1^{g_4};  u_1 ← u_1u_0⁻¹.

Consequently, for every c ≥ 1, every prime p and every pair U of elements of F with unimodular exponent matrix (in particular every balanced presentation of the trivial group on x, y):

    ℓ(U) ≤ d*_{N_c}(U) ≤ ℓ(U) + 8,   d*_{P_c^{(p)}}(U) ≤ ℓ(U) + 8,   d*_Q(U) ≤ ℓ(U) + 8 for every nilpotent quotient Q of F.

For AK(3): d*_{N_c}(AK(3)) ≤ 13 for **all** c, whereas the letter distance d_{N_c}(AK(3)) is ≥ 14 already at c = 5 (E) and tends to d_F(AK(3)) ≥ 15.

*Proof.* Lemma 1.1 with (p,q) = (a,b), e = e_0 gives g_1, g_2 with (a^{g_1} b)^{g_2} b⁻¹ = a e_0: after the first four instructions the pair is (a e_0, b). Now apply Lemma 1.1 with (p,q) = (b, a e_0) — these generate Q modulo γ_2 — and e = e_1: there are g_3, g_4 with (b^{g_3}·a e_0)^{g_4}·(a e_0)⁻¹ = b e_1, which is what the last four instructions compute. For the consequences: ℓ(U) Nielsen instructions bring π(U) to a pair (a e_0, b e_1) with e_i ∈ γ_2(Q) (Nielsen instructions act on exponent matrices by row operations, and elements of N_c with the same image in ℤ² differ by an element of γ_2); then run the 8 instructions backwards. The lower bound is the case Q = ℤ². The value ℓ(AK(3)) = 5 is E's d_{N_1}. ∎

**Remarks (proved here).**
1. *Unimodularity is all that is used.* The pair U need not normally generate F: Theorem 1 applies to (x e_0, y e_1) for arbitrary e_i ∈ [F,F]. In nilpotent quotients "normally generates" and "generates" coincide, and both are equivalent to det M_U = ±1.
2. *Area.* Reading the scheme backwards, b is a product of at most 3, and a of at most 7, conjugates of (a e_0)^{±1}, (b e_1)^{±1}. So in every nilpotent quotient of F the "area" of the generators with respect to U is bounded by a universal constant (after the ℓ(U) abelian moves): Dehn-function-type lower bounds on AC paths (Bridson; Lishak — externally reported) are invisible in all nilpotent quotients.
3. *ACM moves.* For G/O's exact generalized moves (replace u_0 by anything conjugate to u_0^{±1} modulo ⟨⟨u_1⟩⟩) the nilpotent picture is even more degenerate: Q/⟨⟨u_1⟩⟩ is cyclic, so γ_2(Q) ⊆ ⟨⟨u_1⟩⟩ and two ACM moves suffice. Theorem 1 is the finer statement that boundedly many *single* conjugations and multiplications suffice.
4. *n generators.* The same proof gives, for Q = ⟨a_1,…,a_n⟩ nilpotent and e_i ∈ γ_2(Q), a path from (a_i) to (a_ie_i) with n conjugations and 2(n−1) multiplications per coordinate: conjugate a_i, then for each j ≠ i multiply by a_j, conjugate, multiply by a_j⁻¹; the linear terms [ā_i,h_0] + Σ_j [ā_i+ā_j, h_j] exhaust L_{m+1}.
