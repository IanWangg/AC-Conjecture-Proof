# Round 04 · Stable Andrews–Curtis disproof

2026-11-19. A two-incoming auxiliary cut can be removed when each adjacent pair of its three deletion quotients admits a common lifted basis. The two bases used in the middle quotient may differ. An exact out–in–in–out transfer illustrates the splice, and a second transfer shows that the middle quotient can be nonfree. With unchanged endpoint marking, the out–in–in–out order always permits deletion without increasing the ordinary move count.

## Deleting a block across two quotient changes

Use the normalized ordinary section in the ambient free group $F$, as in the [round-three cut analysis](round-03-stable-disproof.md). Absorb swaps into the row indices. Let $T$ be a nonempty block of auxiliary rows and let $A,B$ be its initial and terminal sets of marked generators. An incoming product updates a row in $T$ using an exterior donor. An outgoing product uses a donor in $T$ to update an exterior row. Suppose there are exactly two incoming products. The normal closure in $F$ of the $T$ rows is constant on each of the three intervals separated by these two moves. Inversions, conjugations and internal products preserve that normal closure, and outgoing products leave the $T$ rows alone. Write the three normal closures as $K_A=\langle\!\langle A\rangle\!\rangle$, $K_M$ and $K_B=\langle\!\langle B\rangle\!\rangle$. Let $r=\operatorname{rank}(F)-|T|$.

**Deletion criterion.** Suppose all three quotients are free of rank $r$. For each of the two adjacent pairs, suppose there are $r$ ambient words whose images form a free basis in both quotients of that pair. Then the padded start can reach a basis with the whole block $T$ removed.

To check one incoming boundary, let $K,K'$ be the adjacent normal closures and let $q$ be the exterior donor. The receiver changes from $p$ to $pq^\epsilon$ for $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, and all other rows stay fixed. It follows that

$$
K\vee\langle\!\langle q\rangle\!\rangle
=K'\vee\langle\!\langle q\rangle\!\rangle
$$

where $\vee$ denotes the normal subgroup generated by both subgroups. Identify $F/K$ and $F/K'$ with the same abstract $F_r$ by using the common lifted basis. After killing the image of $q$, the two identifications give the same marked quotient. Indeed, the maps from $F_r$ to this quotient agree on the basis because each basis letter comes from the same ambient word on either side. Therefore the two donor images generate equal normal closures in $F_r$. The two projected exterior tuples also agree coordinate by coordinate in the common quotient. By Magnus's normal-closure theorem, the donor images are conjugate up to inversion. Normalize the two donor rows to the same word, use it to correct each other row by products of its conjugates, and undo the normalization at the target end. In each correction, temporarily invert or conjugate the donor as needed and restore it after the multiplication. If the donor image is trivial then the two exterior tuples already agree. [Magnus's theorem](https://msp.org/gtm/2008/14/gtm-2008-14-004s.pdf).

Project the three intervals to their respective free quotients on the exterior rows. Every outgoing product disappears because its donor vanishes. Each surviving conjugation can be carried out letter by letter in the chosen free basis. At the two incoming boundaries use the splices just proved. Transport between the two bases of $F/K_M$ by the free basis isomorphism, applying the resulting change of coordinates to the whole subsequent path. Under these identifications the starting quotient is the original reduced-rank free group, and the terminal exterior tuple is a free basis of it. A Nielsen path takes that basis to the marked generators. This yields an ordinary path on the retained rows, so the padded start uses $|T|$ fewer auxiliary pairs. The argument gives no bound on the length of the splices.

At the minimum feasible positive padding rank, any cut with two incoming moves must therefore fail either the middle freeness hypothesis or one of the two common-basis hypotheses. For a singleton transfer $\{a\}\to\{b\}$, both hypotheses hold if the middle normal closure is normally generated by a cyclically reduced word with exactly one occurrence of each of $a$ and $b$, counted with either sign. Eliminating $a$ makes the complement of $a$ a basis in the middle and initial quotients. Eliminating $b$ supplies the corresponding basis for the middle and terminal quotients.

## The out–in–in–out order with unchanged marking

Suppose $A=B$ and there are no outgoing products between the two incoming products. Use the fixed projection killing the generators in $A$ and discard the rows of $T$. Every outgoing product occurs where the $T$ normal closure is $K_A$, so its donor projects to the identity. Incoming products and internal moves on $T$ have no effect on the retained rows. Every other move projects to at most one move in the strict metric, since the fixed projection sends each marked letter to a marked letter or the identity. This deletes the block and saves at least the two incoming moves from the ordinary section. The argument also works in the finite characteristic-two Magnus graphs under their marked generator deletions.

In particular, a minimal-padding path with positive padding, or a minimum-length stable path, can have an out–in–in–out cut only if its marking changes. With two products in each direction a marking change transfers exactly one generator, by the [round-three crossing bound](round-03-stable-disproof.md). The in–in–out–out and out–out–in–in orders were already excluded in round three. For unchanged marking, the remaining orders with two crossings in each direction are in–out–in–out, out–in–out–in, and in–out–out–in.

## Exact transfer controls

Start with $(y,x,z)$ and place the third row in $T$. Use the first outgoing product to replace $y$ by $yz^{-1}$, and conjugate it by $z^{-1}$ to obtain $z^{-1}y$. Conjugate the second row to $W=txt^{-1}$, then multiply the first row by $W$. The first incoming product uses $z^{-1}yW$ as donor and changes $z$ to $yW$. The second uses $W^{-1}$ and leaves $y$ in $T$. Restore the exterior rows to $(z^{-1}y,x)$. The final outgoing product and an inversion give $(z,x,y)$.

Take $t=1$. The middle quotient has relator $yx$. The initial coordinate basis $(x,y)$ collapses there because $y=x^{-1}$. The ambient pair $(x,yz)$ is a common lifted basis at the first boundary, since $yz$ maps to $y$ in the initial quotient and to $x^{-1}z$ in the middle. The pair $(x,z)$ is common at the second boundary. Writing $X=x^{-1}$ and $Y=y^{-1}$ in each abstract two-generator basis, the first splice is $(yx,x)\to(YX,x)$ by inversion and conjugation. The change of marking sends the abstract $y$ to $x^{-1}y$, giving $(Y,x)$. The second splice is $(Y,x)\to(Yx,x)$ by one product. The original padded path has eight strict moves.

Take $t=xy$. The middle relator is $yxyxy^{-1}x^{-1}$. With $u=yx$, it reads $u^2(xux^{-1})^{-1}$. The relation $xux^{-1}=u^2$ has a realization on the rationals by $x(s)=2s$ and $u(s)=s+1$, so $u$ survives in the middle quotient. No nontrivial free-group element is conjugate to its square, as seen by cyclically reduced length. Thus the middle quotient is nonfree. The original padded path has twelve strict moves, and the two incoming donors both project to $x$ after killing $y,z$. Equality in that smaller quotient does not ensure freeness of the middle deletion quotient.
