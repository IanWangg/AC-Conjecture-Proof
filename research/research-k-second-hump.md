## Theorem and candidate family

**Theorem.** No primitive element of F(x,y) has image conjugate to r or r^-1 in

    G=<x,y | YxYXyyx=1>.

Thus a single ACM replacement of the r coordinate with u held fixed cannot produce a primitive free relator, even at arbitrary replacement length.

Write t=y, a=x, d=yxY. The fixed relation is equivalent to

    alpha(a)=d,   alpha(d)=a d^-1,
    G=F(a,d) semidirect_alpha Z.

Indeed, in the stable-letter notation u=t^-1 a t^-1 a^-1 t^2 a; applying alpha^2 to its fiber relation gives alpha(a) a^-1 alpha^2(a)=1 and hence alpha^2(a)=a alpha(a)^-1. The inverse automorphism is alpha^-1(a)=d a, alpha^-1(d)=a.

The inverse of the remaining relator has normal form

    r^-1=yXXyXYxx=(d^-1 a^-1 d^2)t=W t.

For an exact check, r=a^-2 d a^2 t^-1, so r^-1=alpha(a^-2 d^-1 a^2)t=d^-1 a^-1 d^2 t.

The homomorphism chi sends x to zero and y to one. A proposed primitive can be inverted to have chi-value one. Its exponent vector is then (k,1), and it is freely conjugate to x^k y by [Nielsen primitive conjugacy uniqueness](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf), Corollary 4.4. Therefore every primitive candidate maps to a^k t for some integer k.

## A finite quotient of order 312,500

Work over F5 and start with the noncommutative algebra on augmentation variables U,V truncated after degree four. Define a linear functional L on homogeneous degree four by

    L(UVUU)=1,  L(UVUV)=4,  L(UVVV)=4,
    L(VUUU)=4,  L(VUUV)=1,  L(VUVV)=1,

and zero on all other quartic monomials. Quotient by ker(L) in degree four. This is a two-sided ideal because a degree-four element times a positive-degree element is zero in the truncation.

The resulting algebra has dimension sixteen: the empty monomial, the two degree-one, four degree-two and eight degree-three monomials, and one quartic symbol Z. One may take Z=UVUU. Multiplication concatenates when total degree is at most three, uses L times Z at degree four, and gives zero at higher degrees.

Let H be the unit subgroup generated by a=1+U and d=1+V. The map alpha(a)=d, alpha(d)=a d^-1 induces an automorphism of this explicit finite algebra. The program does not assume this from the supplied functional: it checks all **4,096 basis associativity triples**, all **256 basis multiplicativity identities** for alpha, and both inverse compositions on every basis element.

Completed exact closure gives

    |H|=15,625,   order(alpha on H)=20.

Thus Q=H semidirect_alpha C20 is an explicit finite group of order **312,500**. The original-generator map x=a, y=t sends u to the identity and r^-1 to Wt. Both identities are directly checked in Q. The original images generate Q since d=yxY.

The complete twisted orbit

    { q W alpha(q)^-1 : q in H }

has **3,125** elements. The candidate a has order five, and none of its five powers belongs to this orbit. This excludes all integers k simultaneously.

This is also a full Q-conjugacy test in the stable-exponent-one coset. If g is any conjugator and v=Wt, choose an integer representative j of its stable exponent and put q=g v^-j. Then q has stable exponent zero and conjugates v as g does, so a fiber conjugator suffices. The finite test therefore proves the theorem in G.

## Independent verification by class-three normal forms

A separate implementation uses literal-string polynomials in the full degree-four truncated algebra. Every element in the degree-three generated fiber has a normal form

    a^s d^t [a,d]^z [a,[a,d]]^v [d,[a,d]]^w,

with all exponents modulo five; the commutator convention is [g,h]=g^-1 h^-1 g h. Collecting works because this quotient has nilpotency class at most three and every element has order five.

The degree-one twisted equation for target a^k fixes (s,t)=(2k+1,k) modulo five. For each k=0,...,4, the independent program checks all 125 remaining triples (z,v,w). Exactly one lower-degree conjugator works in each case. The five triples and the quartic defect values are

| k | (z,v,w) | L(defect) |
|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | (3,4,4) | 2 |
| 1 | (1,0,4) | 2 |
| 2 | (0,4,0) | 4 |
| 3 | (0,3,0) | 2 |
| 4 | (1,4,2) | 2 |

Any alternative higher lift changes the quartic defect by (I-alpha)h. The independent program verifies that L annihilates I-alpha on **all sixteen quartic monomials**. Every displayed value is nonzero, so no lift works. This confirms the exclusion independently of the direct small-group orbit enumeration.
