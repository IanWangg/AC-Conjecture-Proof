# Primitive exclusion in every proper overgroup of two AK(3) markings

2026-11-19. Let $H$ be generated by the original marked AK(3) pair or by the strictly reachable [energy-17 pair](round-09-cyclic31.md). For every proper subgroup $G<F(x,y)$ containing $H$, the normal closure of $H$ in $G$ contains no primitive element of $F(x,y)$. Thus an ordinary AC path from either marking that exposes a primitive must already use conjugators which, together with $H$, generate all of $F(x,y)$. The primitive exclusion holds for arbitrarily many outside words, arbitrarily long paths, and arbitrary interleaving of ordinary moves subject to this subgroup restriction. [Exact fringe census and controls](../results/round10_ac_disproof_outsider_quotient.json).

## Reducing arbitrary proper overgroups to a finite fringe

First let $G$ be finitely generated. In its based Stallings core take the image of the based core of $H$ and write $L$ for the subgroup carried by that image subgraph. This image is a folded principal quotient of the original core. Every edge in the image belongs to a reduced based loop inherited from the original core, so it is the based core of $L$. Choosing a spanning tree in this subgraph and extending it to the full graph exhibits $L$ as a free factor of $G$ and defines a retraction $G\to L$. The maps of the two core graphs and the principal quotient census are as in [Round 09](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md).

For each edge outside the image subgraph and outside the extended tree, signed traversal count is a character of $G$ killing $H$. A word in $\langle\!\langle H\rangle\!\rangle_G$ has zero count on every such edge, even after removing its conjugating prefix and suffix. An ambient primitive cyclic word uses only one sign of each generator that occurs. It cannot traverse an edge with zero signed count. Consequently its cyclic loop would lie in the image subgraph with trees attached. Rebase the loop there, using the spanning tree. This changes the based word by conjugation in $G$ and places it in $L$. Retraction sends $\langle\!\langle H\rangle\!\rangle_G$ into $\langle\!\langle H\rangle\!\rangle_L$, where the finite tests below exclude such a primitive. For infinitely generated $G$, one expression of a putative primitive as a finite product of conjugates of the two generators of $H$ already lies in a finitely generated intermediate subgroup. [Outside-edge character argument](round-10-one-outside-kernel.md#outside-edges-of-an-immersed-core), [rank-two cyclic primitive words](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf).

In both censuses, the only rank-two cases are $H$ itself and the full ambient rose. The original cores pass exact partial-map primitive exclusion. Proper $G$ cannot contain the ambient rose subgroup. The remaining cases are covered by the quotient test below or by the separately checked rank-three exception.

## Exact detection of neutral cyclic paths in a free quotient

A chain of $j$ vertex identifications increasing rank at each step produces $L$ generated by the two source rows and $j$ outside loop words. These loops form a basis, since they generate a group of rank $2+j$. The code expresses the marked generators in a spanning-tree chord basis, applies Nielsen operations with their exact word traces, and verifies the resulting inverse substitution in both directions. Killing the original two marked generators gives the free quotient $L/\langle\!\langle H\rangle\!\rangle_L$. Assign to each directed edge the word obtained by reading its spanning-tree chord in that quotient. On any based loop, evaluation of these edge weights computes its exact quotient image.

In the immersed graph, make one finite automaton for each signed choice of $x,y$ and each choice of a letter whose double is forbidden. The states remember the current vertex and the signed letter to be read next. Every cyclic primitive label is read on a closed path of at least one of these eight automata. Expand its quotient-word edge labels into one-letter or epsilon transitions. A second flag layer records whether the path used a transition, so the check excludes the trivial path. To decide whether a nonempty cyclic path evaluates to the identity, saturate vertex pairs under epsilon transitions, concatenation, and inverse-letter wrapping. A word freely reducing to the identity admits such a recursive decomposition. The code checks the resulting finite relation separately for closure under all three operations. In every claimed exclusion, no diagonal state connects to its flagged copy. This rules out a broader language of cyclic words than just the primitives, without imposing any word-length bound. When a neutral cyclic path exists the constructor also checks its actual ambient label and quotient image. The three published AK(2) zero-voltage primitive loops are positive controls. [Exact saturation and checker](../experiments/round10_ac_disproof_outsider_quotient.py), [previous AK(2) controls](research-v-unbounded.md#4-ak2-falsification-control).

## Reachable nested barbell controls

The [Round 09 nested barbell](round-09-bridge-collar-control.md) has marked lobes `yyyyXXX` and `yyyyxxYXYXyxyyXXXXX`. The ordinary 33-move entry with bridge `Xy`, followed by a conjugation of the second row, reaches the placed pair for any reduced bridge beginning in `X` and ending in `y`. Exhausting all 91 such bridges of lengths two through six, the replayer checks their strict entries and all 41,014 single vertex identifications. Every identification has rank three and passes the signed-double-free voltage certificate. The positive unread-gap argument from the one-outside theorem then excludes primitive exposure under arbitrary conjugations from any fixed one-outsider group built from any of these 91 sources. In particular it covers all unordered pairs of rooting vertices, including the noninjective opposite-root case. This separate test bounds the source bridge length and places no bound on the added conjugating word or the number of subsequent operations inside the chosen overgroup. ## Scope and replay

Inversions, products and conjugations in $G$ preserve membership of each current row in the normal closure of the source subgroup in $G$. The theorem therefore gives the stated restriction on ordinary paths. The first outsider increases rank from two to three by the Round 09 census. Every intermediate proper group that strictly contains $H$ has rank at least three by the same-rank exclusion. Therefore the first step at which the cumulative group becomes $F(x,y)$ is a rank drop to two. Its added conjugator has no unread gap in the preceding core and adjoins by a vertex identification followed by folding. After this step the cumulative normal closure is already the entire ambient group, so this obstruction leaves unrestricted AC open. [Single-adjunction fold and same-rank exclusion](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word).
