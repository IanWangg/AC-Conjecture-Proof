# A fifth-power obstruction in the outer Wirtinger block

2026-11-19. The $C$ outer block excludes every exponent divisible by five in the conjugated-overlap branch at Schreier width two. Together with Round 19, this restricts the absolute exponent of the power to $1,2,3,6$. When three divides the exponent, a separate characteristic-three calculation excludes six of the 27 possible second-degree coordinates for each nonzero residue of the exponent divided by three. [Round 19 quotient and exponent bound](round-19-ac-proof.md), [exact replay](../results/round20_ac_proof_wirtinger_small_powers.json).

## Fifth powers

Keep the free-by-cyclic quotient of the $C$ donor from Round 19, with free kernel generators $u,v,w$, stable letter $b^{-1}$, and shift $\Psi$. The outer equation for $h=V^\alpha b^{-1}$ and $A=A_0b^{-1}$ is
$$
kV^\alpha=A_0\Psi(k).
$$
Write $V=1+n$, $k=1+\kappa$ in the noncommutative Magnus algebra through degree six, using a coefficient field to be specified. The linear part $T$ of $\Psi$ has $I-T$ invertible over the integers. Also $A_0$ has zero degree-one part.

Over $\mathbb F_5$, if $\alpha=5m$ then $V^\alpha=1+m n^5$ through degree six. Writing $n_1,n_2$ for the homogeneous parts of degrees one and two, one has
$$
n^5=n_1^5+\sum_{i=0}^4 n_1^i n_2 n_1^{4-i}
$$
through degree six. As $V^\alpha$ has zero degree-one part, the outer equation forces $k_1=0$. Hence the product has no cross terms between $\kappa$ and $V^\alpha-1$ through degree six. In the vector space of positive-degree polynomials, the outer equation requires
$$
A_0-1-mn^5\ \in\ \operatorname{im}(I-A_0\Psi).
$$
Here $A_0\Psi$ denotes substitution followed by left multiplication by the Magnus expansion of $A_0$.

For each $n_1$ with coordinate vector $(a,b,c)\in\mathbb F_5^3$, take the reference degree-two component from the actual Magnus expansion of $u^a v^b w^c$, with exponents in $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$. The free-group class-two normal form shows that all other possible degree-two components differ from it by a linear combination of the three basic commutator terms. Reducing generator exponents modulo five does preserve their degree-two expansions. For fixed $n_1$, the displayed expression for $n^5$ is affine linear in these three coefficients.

The replay computes the quotient by the image of $I-A_0\Psi$ once, with rank 1,002 out of 1,092 positive-degree monomials. In that quotient it tests all 125 first-degree vectors against the four nonzero residues of $m$. In every one of the 500 cases, the target lies outside the span of the three commutator corrections. The residue $m=0$ gives the previously excluded case $25\mid\alpha$. Therefore $5\nmid\alpha$ and the positive absolute exponents from Round 19 reduce to $1,2,3,6$.

## Third powers in canonical coordinates

Suppose $\alpha=3m$ with $m$ nonzero modulo three. Before quotienting, Round 19 shows that the abelianized outer kernel coordinate of a middle candidate has the form $sT^\nu(T-1)$, where $T$ denotes the Laurent shift and $s=\pm1$. In the $C$ quotient the module generator $xy=t_0$ is the shift of $w=t_{-1}$. Invert $V$ and change the sign of $\alpha$ if necessary, which preserves $V^\alpha$. Conjugate the resulting relator by an appropriate power of the stable letter $b^{-1}$ to move the abelianized $V$ coordinate to $(T-I)w$. This preserves its possible conjugacy to $A$. In the basis $u,v,w$, this coordinate is $(-1,1,1)$ and reduces to $(2,1,1)$ in characteristic three. The multiplier $m$ below uses the sign of $\alpha$ after this normalization.

In characteristic three, through degree six,
$$
V^\alpha=1+P,\qquad P=m n^3+\binom m2 n_1^6 .
$$
The correction $\kappa$ has no degree-one part. Its degree-two term $k_2$ is the unique solution of the degree-two equation. Its degree-three term $k_3$ is uniquely determined by $m n_1^3$ and the same lower data. The inverses of $I-T^{\otimes d}$ at degrees two and three exist modulo three, by the symmetric characteristic values $1$ and $-26$ in Round 19. Consequently the necessary image condition through degree six is
$$
A_0-1-P-k_2(P_3+P_4)-k_3P_3\ \in\ \operatorname{im}(I-A_0\Psi),
$$
where $P_i$ denotes the degree-$i$ part of $P$. This condition again allows all higher coefficients of $k$.

To test the possible $P$, write the quotient of $V$ modulo $\gamma_3$ as
$$
u^a v^b w^c [u,v]^{r_1}[u,w]^{r_2}[v,w]^{r_3}.
$$
Here $(a,b,c)\equiv(2,1,1)$ modulo three. A change of $(a,b,c)$ by multiples of three does not change degree two. Fix a triple $(r_1,r_2,r_3)$ modulo three. Through degree four, the remaining changes to the Magnus expansion are spanned by inserting one cube of each generator in its own power block, and appending commutators of weights three and four. This follows by collecting first in the class-two quotient, then in $\gamma_3/\gamma_4$ and $\gamma_4/\gamma_5$. The latter two groups are generated by the iterated commutators obtained by bracketing the previous weight with a generator. Generator powers have period nine through degree four, and powers of any element of weight at least two have period three there. Products of two of these remaining changes have degree at least six, so their effects through degree four add linearly.

No Magnus term of $V$ above degree four can contribute to $n^3$ through degree six. If $\delta$ is one of the remaining degree-three and degree-four changes, its contribution to $n^3$ is
$$
\delta(n_1+n_2)^2+(n_1+n_2)\delta(n_1+n_2)+(n_1+n_2)^2\delta
$$
through degree six. This is linear and leaves $P_3$ and $P_4$ unchanged. For each triple and each multiplier $m$, the replay therefore tests a finite span in the same quotient by $\operatorname{im}(I-A_0\Psi)$. Exactly 21 of 27 triples pass this necessary test for each multiplier. The six rejected triples in each case are recorded in the receipt. These tests leave the four absolute exponents above unresolved.
