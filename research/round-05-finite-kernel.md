# Lifting ordinary paths across finite and solvable normal kernels

2026-11-19. Ordinary AC-equivalence to a generating tuple lifts across finite normal kernels in the derived subgroup of an arbitrary ambient group. At rank two it also lifts across arbitrary solvable normal kernels in the derived subgroup. AK(3) is therefore ordinarily AC-equivalent to the basis in every finite derived-series layer of the braid kernel and in every joint image of such a layer with finitely many finite markings.

## Kernel lifting theorem

Let $G$ be generated by $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$ with $n\ge 2$. Let $A$ be a normally generating $n$-tuple and let $D\lhd G$ be finite and contained in $[G,G]$. Then $A$ and $B$ are ordinarily AC-equivalent if and only if their images in $G/D$ are ordinarily AC-equivalent. Conjugation by an arbitrary element of $G$ compiles into single-letter conjugations in any marking generating $G$.

First suppose that the tuples agree coordinatewise modulo a normal subgroup $E\subseteq[G,G]$. Two cases suffice.

**Central $E$.** Every hybrid tuple $C$ with coordinates chosen from $A$ and $B$ generates $G$. Indeed $H=\langle C\rangle$ satisfies $G=HE$ and centrality gives $[G,G]=[H,H]\subseteq H$. Thus $E\subseteq H$. To correct coordinate $i$, quotient by the normal closure of the other coordinates of this hybrid. The quotient is cyclic, so their normal closure contains $[G,G]$, hence also $C_i^{-1}b_i\in E$. Factor that difference as a product of conjugates of the other coordinates and their inverses. Multiply the recipient by these factors, restoring each donor after use. Repeat for all coordinates.

**Noncentral minimal normal $E$.** Call a row active if it fails to centralize $E$. The centralizer $C_G(E)$ is a proper normal subgroup, so every normally generating tuple has an active row. For a fixed active donor $d$, the elements

$$
h[g,d]h^{-1}= (hg)d(hg)^{-1}\big(hdh^{-1}\big)^{-1},
\qquad g\in E,\quad h\in G,
$$

generate $E$. They lie in $E$ and generate a nontrivial normal subgroup of $G$, which equals $E$ by minimality. Either sign of such a factor can be multiplied into another row using two donor products and conjugations that restore $d$ after use. Thus an active donor permits any correction in $E$ to another row.

Choose $j$ so that $b_j$ is active. If the current tuple has an active row outside $j$, use it to set row $j$ to $b_j$. If $j$ is its only active row, multiply a different row temporarily on the right by the current row $j$. This row becomes active because it previously centralized $E$. Use it as donor to set row $j$ to $b_j$, then multiply the temporary row on the right by $b_j^{-1}$. This restores its coset modulo $E$. Now $b_j$ can correct all remaining rows.

Lift the given path in $G/D$ to $G$ by lifting conjugators. Its endpoint is normally generating and agrees with $B$ modulo $D$. If $D$ is nontrivial, take a minimal nontrivial $G$-normal subgroup $E\subseteq D$. Induction in $G/E$ corrects the tuple modulo $E$, since $D/E$ has smaller order and lies in the derived subgroup of $G/E$. Lift the correction and apply the central or the noncentral case. Projection proves the other implication.

Neither case uses finiteness of its factor. The proof applies if $D$ has a continuous well-ordered ascending series of $G$-normal subgroups from $1$ to $D$ whose factors are central or minimal normal in their ambient quotients. At a successor step, correct modulo the preceding term by applying one of the two cases in the quotient. Lift and correct the residual by induction. At a limit step the finitely many coordinate errors lie in one earlier term. The finite-group connectivity theorem of Borovik, Lubotzky and Myasnikov allows arbitrary normally generating targets. The present criterion assumes the target generates the ambient group, which can be infinite. [Finitary Andrews–Curtis theorem, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1295).

## Solvable kernel at rank two

Let $G=\langle x,y\rangle$. Suppose the current pair is $(xc,yd)$ with $c,d\in[G,G]$ and that $d$ belongs to a solvable normal subgroup $D\lhd G$. Then the pair is ordinarily AC-equivalent to $(x,y)$. This gives the lifting theorem with a solvable kernel in rank two, even if $G/D$ is nonsolvable. After lifting a path in $G/D$, both row errors lie in $D\subseteq[G,G]$. The stronger one-row version uses a relative variant of Guyot's commutator substitution. [Guyot, Proposition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06912#S2).

The normal closure of $[x,y]$ is exactly $[G,G]$. Fix an expression for $c$ as a product of conjugates of $[x,y]^{\pm1}$. Make $c'$ by replacing just these commutator factors by $[x,d^{-1}]^{\pm1}$, retaining all the conjugators. Then $c'\in D$ and $c'=c$ in $G/\langle\!\langle yd\rangle\!\rangle$. Thus a product of conjugates of the second row and its inverse changes $xc$ to $xc'$.

In $Q=G/\langle\!\langle xc'\rangle\!\rangle$, the image of $x$ belongs to the image $\bar D$ of $D$. If $x\in\bar D^{(k)}$ in $Q$, then all conjugates of $[x,y]$ and hence $d$ lie in $\bar D^{(k)}$, a normal subgroup of $Q$. Therefore $[x,d^{-1}]\in\bar D^{(k+1)}$, giving $c'\in\bar D^{(k+1)}$ and $x\in\bar D^{(k+1)}$. When the derived series of $\bar D$ terminates, the image of $x$ is trivial. In $G$ the normal closure of the new first row consequently contains $x$ and $[G,G]$, since $G/\langle\!\langle x\rangle\!\rangle$ is cyclic. Use the new first row to correct $yd$ to $y$. The normal closure of $y$ contains $[G,G]$, so use $y$ to correct $xc'$ to $x$. The three row corrections compile into ordinary moves by restoring the donors.

## Application to AK(3)

Write $F=F(x,y)$ and let $M\lhd F$ have finite index. With $G=F/(R\cap M)$, the kernel $R/(R\cap M)$ of $G\to F/R$ embeds in $F/M$ and lies in $[G,G]$. The marked basis generates $G$. Apply the lifting theorem to the images of AK(3) and the basis. Taking $M$ to be the intersection of finitely many finite-index normal subgroups handles any finite collection of finite markings simultaneously. The path lifts as a strict ordinary sequence in $F$ whose endpoint matches the basis modulo $R\cap M$.

For the normal closure $N$ of the braid relator, the known 533-move path matches the basis modulo $N^{(1)}=[N,N]$. The kernel $N^{(1)}/N^{(s)}$ of $F/N^{(s)}\to F/N^{(1)}$ is solvable and lies in the derived subgroup. The rank-two result proves the equivalence in $F/N^{(s)}$ for every finite $s\ge 1$. These layers also recover the lower-central result, since $N^{(s)}\subseteq\gamma_{2^s}(N)$. More generally, suppose a pair has a path to the marked basis in $F/R$ with $R\subseteq [F,F]$. The path-to-basis property persists in the joint quotient $F/(R\cap\ker\phi)$ for any marking $\phi$ with solvable image $\phi(R)$.

Combining these results covers joint images of the derived-series braid layers with arbitrary finite markings, including fiber products with dependent images. The [relative lower-central paths](round-02-relative-quotients.md) and the [two explicit perfect finite factors](round-04-finite-corrections.md) are special cases. The arguments supply no uniform length bound over the filtration or the finite markings.

## Where the solvable correction stops in the free group

Let $(U,V)=(xc,yd)$ be the free endpoint of the 533-move path, so $c,d\in [N,N]$. For every choice of commutator expansion of $c$ in the rank-two proof, write $W=xc'$. The change $(U,V)\to(W,V)$ is already an ordinary AC path in $F$, because its only receiver correction lies in the normal closure of $V$ there. The [round-three finite witness](round-03-frozen-donors.md) excludes a primitive first row after every such path with the second row frozen.

In $Q=F/\langle\!\langle W\rangle\!\rangle$ the image of $x$ belongs to the image of $[N,N]$, because $c'\in[N,N]$. Also $W\in\langle\!\langle x\rangle\!\rangle$ in $F$. The image of $x$ survives in $Q$: if it vanished, $W$ and $x$ would have the same normal closure and the Magnus theorem would make $W$ conjugate to $x^{\pm1}$, contradicting the frozen-donor test. [Magnus, 1930](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1930.163.141).

Since $Q$ is generated by the images of $x,y$ and $x$ is trivial in the abelianization, the normal closure of its image is exactly $[Q,Q]$. The image of $[N,N]$ is normal and contains that of $x$, so it too equals $[Q,Q]$. Hence the images of both $x$ and $d$ lie in $[Q,Q]$. Every commutator factor of $c'$ now lies in $[[Q,Q],[Q,Q]]$. This puts $x$ in the same subgroup, proving that $[Q,Q]$ is nontrivial and perfect. The image of $d$ also survives in $Q$, since $d=1$ there would give $c'=1$ and kill $x$.

Consequently, after the free step to $(W,V)$, one cannot set the second row to $y$ using this frozen donor in the free group. Such a correction would imply $d=1$ in $Q$. In each $F/N^{(s)}$ the correction is possible by the solvable-kernel argument. Its minimum strict length tends to infinity with $s$. The lengths are monotone under projection. A bound at arbitrarily deep layers would give one fixed strict move sequence on infinitely many layers, by finiteness of the move alphabet at fixed length. The sequence would then work in $F$ since $\bigcap_sN^{(s)}=1$, using residual nilpotence of the free group $N$ and $N^{(s)}\subseteq\gamma_{2^s}(N)$.

## Exact control in an infinite ambient group

Use $(a,b,c,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\times C_3\times C_5$ with multiplication

$$
(a,b,c,v)(a',b',c',v')=(a+a',\ b+b',\ c+c'+ab',\ v+2^a v').
$$

The last two coordinates use arithmetic modulo three and five. The marks $x=(1,0,0,0)$ and $y=(0,1,0,1)$ generate the group because their commutator has last two coordinates $(1,1)$ and generates the whole kernel of the projection to $\mathbb Z^2$. The starting pair $((1,0,2,2),(0,1,1,0))$ agrees with $(x,y)$ in the projection. It normally generates the group. Its own commutator generates the $C_3$ kernel, and a commutator of its first row with a generator of the $C_5$ kernel supplies the other factor. Its two rows generate a subgroup contained in the proper subgroup $v=2(2^a-1)$.

The replayer uses the mixed donor step to correct the noncentral $C_5$ factor modulo $C_3$, then corrects the central $C_3$ kernel. It verifies all 144 strict free moves and the exact tuple images at both stage boundaries. [Control results](../results/round05_finite_kernel_control.json).

## Exact control with nonsolvable quotient and infinite kernel

Take $G=A_5\times\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$, with $x$ mapped to a 3-cycle and the dilation by two, and $y$ to a 5-cycle and translation by one. Use the permutations from the [round-four chart control](round-04-chart-lifts.md). These pairs generate both factors. A subgroup surjecting to both factors can impose only a common quotient of the two factors. The only such quotient here is trivial because $A_5$ is simple nonabelian and $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$ is solvable. Thus $x,y$ generate $G$.

Let $c=[x,y]$ and $d=c^3$. The image of $c$ in $A_5$ has order three, and its translation in $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$ is one. Thus $d$ is an element of the infinite abelian normal translation group $D=\mathbb Z[1/2]$, and $c$ lies outside $D$. The nonsolvable quotient by $D$ is $A_5\times\mathbb Z$. The replayer explicitly changes $(xc,yd)$ to the marked basis in this ambient group using the commutator substitution above, followed by two donor corrections. It checks the 96 elementary moves in the free group and evaluates the endpoint in the product using exact dyadic rational arithmetic.
