# Round 01 ordinary AC disproof track

Tested 2026-11-19. This extends the [prior two-phase exclusion](research-x.md) from intersecting first-phase endpoint axes to certified disjoint-axis sources. The unrestricted ordinary AC conjecture and AK(3) remain open in this analysis.

## Statement

Write $F=F(x,y)$ and start at the literal AK(3) pair `p=xxxYYYY,b=xyxYXY`, with capitals denoting inverses. Independently conjugate the two relators. If the resulting axes are disjoint, let the bridge be their shortest connecting path in the Cayley tree. The following first-phase outcomes cannot expose an ambient primitive through any sequence consisting of Nielsen moves, a second independent conjugation phase, and more Nielsen moves.

1. Every first-phase outcome with bridge length from 1 through 5.
2. Every outcome whose two relators, after common conjugation, lie in a certified source subgroup from the finite scan. In particular, put `P=YYxxxYY` and $Q=x b x^{-1}$. The subgroups $\langle P,tQt^{-1}\rangle$ for every $t\in\langle P,Q\rangle$ are covered. The explicit subfamily $t=(QP)^m$ has bridge length $15m+1$ for all $m\ge 0$. No member is conjugate into any of the 42 earlier rotation-source subgroups.

Both conclusions allow arbitrary Nielsen lengths and arbitrary second conjugators. In fact, for every covered intermediate subgroup $H$, the subgroup generated by independent conjugates of any $u,v\in H$ has no ambient primitive. [V's arbitrary-word reduction](research-v-unbounded.md).

## Finite coverage of the first-phase sources

Translate the first bridge endpoint to the identity by common conjugation. The two relators become $(P,q B q^{-1})$, where $q$ labels the nonempty shortest bridge, $P$ is a cyclic rotation of $p$, and $B$ is a cyclic rotation of $b$. The first direction of $q$ avoids the two directions along the $P$ axis. The inverse last direction avoids the two on the $B$ axis. Conversely, those conditions on a freely reduced $q$ give an immersion of the two lines connected by the path $q$ into the Cayley tree. The two axes are disjoint and their shortest bridge is the given path. The finite subgroup graph consists of a $P$ circle, a $B$ circle and the path labelled $q$. [Q's rotation and barbell reduction](research-q.md), [Stallings, Topology of Finite Graphs](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993).

For bridge length $s\ge 1$, the complete menu has $42\cdot 3^{s-1}$ literal sources. Each of the seven $P$ rotations permits two initial letters and each subsequent letter has three choices. Each signed letter is allowed as the incoming bridge direction at exactly three of the six $B$ rotations. The source graph has $12+s$ vertices. Totals count source entries with multiplicity.

For each source core $\Gamma$, identify each distinct pair of vertices and fold to the overgroup graph $K$. The additional loop $T$ is the path from the base to the first vertex followed by the reversed path to the second vertex. Each $K$ has rank three. Coordinates in an integral spanning-tree basis give a character $\chi:\pi_1(K)\to\mathbb Z$ with evaluations $(0,0,1)$ on $(P,qBq^{-1},T)$. The character kills the source subgroup, so it vanishes on the image of any closed path in $\Gamma$, including paths based away from the original base.

For each of the four signed alphabets and either forbidden double, form the directed edge-state graph of admissible continuations. In each cyclic strongly connected component $C$, the constructor supplies a sign $\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}$ and integer potentials $h$ satisfying

$$
h(v)\le h(u)+|C|\epsilon\operatorname{wt}(u,v)-1
$$

for every internal edge. Its weight is the character contribution of the letter read at the source state. Summing around any nonempty directed closed walk forces its character value to be nonzero. Every cyclic primitive in rank two is represented in one of these eight graphs by the signed Euclidean description. Thus $\ker\chi$ contains no ambient primitive. The independent verifier refolds the three loops, checks the full projection and every potential inequality, and reconstructs the strongly connected components by mutual reachability. [Clay, Conant and Ramasubramanian, Section 4](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf).

For arbitrary nonidentity $u,v\in H$, their cyclic reductions and rotations label loops in $\Gamma$. If the new axes intersect, common conjugation makes the two words cyclic at a shared vertex. If the two loops in $\Gamma$ have the same base vertex, their subgroup is conjugate into $H$. If the vertices differ, identifying them puts both loops in a certified kernel. The group $H$ itself lies in each such kernel. For disjoint new axes, the new barbell bridge is separating. A cyclic path using the bridge reads opposite signs of a letter and cannot be primitive. A path in one lobe reads a power of a conjugate of a source word. Those words are nonprimitive, and no proper power is primitive. A trivial source word gives a cyclic subgroup. A Nielsen prelude keeps the source words in $H$, and a Nielsen suffix preserves the subgroup they generate after conjugation. [V's geometric deduction](research-v-unbounded.md).

## A genuinely disjoint unbounded family

Take the length-one seed `P=YYxxxYY,Q=xxyxYXYX` and write $H=\langle P,Q\rangle$. All 78 vertex identifications pass. For every $t\in H$, we have $\langle P,tQt^{-1}\rangle\le H$, so the quantified result for $H$ applies. The original relators reach $(P,tQt^{-1})$ by conjugating the first by `YY` and the second by $tx$. These are ordinary moves, using one signed-letter conjugation per letter.

For $t=(QP)^m$, the bridge $(QP)^m x$ is freely reduced and has length $15m+1$. Its first direction $x$ avoids the two $P$ directions $Y,y$. Its last edge arrives along $x$, so the reversed direction $X$ avoids the two $b$ directions $x,y$. This gives genuine disjoint endpoint axes. The entry uses $15m+3$ signed-letter conjugations.

The literal $P$ has one based loop per source graph across the 42 earlier rotation cores. At each of these 42 vertices, $Q$ fails to close and $QP$ fails even to read. If a conjugate of $\langle P,tQt^{-1}\rangle$ lay in a rotation source group, its two freely reduced words would label closed paths at a common vertex of that core. The vertex lies in the core because it supports a nontrivial cyclically reduced $P$ loop, and a reduced closed path starting there cannot enter an attached tree. For $m=0$ the path for $Q$ fails its closure test. For $m\ge1$, the freely reduced word $tQt^{-1}$ begins with $QP$, which fails its path test. This verifies non-inclusion for all $m$.

## Two independently reached exterior waypoints

The AK(3) direction supplied two cyclic-total-length-20 tuples by replaying a published Lisitsa–Vernitski path in reverse. The ordered pairs are `(XXyXYYYxxYxyy,XYxYYxY)` and `(XXyyxYxyxYYXy,XYxYYxY)`. For each tuple, the 18-vertex core passes all 153 vertex-identification certificates. The independent pass checks 1,576 and 1,318 respective potential inequalities. Therefore any Nielsen prelude at either waypoint, one arbitrary independent conjugation phase, and any Nielsen suffix still cannot expose an ambient primitive. This extends the archive track's test of independent conjugation of the two displayed coordinates. The same subgroup deduction applies here because it requires a certified core, whose shape may vary. [Archive replay and provenance](round-01-ak3.md#replay-of-the-september-2026-paths), [Lisitsa–Vernitski archive](https://zenodo.org/records/23061803).

## Control and reproduction

A genuine AK(2) barbell uses `P_2=xYYYx,B_2=yxYXYx,q=y`. Identifying the constructor's vertices $0,3$ gives a rank-three overgroup with connecting loop `T=Y` and a zero-voltage cyclic loop `xYxYYxYY` at vertex 2. To witness its primitivity, start from $(x,Y)$, apply `RLRL`, and take the second coordinate, using $R(a,b)=(ab,b)$ and $L(a,b)=(a,ab)$. The checker finds the loop and rejects the sufficient sign test in that kernel. The control concerns the coarse overgroup kernel. Whether an actual two-phase path from this AK(2) barbell reaches that primitive remains outside this check.
