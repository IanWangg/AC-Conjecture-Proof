# Restricting the three-conjugation schedule for AK(3)

2026-11-19. Suppose a free ordinary or stable AK(3) trivialization uses exactly three original-letter conjugations in its fixed-rank section after the usual normalization. In the marking $p=x^3y^{-4}$, $b=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}$, the three signed conjugating letters must be **$x,x,y^{-1}$**, in that order. Each of the two intervals between them must contain a product that survives auxiliary erasure. [Finite product automata and receipts](../results/round11_three_conjugations.json).

Erase the extra generators while keeping their rows. Products and inversions keep every row in its current containing subgroup. A retained-letter conjugation by $c$ of a single row in $K$ changes the containing subgroup to a subgroup of $\langle K,cKc^{-1}\rangle$. The [Round 06 certificate](round-06-stable-disproof.md#three-necessary-original-letter-conjugations-in-the-free-group) gives an exact primitive-free certificate for 56 of the 64 three-letter containing groups. Each of the other eight had a primitive in the containing group that was used in that test. At a successful endpoint, the erased tuple must contain both $x$ and $y$ and hence generate the ambient group.

For one of the eight remaining schedules write $K$ for its two-letter prefix envelope and $c$ for the last letter. The last conjugation uses only one actual word $w\in K$. Every later row lies in $\langle K,cwc^{-1}\rangle$, even if arbitrary products follow. Thus a necessary condition for this schedule is that some $q\in cKc^{-1}$ satisfies $\langle K,q\rangle=F(x,y)$.

Read the longest prefix of reduced $q$ and the longest inverse suffix in the immersed core of $K$. A positive uncovered gap would attach an extra arc, or a stem and circuit, increasing the rank of $K$ by one. That cannot give the ambient rose, because $K$ already has rank at least two. If no gap remains, some split $q=\alpha\beta$ has $\alpha$ readable from the base to a vertex $a$ and $\beta$ readable from a vertex $b$ to the base. Adding $q$ then folds precisely the identification of $a,b$. Conversely, when such a split has a vertex identification folding to the ambient rose, adding $q$ generates the whole ambient group. [Gap and fold argument](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word).

The checker folds every distinct vertex pair, verifying the answer independently by adjoining the corresponding tree-path word. It builds a finite automaton that reads in the core of $K$, makes one silent transition across a vertex identification folding to the rose, and resumes reading in the same core. The start and finish are at the base, with exactly one silent transition. Intersect this language with the core language of $cKc^{-1}$ and accept only freely reduced input. A graph search on the product states decides whether the intersection is empty for every word length. The seven negative cases are `xxy`, `xyx`, `xyX`, `yxy`, `yxY`, `yyx` and `yyX`. For `xxY`, the word `xyyyXYYYY` passes the relaxed last-row test and independently folds the two-step envelope to the ambient rose. The first two actual rows conjugated have not been reconstructed for this witness. Thus the test leaves one schedule without providing a trivialization.

Let $H_0=\langle p,b\rangle$ and let $K_1=\langle H_0,xH_0x^{-1}\rangle$ and $K_2=\langle K_1,xK_1x^{-1}\rangle$. For the surviving schedule, suppose no product survives erasure in the interval between the first two conjugations. If the receiver identity is the same, the two conjugations combine to $x^2$. The tuple before the last conjugation lies in $\langle H_0,x^2H_0x^{-2}\rangle$. If the identities differ, the tuple lies in $K_1$. The automaton finds neither group can become the rose after adjoining one word from its $y^{-1}$ conjugate.

If no product survives in the second interval and the two receiver identities there are the same, their conjugations combine to $y^{-1}x$. The necessary one-row test starts at $K_1$ with this composite conjugator, and fails. If the identities differ, the final receiving row before its conjugation still lies in $K_1$, while all rows after the second conjugation lie in $K_2$. Intersecting the completion language for $K_2$ with the subgroup $y^{-1}K_1y$ also gives the empty language. Inversions commute with transporting a row by conjugation. Permutations can be tracked through row identities, and erased-letter conjugations act trivially. This proves that both intervals require an effective product.

The two subgroup tests also apply with arbitrary products. The projection of the second receiving row immediately before its $x$ conjugation must lie outside $H_0$, since otherwise all projected rows would still be in $K_1$ after that move. The projection of the third receiving row immediately before its $y^{-1}$ conjugation must lie outside $K_1$, because no word of $y^{-1}K_1y$ completes $K_2$.
