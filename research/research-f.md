# Research F: a one-unit gap above the AK(n) cyclic plateau

Date: 2026-11-02. Status: proved limited cyclic-length lower bound, with independently implemented finite checks. No novelty claim is made. This does not decide the ordinary or stable AC status of AK(3).

## Definitions and result

Use X=x^-1, Y=y^-1 and write

    p_n=x^nY^(n+1),  q_n=y^nX^(n+1),  b=xyxYXY.

Let Orb(w) contain the cyclic rotations of w and w^-1. All lengths in this note mean cyclically reduced free-word lengths. A quotient-conjugacy replacement changes one coordinate u while keeping the other v fixed, with the new image conjugate to u or u^-1 in F(x,y)/<<v>>. Relator permutation is allowed. Every ordinary AC move projects to such a replacement on cyclic representatives.

**Theorem.** For n>=3, every quotient-conjugacy-replacement path from AK(n)=(p_n,b) with total cyclic relator length at most 2n+8 stays, up to relator permutation, in

    (Orb(p_n) union Orb(q_n)) x Orb(b).

Consequently every ordinary AC trivialization of AK(n), if one exists, has total cyclic relator length at least **2n+9** somewhere. For AK(3), this gives the lower bound **15**, improving research A's cyclic bound 14 by one. This is a necessary length excursion, not an invariant separating AC components.

## Proof

Research A establishes these exact minimality and equality statements:

1. In B=<x,y|xyx=yxy>, any cyclically reduced word conjugate to p_n or its inverse has length at least 2n+1; equality forces membership in Orb(p_n) union Orb(q_n).
2. In G_n=<x,y|x^n=y^(n+1)>, n>=3, any cyclically reduced word conjugate to b or its inverse has length at least six; equality forces membership in Orb(b). The same holds with q_n as the defining relator.

The new step is that neither minimum can increase by exactly one.

### Power relator: the next even length is impossible

The exponent-sum homomorphism h:B->Z given by h(x)=h(y)=1 is well-defined. It sends p_n and q_n to -1. If u is conjugate to either of these or to its inverse, then h(u)=1 or -1. The length of a free word has the same parity as its total exponent sum. Therefore |u| is odd. In particular, it cannot equal 2n+2. Apart from the equality states, the next possible power-relator length is at least 2n+3.

### Braid relator: no length-seven conjugate

Suppose a cyclically reduced length-seven word u is conjugate to b or b^-1 in G_n. In the further quotient C_n*C_(n+1), the target has a cyclically reduced six-syllable necklace, every syllable being one of x,x^-1,y,y^-1. Expanding u into seven one-letter syllables and reducing to this necklace must lose exactly one syllable.

The only possible reduction is one combination of two adjacent syllables in the same factor into one nonidentity syllable, possibly across the cyclic boundary. Identity cancellation loses at least two syllables; any further combination would also lose another syllable. Thus u must be obtained from a cyclic rotation of b or b^-1 by replacing one target signed generator with two signed generators from the same factor.

Two same-factor letters in a freely reduced word have the same sign. In a factor of order m>=4, their exponent is +2 or -2, which cannot equal +1 or -1 modulo m. Hence this replacement is impossible for n>=4.

For n=3, the only remaining factor is the C_3 generated by x. The possible replacements are x->XX or X->xx. Each changes the exponent vector from that of the corresponding target b or b^-1 by (3,0) or (-3,0).

But conjugate words in G_3 must agree in its abelianization

    Z^2 / <(3,-4)>.

Neither (3,0) nor (-3,0) belongs to the indicated relation lattice: its second coordinate would force the multiple to be zero. This contradicts the assumed conjugacy. Thus length seven is impossible also for n=3.

If the frozen power relator is q_n, use C_(n+1)*C_n instead. For n=3 the exceptional order-three replacement now changes the exponent vector by (0,3) or (0,-3), neither belonging to <(4,-3)>. The same conclusion follows. Inversion or cyclic rotation of the frozen relator changes none of these quotient groups.

### Closure under the larger cap

Start at the family in the theorem, where every tuple has total length 2n+7. Update its power coordinate while keeping the braid coordinate fixed. The minimum/equality classification from A and the parity argument imply that either the new tuple stays in the family, or its total length is at least

    (2n+3)+6 = 2n+9.

Update the braid coordinate instead. The minimum/equality classification and length-seven exclusion imply that either it stays in the family, or its total length is at least

    (2n+1)+8 = 2n+9.

Therefore no step satisfying the cap 2n+8 can leave the family. Induction proves the assertion for an arbitrary path under that cap; the path need not be monotone. Since the standard tuple has length two, a trivialization must violate the cap. This completes the proof.

For clarity about ordinary paths with conjugating tails: replace each relator at every stage by a cyclically reduced conjugate. The normal closure of the frozen coordinate does not change, so each projected step is still an allowed quotient-conjugacy replacement. Thus the theorem applies to cyclic lengths along the original ordinary path as well. It does not assume a length-controlled elementary expansion of arbitrary generalized replacements.

## Why this argument stops at length 15 for AK(3)

The first allowed increase is real in the generalized graph. In G_3, put z=x^3=y^4. This element is central. The length-eight word

    b' = XXyxYxxY

is equal to b, because XX=z^-1 x and xx=z X, and the two central factors cancel:

    XX y x Y xx Y = (z^-1 x)y x Y(z X)Y = xyxYXY.

The word b' is cyclically reduced and is not a rotation or inverse of b. Hence (p_3,b) has a legitimate generalized replacement to (p_3,b') of total cyclic length 7+8=15. The theorem's confined initial family therefore ceases to be closed under the cap 15. This witness is not a trivialization, and no elementary-peak claim follows from it.

## Reproduction

```sh
python3 experiments/verify_gap_one.py
```

This standalone standard-library program was implemented independently of A's verifier. It enumerates cyclically reduced words of lengths one through seven, compares cyclic free-product normal forms, and then applies the necessary torus-knot abelianization condition. It also verifies the length-eight witness using the central-extension normal form for <x,y|x^3=y^4>.

Executed results:

- For n=3 and either frozen-power ordering, the free-product quotient admits 42 length-seven candidates; **all 42 fail** the full torus-knot abelianization condition.
- For n=4 and n=5, either ordering, the free-product quotient admits **zero** length-seven candidates.
- In each tested ordering the six-letter candidates are exactly the 12 members of Orb(b), and no shorter candidates occur.
- The exact torus normal forms of b and XXyxYxxY agree.

These finite checks support the implementation and catch the exceptional order-three case. The all-n result follows from the proof, not extrapolation from the finite checks.
