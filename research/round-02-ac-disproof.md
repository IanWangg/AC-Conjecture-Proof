# Round 02 · Ordinary Andrews–Curtis disproof

2026-11-19. The two-phase primitive exclusion extends to all shortest first bridges of length at most eight and to a separate family of arbitrary-length bridges. Neither result covers unrestricted ordinary AC paths.

Put $p=x^3y^{-4}$, $b=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}$ and $F=F(x,y)$. A first independent conjugation phase on the AK(3) pair with disjoint final axes gives a folded barbell. Its two circles carry rotations $P,B$ of $p,b$, and its bridge carries the shortest connecting word $q$. For each source subgroup $H$ certified below, every subgroup generated by independent conjugates of any $u,v\in H$ is ambient-primitive-free. An arbitrary Nielsen prelude to that second phase and an arbitrary Nielsen suffix therefore cannot expose a primitive coordinate. [Round 01 axes and phase reductions](round-01-ac-disproof.md), [Stallings, *Topology of finite graphs*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993).

## Finite join in place of the infinite voltage kernel

Let $\Gamma$ be an immersed core of $H$. For vertices $a,b$, take two isomorphic copies of $\Gamma$, based at $a$ and $b$, wedge those basepoints and fold. The subgroup of the resulting graph is $J_{a,b}=\langle H_a,H_b\rangle$, where $H_v$ is the subgroup of labels of loops at $v$. In a basis at vertex 0, if $s_v$ is a path to $v$, then $H_v=s_v^{-1}H s_v$. Thus a direct alternative is to fold four words, two rebased copies of an $H$ basis. The join can have rank four even when the individual groups have rank two. The case $a=b$ uses $H_a$ itself.

If the second-phase axes intersect, common conjugation puts both words in cyclically reduced form at their shared Cayley-tree vertex. The two words then label loops at possibly different vertices $a,b$ of $\Gamma$, so their subgroup is contained in $J_{a,b}$. If the second-phase axes are disjoint, the subgroup has a folded barbell diagram. A cyclically reduced path crossing its separating bridge reads both signs of a generator, so cannot be primitive. Each loop confined to one lobe reads a power of a source word. The source subgroup itself is primitive-free and no nonzero power of one of its elements is primitive. A trivial source word gives only a cyclic subgroup. These cases give the claimed conclusion whenever every join and the source core are primitive-free. [Previous arbitrary-word proof](research-v-unbounded.md#3-the-arbitrary-word-and-arbitrary-tail-deduction).

The corresponding coarse character kernel from round 01 contains the rebased join after common transport to the coarse quotient basepoint. Its pullback is exact on each source graph, so both rebased groups have zero voltage. The finite join only needs those two copies. This difference is detectable. The genuine AK(2) barbell with cyclic lobes **xYYYx**, **yxYXYx** and bridge **y** has the zero-voltage primitive **xYxYYxYY** in the coarse kernel for its first and fourth vertices. Starting at the signed basis **(x,Y)**, the program **RLRL** gives that word as the second coordinate, with $R(a,b)=(ab,b)$ and $L(a,b)=(a,ab)$. Independent four-word refolding and exact partial-map certificates exclude ambient primitives in all 55 two-copy joins of this barbell. On the literal AK(2) tuple, the same checker detects three primitive-containing joins, with a witness in each. [Round 01 control](round-01-ac-disproof.md#control-and-reproduction), [finite primitive detector](research-r.md), [primary primitive-class theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.6574).

## All first bridges through length eight

The independent bridge-end directions leave exactly $42\cdot3^{s-1}$ literal sources for bridge length $s$. A source has $12+s$ vertices. The native scanner constructs each barbell directly, folds the two copies at every unordered distinct vertex pair and checks that the map from each source copy preserves every labelled edge. It tests all eight signed double-free transition graphs by depth-first search. Every join has rank four and every tested transition graph is acyclic. This suffices for primitive exclusion because every cyclically reduced primitive in rank two uses a single sign of each generator and has at least one generator isolated in the cyclic word. The Python sampler independently uses four-word bouquet folding and topological sorting on five source barbells of each length. [Clay, Conant and Ramasubramanian, §4](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf).

## A uniform criterion for immersed barbells

Suppose the following hold for an immersed barbell $\Gamma$.

1. Each signed rotation of either lobe label reads from exactly one vertex of $\Gamma$.
2. The bridge label, oriented from the first lobe to the second, reads from exactly one vertex of $\Gamma$.
3. Every subgroup generated by two independently chosen rotations of the lobe words, allowing two choices from the same lobe, is ambient-primitive-free.

Then the two-phase exclusion holds for $\Gamma$. To see this, take the component $C$ of $(a,b)$ in the labelled fibre product of $\Gamma$ with itself over the two-petal rose, for $a\ne b$. Its vertices are ordered pairs of vertices reached by the same labelled path. The component misses the diagonal because paths can be reversed and labels determine paths from a fixed vertex. Both projections of $C$ are graph immersions. If one projection identifies two distinct vertices of $C$, a nonempty reduced path between them projects to a reduced closed path. The first repeated vertex on such a closed path gives a simple closed subpath. In a barbell this subpath is a signed lobe rotation. The other projection reads the same label from a distinct vertex, contradicting condition 1. Both projections are therefore embeddings. The same argument prevents a cycle in $C$, since its projection would give a reduced closed path with a lobe rotation. Thus $C$ is a tree.

The wedge fold is precisely the pushout of the two copies along their embedded images of $C$. All identifications in $C$ are forced by following equal labels from the wedge vertex. The pushout is folded because any common outgoing label at an identified vertex leads to another edge of $C$. Condition 2 prevents either projection of $C$ from containing the full bridge. In particular it meets at most one lobe per copy. A bridge edge outside the image of $C$ in its copy separates the resulting graph, since the attached tree and the other copy lie on one side. Delete all these separating edges when testing cyclic primitive paths.

Any remaining component away from the glued tree has at most one lobe. In the component with the glued tree, each retained lobe meets that tree in a connected arc, possibly a point, because it originally attaches to the rest of its own barbell at only one vertex. There are at most two lobes. If they are disjoint the rest is a tree joining them, and any path going between them uses both signs of some bridge letter. If they overlap their intersection is connected, as it is the intersection of their two subtrees in $C$. Their union has fundamental group generated by traversing the two lobe circles based at a common vertex. Remaining tree edges add no cyclic loops. Condition 3 then handles the intersecting case. A single lobe is primitive-free by the same condition applied to a duplicate choice. This proves the criterion. [Stallings, labelled graph folding and pullbacks](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993).

## A long family outside the earlier short-bridge seeds

Use $P=y^{-4}x^3$, $B=b$ and $q=xy^m$ for every $m\ge18$. The bridge directions at the two endpoints are admissible. Conjugating the original $p$ by $y^{-4}$ and $b$ by $q$ gives the actual pair $(P,qBq^{-1})$ in $m+5$ signed-letter conjugations.

For condition 1, all signed lobe rotations have length at most seven and contain both a positive and a negative letter, so no reading lies entirely inside a forward or backward bridge. All possible readings are inside one lobe together with the first seven bridge edges at that end. These local labelled neighbourhoods are independent of $m$ in the stated range. The control script checks all 26 signed lobe rotations on the representative $m=18$, with each label reading from exactly one vertex.

For condition 2, the positive $y$ runs in the two lobes alone have length at most four. The only $y$ run of length at least $m$ in the barbell is the run following the initial bridge $x$, extended by precisely one positive $y$ edge into the braid lobe through the reversed last letter of $B$. In this run, only its first vertex has an incoming $x$ edge. The next vertex is internal to the bridge and cannot provide the second possible start for a run of length $m$. Hence $xy^m$ reads uniquely. For condition 3, exact closed partial-map certificates test all $49+42+36=127$ rotation pairs, with zero primitive witnesses. No truncation of primitive-word length is used.

No subgroup in this family is conjugate into the original rotation cores or any short-bridge source core of round 01. The 42 rotation cores have a checked uniform maximum positive $y$ run. For every bridge source of length at most five the graph has at most 17 vertices and no pure $y$ cycle, because any reduced closed path includes a lobe and both lobe labels are mixed. Such a graph has positive $y$ runs of length at most 16. An inclusion would place a freely reduced loop labelled $qBq^{-1}$ in the target core after common transport, where it would require reading $y^m$. The common basepoint lies in that core because it also supports the nontrivial cyclically reduced $P$ loop. Thus the uniform theorem extends beyond containment in the previously certified seeds.

The geometric criterion permits other bridge words with the two uniqueness properties. It does not classify bridges with repeated readable lobe words or repeated occurrences of the entire bridge. Even for the sources covered here, the result restricts a fixed two-phase pattern and does not provide an invariant under further alternating Nielsen and conjugation phases.
