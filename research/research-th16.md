## 2. Classical small cancellation cannot apply to short relators in rank 2 (T1; proved here)

Definitions (Lyndon–Schupp, Ch. V): for a cyclically reduced r, R = Orb(r) is its symmetrized closure; a **piece** is a nonempty common prefix of two distinct elements of R; C′(λ): every piece u of r′ ∈ R has |u| < λ|r′|; C(p): no element of R is a product of fewer than p pieces. Write μ(r) for the maximal piece length.

**Proposition 1 (pigeonhole; proved here).** Let r ∈ F₂ be cyclically reduced of length ℓ, not a proper power. Then 2ℓ ≤ 4·3^{μ(r)}. Consequently, if R satisfies C′(1/6) then ℓ ≤ 2 or ℓ ≥ 13; if R satisfies C′(1/8) then ℓ ≤ 2, ℓ ∈ {17, 18} or ℓ ≥ 25; and if R satisfies C′(1/4) and T(4) then ℓ ≤ 2 or ℓ ≥ 9.

*Proof.* Since r is not a proper power and r is not conjugate to r⁻¹ in a free group, the 2ℓ words of R are pairwise distinct. Two distinct words of equal length ℓ have a common prefix of length < ℓ, so μ := μ(r) ≤ ℓ − 1, and by definition of μ no two elements of R share their prefix of length μ+1. Hence "prefix of length μ+1" is an injection of R into the set of reduced words of length μ+1, which has 4·3^μ elements. Under C′(λ), μ < λℓ, i.e. μ ≤ ⌈λℓ⌉ − 1. For λ = 1/6: if 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 6 then μ = 0 and 2ℓ ≤ 4, impossible; if 7 ≤ ℓ ≤ 12 then μ ≤ 1 and 2ℓ ≤ 12, impossible. For λ = 1/8: 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 8 gives μ = 0 (impossible); 9 ≤ ℓ ≤ 16 gives μ ≤ 1, ℓ ≤ 6 (impossible); 17 ≤ ℓ ≤ 24 gives μ ≤ 2, ℓ ≤ 18. For T(4): the *star graph* of r has vertices x, X, y, Y and an edge {u⁻¹, v} for every cyclic two-letter subword uv of r. A triangle p, q, s gives elements e₁ = q⋯p⁻¹, e₃ = p⋯s⁻¹, e₂ = s⋯q⁻¹ of R, no two of them mutually inverse, such that each of e₁e₃, e₃e₂, e₂e₁ has cancellation, contradicting T(4). So under T(4) the star graph is triangle-free, hence has at most 4 edges (Mantel), and at most 8 reduced two-letter words occur in r^{±1}. If 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 8, C′(1/4) gives μ ≤ 1, so the 2ℓ two-letter prefixes are distinct: 2ℓ ≤ 8, ℓ ≤ 4, then μ = 0 and ℓ ≤ 2. ∎

(A relator of a balanced presentation of the trivial group is never a proper power: its exponent vector is a row of a unimodular matrix. Brute force confirms the C′(1/6) statement for 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 11: `c16_possible`, §6.)

**Exact piece data (computation, `--pieces`).** "min # pieces" is the least number of pieces whose product is a cyclic conjugate of r (C(p) holds iff it is ≥ p); "triangle" says whether the star graph contains a triangle (then T(4) fails, by the proof above).

## 3. The substitute: displacement, and slides (T2)

**Lemma 3 (displacement; proved here).** Let G_r act by isometries on a metric space Ω and fix v₀ ∈ Ω. Put ω(g) = d(v₀, g v₀) for g ∈ {x, y}, ω(W) = ω(x)·n_x(W) + ω(y)·n_y(W) for a word W with n_x letters x^{±1} and n_y letters y^{±1}, and ‖g‖ = min_h d(h v₀, g h v₀) for g ∈ G_r. Then ‖·‖ is invariant under conjugation and inversion, and every word s′ whose image in G_r is conjugate to s^{±1} satisfies ω(s′) ≥ ‖s‖.

*Proof.* ‖hgh⁻¹‖ = ‖g‖ by definition; d(v, g⁻¹v) = d(gv, v). For s′ = g₁⋯g_L (letters), the triangle inequality along v₀, g₁v₀, g₁g₂v₀, … gives d(v₀, s′v₀) ≤ Σ d(v₀, g_i v₀) = ω(s′), and ‖s‖ = ‖s′‖ ≤ d(v₀, s′v₀). ∎

*Instances.* For a homomorphism G_r → A * B take the Bass–Serre tree and v₀ = midpoint of the base edge: d(v₀, g v₀) is the syllable length of g, and ‖g‖ is the syllable length of a cyclically reduced conjugate (1 for nontrivial elliptic g); all cyclically reduced conjugates are rotations of each other (Magnus–Karrass–Solitar Thm 4.2). research-a Lemma 1 is ω = (2,2), ‖p_n‖ = 4n+2; Lemma 2 is ω = (1,1), ‖b‖ = 6. Section 4 uses ω = (1,2). For an HNN extension with stable letter x and v₀ the base vertex, ω = (1,0): the lemma bounds only the number of x-letters, and the y-letters must be controlled by the next statement.

**Classical facts for BS(P,Q) = ⟨x,y | x⁻¹y^P x = y^Q⟩ (externally reported: Britton's lemma and the normal form theorem, Lyndon–Schupp Thm IV.2.1; Collins' lemma, Thm IV.2.5; numbering from memory).** Write a cyclic word with n ≥ 1 letters x^{±1} as W = x^{ε₁}y^{a₁}x^{ε₂}y^{a₂}⋯x^{ε_n}y^{a_n}. A **pinch** is a cyclic subword X y^{Pj} x or x y^{Qj} X; replacing it by y^{Qj}, resp. y^{Pj}, gives a conjugate element with two x-letters fewer. W is *cyclically Britton-reduced* if it has no pinch.
(i) (Collins) If W, W′ are cyclically Britton-reduced, n(W) ≥ 1, and conjugate in BS(P,Q), then n(W′) = n(W), and W′ has a rotation V = x^{ε₁}y^{a′₁}⋯x^{ε_n}y^{a′_n} (same signs) with W = z V z⁻¹ in the group for some power z of y.
(ii) (normal form theorem) Two Britton-reduced sequences with the same signs represent the same element iff they differ by **slides**: with (out_i, in_i) = (P, Q) if ε_i = +1 and (Q, P) if ε_i = −1 (from y^P x = x y^Q, y^Q X = X y^P), a slide k_i at x^{ε_i} subtracts out_i·k_i from the exponent on its left and adds in_i·k_i to the exponent on its right.
Combining (i) and (ii), cyclically: **a′_i = a_i + in_i k_i − out_{i+1} k_{i+1} (indices mod n) for some k ∈ ℤⁿ** (the conjugator z is the slide through x^{ε₁} read around the cycle). Conversely any such a′ gives a conjugate. The slide matrix has determinant Q^p P^q − P^p Q^q (p, q the numbers of positive and negative signs), nonzero when the x-exponent sum is nonzero and P ≠ Q, so "all conjugates of length ≤ L" is a finite exact computation (`BS.min_conjugates`).

## 4. The Miller–Schupp nodes MS(n, w₁), MS(n, w₂) are plateaus of the ACM graph (T3–T5; proved here)

Throughout n ≥ 3. Let ι be the automorphism y ↦ y⁻¹ of F. As words ι(w₁) = Xyxxy = w₂⁻¹, ι(m_n) = XYⁿxy^{n+1} is a rotation of m_n⁻¹, and ι(m_n′) is a rotation of (m_n′)⁻¹. An automorphism transports ACM moves (s′ ~ s^{±1} in G_r iff θ(s′) ~ θ(s)^{±1} in G_{θ(r)}) and ι preserves length, so **every statement below for w₂ is the ι-image of the statement for w₁**; proofs are given for w₁.

**Theorem A (w-direction; proved here, using the classical facts of §3).** Let P, Q ≥ 3, P ≠ Q. Every cyclically reduced word W′ conjugate to w_i^{±1} in BS(P,Q) has |W′| ≥ 5, with equality iff W′ ∈ Orb(w_i). In particular this holds in G_{m_n} = BS(n,n+1) and G_{m_n′} = BS(n+1,n). Moreover, if Q = P+1 then b = xyxYXY is conjugate to w₁ in BS(P,Q).

*Proof.* Replacing W′ by its inverse we may assume W′ ~ w₁. Cyclically, w₁ = X y⁻¹ x y⁰ x y⁻¹: signs ε = (−,+,+), exponents a = (−1, 0, −1). Its only sign changes are X y⁻¹ x and x y⁻¹ X, and −1 is divisible by neither P nor Q, so w₁ is cyclically Britton-reduced with three x-letters. If W′ is not cyclically Britton-reduced, removing pinches leads (Collins (i): the final word has exactly 3 x-letters and is not a power of y) to a word with 3 x-letters, so W′ has at least 5 x-letters and contains a pinch X y^{Pj} x or x y^{Qj} X with j ≠ 0 (W′ is freely cyclically reduced), i.e. at least 3 letters y^{±1}: |W′| ≥ 8. If W′ is cyclically Britton-reduced, then after a rotation it is X y^{a′₁} x y^{a′₂} x y^{a′₃} with, by the slide formula ((out,in) = (Q,P), (P,Q), (P,Q)),

    a′₁ = −1 + P(k₁ − k₂),   a′₂ = Q k₂ − P k₃,   a′₃ = −1 + Q(k₃ − k₁).

Since P, Q ≥ 3, a′₁ ≠ 0 and a′₃ ≠ 0, so |W′| = 3 + Σ|a′_i| ≥ 5; equality forces a′₁ = a′₃ = −1 (the only residues of absolute value 1), hence k₁ = k₂ = k₃ = k, and a′₂ = (Q−P)k = 0 forces k = 0: W′ is a rotation of w₁. Finally b is the rotation X y⁻¹ x y x y⁻¹ of itself, with exponents a + (0,1,0), obtained with k₁ = k₂ = k₃ = 1 when Q − P = 1. ∎

**Theorem B (m-direction; proved here, using research-j Lemma J0).** Every cyclically reduced word W conjugate to m_n^{±1} in G_{w_i} has |W| ≥ 2n+3, with equality iff W ∈ Orb(m_n) ∪ Orb(m_n′).

*Proof (i = 1).* In the basis (x, t = yx) of F, w₁ = X·xT·xx·xT is conjugate to x³t⁻², so G_{w₁} = ⟨x,t | x³ = t²⟩, with centre ⟨x³⟩ and quotient C₃ * C₂ = ⟨a⟩ * ⟨t⟩, x ↦ a, y = tx⁻¹ ↦ tā (ā = a⁻¹), Y ↦ at; abelianization x ↦ 2, t ↦ 3, y ↦ 1. By Lemma J0 two elements are conjugate iff their cyclically reduced syllable necklaces in C₃ * C₂ agree and their abelianizations agree. The image of m_n = XyⁿxY^{n+1} is ā(tā)ⁿa(at)^{n+1} = (āt)ⁿ(at)^{n+1}: cyclically reduced, 4n+2 syllables, a-exponent necklace (−1)ⁿ(+1)^{n+1}, abelianization −1; for m_n⁻¹: necklace (−1)^{n+1}(+1)ⁿ, abelianization +1.

Let W have n_x letters x^{±1} and n_y letters y^{±1}. (1) ab(W) = 2e_x + e_y = ±1, so n_y is odd. (2) Displacement (Lemma 3 with ω = (1,2)): n_x + 2n_y ≥ 4n+2, and the cyclic syllable length of the image of W is n_x + 2n_y minus the number of syllables lost in reduction, so the *loss* is exactly n_x + 2n_y − (4n+2). Assume |W| = n_x + n_y ≤ 2n+3. Then n_y ≥ 2n−1, so (n_x, n_y) is one of (0, 2n+3), (0, 2n+1), (1, 2n+1), (2, 2n+1), (4, 2n−1) (n_y odd; n_x ≥ 4n+2−2n_y). The first two are powers of y with |ab| ≥ 7. (1, 2n+1) is x^{±1}y^k with |k| = 2n+1 and ab = ±2 + k ≠ ±1. So |W| ≥ 2n+3, and at equality (n_x, n_y) ∈ {(2, 2n+1), (4, 2n−1)}.

*(4, 2n−1): loss 0.* Then no two syllables merge. xx gives a·a, so x-letters are isolated; y·x^{±1} gives ā·a^{±1} and x^{±1}·Y gives a^{±1}·a, so every x-letter is preceded by Y and followed by y. But the y-run following an x-letter is followed by another x-letter, which is then preceded by y: contradiction.

*(2, 2n+1): loss 2.* If the two x-letters are adjacent, W = x^{±2}y^k, |k| = 2n+1, ab = ±4 + k ≠ ±1 for n ≥ 3. Otherwise W = x^{ε₁}y^{k₁}x^{ε₂}y^{k₂}, and the loss is the sum of the losses at the two x-letters, computed from the last syllable of the run on the left (ā after y, t after Y) and the first syllable of the run on the right (t before y, a before Y); writing (sign of left run, ε, sign of right run): (+,+,−): āa = 1, loss 2. (+,−,+): āā = a, loss 1. (+,−,−): āāa = ā, loss 2. (−,+,+) and (−,−,+): loss 0. (−,+,−): aa = ā, loss 1. (+,+,+) and (−,−,−): after āa = 1 (resp. āa = 1 between X and Y) the two exposed t's cancel as well, loss ≥ 4. In the admissible cases only the x-syllable, the last syllable of the left run and the first syllable of the right run are involved, so the two junctions do not interact. Total loss 2 leaves: both runs positive and ε₁ = ε₂ = −1 (ab = 2n−3 ≠ ±1); both negative and ε₁ = ε₂ = +1 (ab = 3−2n ≠ ±1); or runs of opposite signs, W = x^{ε₁}y^kx^{ε₂}Y^l with k, l ≥ 1, k + l = 2n+1 (losses 0 + 2 for all four sign choices). In the last case the image is a^{ε₁}(tā)^{k−1}t(at)^l if ε₂ = +1 and a^{ε₁}(tā)^kt(at)^{l−1} if ε₂ = −1: a two-block necklace with N₋ = k − [ε₂ = +1] + [ε₁ = −1] entries −1, and ab(W) = 2(ε₁+ε₂) + 2k − (2n+1). W ~ m_n iff (N₋, ab) = (n, −1); W ~ m_n⁻¹ iff (N₋, ab) = (n+1, +1). Both systems reduce to −(ε₁+ε₂) − [ε₂ = +1] + [ε₁ = −1] = 0, i.e. ε₁ = −ε₂, with k = n, resp. k = n+1. The four solutions are XyⁿxY^{n+1} = m_n, xyⁿXY^{n+1} (a rotation of (m_n′)⁻¹), xy^{n+1}XYⁿ (a rotation of m_n⁻¹) and Xy^{n+1}xYⁿ = m_n′. ∎

(So m_n′ ~ m_n⁻¹ in G_{w₁}: the pair m_n, m_n′ plays the role of research-a's p_n, q_n.)

*Proof.* Induction along the path exactly as in research-a: with the first relator in Orb(m_n) (resp. Orb(m_n′)) frozen, the group is BS(n,n+1) (resp. BS(n+1,n)) and Theorem A keeps the second relator in Orb(w_i); with w_i frozen, Theorem B keeps the first in Orb(m_n) ∪ Orb(m_n′) (the conjugacy class of (m_n′)^{±1} in G_{w_i} is that of m_n^{∓1}). ### 5.1 P_3 and Q: free-by-cyclic relators, where displacement and slides say nothing

Here c = xyxYYXy, k₃ = xxxYXyy, P_3 = (c, k₃) (research-k), q₁ = xxyXYY, q₂ = xxxYYXy, Q = (q₁, q₂) (research-d), q₂′ = xxYYYXy, θ: x ↦ x⁻¹, swap: x ↔ y. Direct checks (`selftest_fbc`): q₂ is a rotation of θ(k₃⁻¹); swap(q₁) = q₁⁻¹ up to rotation; swap(q₂) is a rotation of (q₂′)⁻¹. So **θ(Q) = (XXyxYY, k₃) up to Orb**, and swap(Q) = (q₁, q₂′) up to Orb.

All four frozen groups are free-by-cyclic: G_c ≅ F₂ ⋊ ℤ (research-o Thm O1, monodromy matrix [[0,1],[1,−1]]); G_{q₁} ≅ F(a,b) ⋊ ℤ with y = xa, b = xax⁻¹ and xbx⁻¹ = ab (proved here by the argument of research-o Thm O1: x²y = y²x reads a₃x³ = a₁a₂x³ for a_i = xⁱax⁻ⁱ), monodromy [[0,1],[1,1]]; G_{k₃} ≅ F(t₀,t₁,t₂) ⋊ ℤ (research-o §3; t = yx², ψ: t₀ ↦ t₁ ↦ t₂ ↦ t₂t₀), and G_{q₂} = θ(G_{k₃}). For such a group the HNN tree is a line, Lemma 3 gives only |degree|, and the analogue of the slide lattice, (1 − M)ℤ^k for the monodromy matrix M, is all of ℤ^k because det(1 − M) = ±Δ(1) = ±1. In BS(P,Q) the rigidity of Theorem A came from the proper edge groups Pℤ, Qℤ; a free-by-cyclic group has no such residues at the abelian level. Local minimality is then a twisted-conjugacy question in a free group (research-o §2.2), and I used two exact tools that are not mine:

- **(index-2 sieve; method of research-o §6.3).** Let G = F(a,b) ⋊_φ ⟨z⟩ and H = F(a,b) ⋊_{φ²} ⟨z²⟩, of index 2. If W ~ s^{±1} in G (odd degree), then W² is H-conjugate to s^{±2} or to z s^{±2} z⁻¹ (write the conjugator as h z^δ). So either tr²ρ(W²) = tr²ρ(s²) for *all* homomorphisms ρ: H → PSL(2,p), or tr²ρ(W²) = tr²ρ(z s² z⁻¹) for all of them. All ρ are enumerated up to conjugacy: ρ(z²) = T over GL₂-class representatives, ρ(a) arbitrary, ρ(b) forced by T a T⁻¹ = φ²(a), the second relation checked up to sign.
- **(twisted traces; research-th03 §7, script `th03_q2.GK`).** A linear functional ℓ on the Magnus algebra of F(t₀,t₁,t₂) truncated in degree 3 with ℓ(X_i m) = ℓ(m Ψ(X_i)) for all i, m (Ψ induced by ψ) satisfies ℓ(a m) = ℓ(m Ψ(a)) for all a (induction on the degree of a), hence, with g = 𝔐(v) and 𝔐(ψ(v)) = Ψ(g): ℓ(g·𝔐(u)·Ψ(g)⁻¹) = ℓ(𝔐(u)·Ψ(g)⁻¹·Ψ(g)) = ℓ(𝔐(u)). So u ↦ ℓ(𝔐(u)) is constant on twisted conjugacy classes, i.e. on the conjugacy class of (u,1) (research-o §2.2). The solution space is 3-dimensional (exact linear algebra over ℚ).

**Theorem H (Q is an isolated local minimum of the ACM graph; proved here by exact computation, `--fbc`).**
(a) Every cyclically reduced word conjugate to q₂^{±1} in G_{q₁} has length ≥ 7, and those of length 7 are exactly Orb(q₂). In particular q₂′ is **not** conjugate to q₂^{±1} in G_{q₁}.
(b) Every cyclically reduced word conjugate to q₁^{±1} in G_{q₂} has length ≥ 6, and those of length 6 are exactly Orb(q₁).
(c) Hence no ACM move from Q decreases the length, every non-increasing ACM path from Q stays in Orb(q₁) × Orb(q₂) (it only rotates and inverts the two relators), and every ACM path from Q to (x,y) contains a state of total length ≥ 14.

*Proof.* (a) Conjugates have degree ±1 for the degree x, y ↦ 1. Among the 928 cyclically reduced words of length ≤ 7 and degree ±1, the index-2 sieve with p = 7 (162 homomorphisms) leaves exactly the 28 words of Orb(q₂) ∪ Orb(q₂′); p = 13 (390) removes nothing more, and cannot: SL₂-traces do not see the mirror. For q₂′ use an automorphism instead: β: x ↦ xy⁻¹x⁻¹, y ↦ y⁻¹x⁻¹ is an automorphism of F (x = β(x)β(y)⁻¹) and β(q₁) is conjugate to the rotation xYYXyxy of c, so β induces G_{q₁} ≅ G_c. Since swap(q₂) is a rotation of (q₂′)⁻¹ and has the same degree as q₂, q₂′ ~ q₂^{±1} iff swap(q₂) ~ q₂ in G_{q₁}, iff β(swap q₂) ~ β(q₂) in G_c. Normalized to degree 1, these two elements have normal forms (aBaba, 1) and (abABAB, 1) in research-o's coordinates, on which research-th03's mod-2 twisted trace ε (its T16: ε(u) = C(p+1,2) + C(q+1,2) + μ_ab(u) mod 2 is constant on conjugacy classes of degree-1 elements of G_c) takes the values 1 and 0. So they are not conjugate, and Orb(q₂′) drops out. (b) W ~ q₁^{±1} in G_{q₂} iff θ(W) ~ θ(q₁)^{±1} in G_{k₃}, and θ preserves length. θ(q₁) = XXyxYY has degree 1 (x ↦ 1, y ↦ −2) and twisted-trace invariant (1, −2, −2); among the cyclically reduced words of length ≤ 6 and degree ±1 exactly the 12 words of Orb(θ(q₁)) have this invariant (words of degree −1 are inverted first). (c) Induction along the path, as in research-a: with q₂ (or a rotation or inverse of it) frozen use (b), with q₁ frozen use (a). ∎

(Unlike AK(n) and MS(n,w_i), whose plateaus consist of two orbits — p_n, q_n, resp. m_n, m_n′ — the plateau of Q is a single orbit: the mirror image swap(Q) = (q₁, q₂′) up to Orb is another local minimum, not reachable from Q without increasing the length.)

**Corollary H′ (the exits of height 14; proved here from Proposition G(b) below and parity).** From the state (q₁, q₂), the ACM neighbours of total length 14 are exactly the pairs (u, q₂) with u ∈ Orb(θ(c)), θ(c) = XyXYYxy: with q₁ frozen there is none, because a conjugate of q₂^{±1} in G_{q₁} has odd degree, hence odd length, so the next possible length is 9; with q₂ frozen, the θ-image of Proposition G(b) says that the words of length ≤ 7 conjugate to q₁^{±1} in G_{q₂} are Orb(q₁) and Orb(θ(c)). So **the only exit of height 14 from (q₁, q₂) is the passage to θ(P_3)** (K's presentation, mirrored).

**Proposition G (P_3 is not a local minimum; proved here, certificate — an independent duplicate of research-th03 T14/T18).** *Overlap.* I found (a)–(c) at 04:40 UTC from the twisted-trace survivors and only afterwards (04:53 UTC) saw that TH03's draft now contains the same facts: its T18 gives (a) and (b) (classification up to length 8, certificate `th03_p3_to_len13_k3_frozen.json`, 22 moves, and the identification with the image of Q under x ↦ x⁻¹), and its T14 gives (c) and more (exactly one further cyclic word of length 7, XYxxyXy, is a c-frozen neighbour; the mirror words are excluded by its invariant ε). The statements agree; the c-frozen side is confirmed here with a different tool (index-2 PSL(2,p) instead of TH03's invariants I, J, ε).
(a) In G_{k₃}: y⁻¹cy = yxy⁻²x⁻² (exact normal forms; equivalently three cyclic products with rotations of k₃^{±1}: c → xYYXyyyxxx → YXyyyxYYx → yxYYXX). ## 7. Failed attempts (with reasons)

1. **The seed's literal theorem.** A Greendlinger/Schupp-type length rigidity for C′(λ) relators is vacuous for every relator of length 3–12 (Proposition 1) and impossible for the groups at hand (Remark 2b). I did not prove the long-relator version: I could not consult the statement of the thin-annulus theorem (Lyndon–Schupp V.5), and a self-contained proof via disc diagrams (cut the annulus along a shortest conjugator and apply Greendlinger's lemma to the four corners) needs a case analysis I did not finish; nothing here depends on it.
2. **Non-metric C(6)** holds for q₁, q₂, k₃ but gives no length rigidity (hexagon rings with boundary lengths N and 3N, Remark 2c); not pursued.
3. **Cheap finite quotients for P_3 and Q** (`--sieve`): PSL(2,p), p ≤ 13, on the group itself (trace) together with S_n, n ≤ 8 (cycle type) leave 162 (P_3, c frozen), 46 (P_3, k₃ frozen), 58 (Q, q₁ frozen) and 10 (Q, q₂ frozen: x^{±1} and Orb(x³Y)) shorter words unseparated, among them generators. Reason: SL₂-traces do not separate a word from its reverse, and these groups have almost no small non-abelian quotients (research-o §6.1). They were replaced by the two tools of §5.1.
4. **Index-2 sieve for G_{k₃}** (H = F₃ ⋊_{ψ²} ℤ, p ≤ 13, 118–874 homomorphisms per prime): separates nothing beyond the cheap sieves (68 shorter words remain). Replaced by TH03's twisted traces, which settle this side completely.
5. **Uniform shortening of P_n.** For n = 4, 5, 6 no word shorter than c was found in the k_n-frozen class of c within ≤ 4 cyclic products and length ≤ 2n+8; I have no proof either way.
6. **The mirror on Q's plateau, by direct means.** Whether q₂′ ~ q₂^{±1} in G_{q₁} (twisted conjugacy of BABBAb and Ababbab for a ↦ b ↦ ab) could not be decided by PSL(2,p) traces of squares (p = 7, 13), by twisted orbits in S_n (n ≤ 6, all pairs of images), or by exhaustive search of the twisted class within word length ≤ 22 (146,055 elements; cap 28 exhausted a budget of 3·10⁶). It was then settled negatively by transporting the question to G_c and using TH03's ε (Theorem H(a)).

## 8. Honest limits

- Nothing here trivializes or obstructs AK(3). A local minimum of the ACM graph is a statement about single generalized moves; AC-trivial presentations also sit on plateaus, and research-k (K2) shows that no monotone quantity exists. Theorem H and research-a say only that the two length-13 members cannot be shortened in one move.
- Theorems A–D are proved by hand, modulo the quoted classical theorems (Britton's lemma, the normal form theorem and Collins' lemma for HNN extensions; the conjugacy theorem for free products) and research-j's Lemma J0; the theorem numbers in Lyndon–Schupp are cited from memory. They are cross-checked by exact computation for n = 3..8.
- Theorem H, Proposition G(b),(c) and Proposition E are exact finite computations and depend on the correctness of `th16_rigidity.py` and, for the G_{k₃} side, of TH03's `th03_q2.GK` (which is being refereed separately in this round). Proposition G(a) is independent of both: it is a replayable certificate.
- For Q the first exit has height exactly 14 (Corollary H′); nothing is proved about what happens beyond it. Theorem H(a) for q₂′ rests on TH03's mirror invariant ε (its T16).
- Proposition E uses research-d's list of 34 benchmark members as given. No novelty claim: MS–AK relations are classical (Miller–Schupp, Myasnikov–Myasnikov–Shpilrain, Panteleev–Ushakov), and I could not check the literature for the plateau statements.

5. **Do not invest in metric small cancellation for this problem** (Proposition 1, Remark 2); if a diagrammatic approach is wanted, the non-metric hexagon geometry of G_{q₁} (C(6) with six one-letter pieces, link K₄) is the only place where it is not vacuous.
