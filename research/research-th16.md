## 2. Classical small cancellation cannot apply to short relators in rank 2 (T1; proved here)

Definitions (Lyndon–Schupp, Ch. V): for a cyclically reduced r, R = Orb(r) is its symmetrized closure; a **piece** is a nonempty common prefix of two distinct elements of R; C′(λ): every piece u of r′ ∈ R has |u| < λ|r′|; C(p): no element of R is a product of fewer than p pieces. Write μ(r) for the maximal piece length.

**Proposition 1 (pigeonhole; proved here).** Let r ∈ F₂ be cyclically reduced of length ℓ, not a proper power. Then 2ℓ ≤ 4·3^{μ(r)}. Consequently, if R satisfies C′(1/6) then ℓ ≤ 2 or ℓ ≥ 13, and if R satisfies C′(1/8) then ℓ ≤ 2, ℓ ∈ {17, 18} or ℓ ≥ 25.

*Proof.* Since r is not a proper power and r is not conjugate to r⁻¹ in a free group, the 2ℓ words of R are pairwise distinct. Two distinct words of equal length ℓ have a common prefix of length < ℓ, so μ := μ(r) ≤ ℓ − 1, and by definition of μ no two elements of R share their prefix of length μ+1. Hence "prefix of length μ+1" is an injection of R into the set of reduced words of length μ+1, which has 4·3^μ elements. Under C′(λ), μ < λℓ, i.e. μ ≤ ⌈λℓ⌉ − 1. For λ = 1/6: if 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 6 then μ = 0 and 2ℓ ≤ 4, impossible; if 7 ≤ ℓ ≤ 12 then μ ≤ 1 and 2ℓ ≤ 12, impossible. For λ = 1/8: 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 8 gives μ = 0 (impossible); 9 ≤ ℓ ≤ 16 gives μ ≤ 1, ℓ ≤ 6 (impossible); 17 ≤ ℓ ≤ 24 gives μ ≤ 2, ℓ ≤ 18. ∎

(A relator of a balanced presentation of the trivial group is never a proper power: its exponent vector is a row of a unimodular matrix. Brute force confirms the C′(1/6) statement for 3 ≤ ℓ ≤ 11: `c16_possible`, §6.)

**Exact piece data (computation, `--pieces`).** "min # pieces" is the least number of pieces whose product is a cyclic conjugate of r (C(p) holds iff it is ≥ p); "triangle" says whether the star graph of r on {x,X,y,Y} contains a triangle (then T(4) fails; Hill–Pride–Vella's star-graph form of T(q), externally reported, used only for this remark).

So no relator of AK(3), P_3 = (c,k₃), Q = (q₁,q₂) or MS(3,w_i) satisfies C′(1/6) (all have μ/ℓ ≥ 1/6), none satisfies C(4)–T(4) (the two triangle-free ones, b and p₃, are torus relators with min # pieces 4 < 6, and for them G_r has centre, which is the mechanism research-a exploits instead), and only k₃, q₁, q₂ satisfy C(6) as single relators. Joint maximal pieces: AK(3) 3, P_3 3, Q 2, MS(3,w_i) 3, against shorter relator lengths 6, 7, 6, 5.

**Remark 2 (why this is unavoidable; externally reported facts).** (a) Dehn's algorithm (Lyndon–Schupp Thm V.4.4: if R satisfies C′(λ), λ ≤ 1/6, every nontrivial word in the normal closure contains a subword longer than (1−3λ)|r′| of some r′ ∈ R) shows that a pair {r,s} whose *joint* symmetrized closure is C′(1/6) presents a nontrivial group unless a relator has length 1; so every nontrivial member of an AC class of balanced trivial presentations violates joint C′(1/6). (b) C′(1/6) groups are word-hyperbolic (Gromov) and hyperbolic groups contain no ℤ² and no Baumslag–Solitar subgroups; the trefoil group B = G_b, the torus-knot groups G_{p_n}, BS(3,4) = G_{m₃} and G_c (which contains a Klein-bottle group, research-o §2.3) therefore admit no C′(1/6) presentation whatsoever. The seed's metric theorem (for a C′(1/8) relator r and a short partner s, the conjugates of s^{±1} in G_r other than Orb(s) are long; it would follow from the thin-annulus theorem for conjugacy diagrams, Lyndon–Schupp V.5, which I could not consult here and therefore do **not** state or use) can only concern long relators, |r| ≥ 17; I did not examine whether AK(3)'s class contains such pairs. (c) q₁, q₂ and k₃ do satisfy the *non-metric* condition C(6) as single relators (q₁ = xxyXYY even has exactly the six one-letter pieces: each of the 12 reduced two-letter words occurs exactly once in q₁^{±1}). Non-metric C(6) gives no length rigidity by itself: a ring of N hexagons, each with two interior edges separated by one boundary edge on one side and three on the other, is an annular (3,6)-map with boundary lengths N and 3N. ## 3. The substitute: displacement, and slides (T2)

**Lemma 3 (displacement; proved here).** Let G_r act by isometries on a metric space Ω and fix v₀ ∈ Ω. Put ω(g) = d(v₀, g v₀) for g ∈ {x, y}, ω(W) = ω(x)·n_x(W) + ω(y)·n_y(W) for a word W with n_x letters x^{±1} and n_y letters y^{±1}, and ‖g‖ = min_h d(h v₀, g h v₀) for g ∈ G_r. Then ‖·‖ is invariant under conjugation and inversion, and every word s′ whose image in G_r is conjugate to s^{±1} satisfies ω(s′) ≥ ‖s‖.

*Proof.* ‖hgh⁻¹‖ = ‖g‖ by definition; d(v, g⁻¹v) = d(gv, v). For s′ = g₁⋯g_L (letters), the triangle inequality along v₀, g₁v₀, g₁g₂v₀, … gives d(v₀, s′v₀) ≤ Σ d(v₀, g_i v₀) = ω(s′), and ‖s‖ = ‖s′‖ ≤ d(v₀, s′v₀). ∎

*Instances.* For a homomorphism G_r → A * B take the Bass–Serre tree and v₀ = midpoint of the base edge: d(v₀, g v₀) is the syllable length of g, and ‖g‖ is the syllable length of a cyclically reduced conjugate (1 for nontrivial elliptic g); all cyclically reduced conjugates are rotations of each other (Magnus–Karrass–Solitar Thm 4.2). research-a Lemma 1 is ω = (2,2), ‖p_n‖ = 4n+2; Lemma 2 is ω = (1,1), ‖b‖ = 6. Section 4 uses ω = (1,2). For an HNN extension with stable letter x and v₀ the base vertex, ω = (1,0): the lemma bounds only the number of x-letters, and the y-letters must be controlled by the next statement.

**Classical facts for BS(P,Q) = ⟨x,y | x⁻¹y^P x = y^Q⟩ (externally reported: Britton's lemma and the normal form theorem, Lyndon–Schupp Thm IV.2.1; Collins' lemma, Thm IV.2.5; numbering from memory).** Write a cyclic word with n ≥ 1 letters x^{±1} as W = x^{ε₁}y^{a₁}x^{ε₂}y^{a₂}⋯x^{ε_n}y^{a_n}. A **pinch** is a cyclic subword X y^{Pj} x or x y^{Qj} X; replacing it by y^{Qj}, resp. y^{Pj}, gives a conjugate element with two x-letters fewer. W is *cyclically Britton-reduced* if it has no pinch.
(i) (Collins) If W, W′ are cyclically Britton-reduced, n(W) ≥ 1, and conjugate in BS(P,Q), then n(W′) = n(W), and W′ has a rotation V = x^{ε₁}y^{a′₁}⋯x^{ε_n}y^{a′_n} (same signs) with W = z V z⁻¹ in the group for some power z of y.
(ii) (normal form theorem) Two Britton-reduced sequences with the same signs represent the same element iff they differ by **slides**: with (out_i, in_i) = (P, Q) if ε_i = +1 and (Q, P) if ε_i = −1 (from y^P x = x y^Q, y^Q X = X y^P), a slide k_i at x^{ε_i} subtracts out_i·k_i from the exponent on its left and adds in_i·k_i to the exponent on its right.
Combining (i) and (ii), cyclically: **a′_i = a_i + in_i k_i − out_{i+1} k_{i+1} (indices mod n) for some k ∈ ℤⁿ** (the conjugator z is the slide through x^{ε₁} read around the cycle). Conversely any such a′ gives a conjugate. The slide matrix has determinant Q^p P^q − P^p Q^q (p, q the numbers of positive and negative signs), nonzero when the x-exponent sum is nonzero and P ≠ Q, so "all conjugates of length ≤ L" is a finite exact computation (`BS.min_conjugates`).

## 4. The Miller–Schupp nodes MS(n, w₁), MS(n, w₂) are plateaus of the ACM graph (T3–T5; proved here)

Throughout n ≥ 3. Let ι be the automorphism y ↦ y⁻¹ of F. As words ι(w₁) = Xyxxy = w₂⁻¹, ι(m_n) = XYⁿxy^{n+1} is a rotation of m_n⁻¹, and ι(m_n′) is a rotation of (m_n′)⁻¹. An automorphism transports ACM moves (s′ ~ s^{±1} in G_r iff θ(s′) ~ θ(s)^{±1} in G_{θ(r)}) and ι preserves length, so **every statement below for w₂ is the ι-image of the statement for w₁**; proofs are given for w₁.

**Theorem A (w-direction; proved here, using the classical facts of §3).** Let P, Q ≥ 3, P ≠ Q. Every cyclically reduced word W′ conjugate to w_i^{±1} in BS(P,Q) has |W′| ≥ 5, with equality iff W′ ∈ Orb(w_i). In particular this holds in G_{m_n} = BS(n,n+1) and G_{m_n′} = BS(n+1,n). Moreover, if Q = P+1 then b = xyxYXY is conjugate to w₁ in BS(P,Q).

*Proof.* Replacing W′ by its inverse we may assume W′ ~ w₁. Cyclically, w₁ = X y⁻¹ x y⁰ x y⁻¹: signs ε = (−,+,+), exponents a = (−1, 0, −1). Its only sign changes are X y⁻¹ x and x y⁻¹ X, and −1 is divisible by neither P nor Q, so w₁ is cyclically Britton-reduced with three x-letters. If W′ is not cyclically Britton-reduced, removing pinches leads (Collins (i): the final word has exactly 3 x-letters and is not a power of y) to a word with 3 x-letters, so W′ has at least 5 x-letters and contains a pinch X y^{Pj} x or x y^{Qj} X with j ≠ 0 (W′ is freely cyclically reduced), i.e. at least 3 letters y^{±1}: |W′| ≥ 8. If W′ is cyclically Britton-reduced, then after a rotation it is X y^{a′₁} x y^{a′₂} x y^{a′₃} with, by the slide formula ((out,in) = (Q,P), (P,Q), (P,Q)),

    a′₁ = −1 + P(k₁ − k₂),   a′₂ = Q k₂ − P k₃,   a′₃ = −1 + Q(k₃ − k₁).

Since P, Q ≥ 3, a′₁ ≠ 0 and a′₃ ≠ 0, so |W′| = 3 + Σ|a′_i| ≥ 5; equality forces a′₁ = a′₃ = −1 (the only residues of absolute value 1), hence k₁ = k₂ = k₃ = k, and a′₂ = (Q−P)k = 0 forces k = 0: W′ is a rotation of w₁. Finally b is the rotation X y⁻¹ x y x y⁻¹ of itself, with exponents a + (0,1,0), obtained with k₁ = k₂ = k₃ = 1 when Q − P = 1. ∎

**Theorem B (m-direction; proved here, using research-j Lemma J0).** Every cyclically reduced word W conjugate to m_n^{±1} in G_{w_i} has |W| ≥ 2n+3, with equality iff W ∈ Orb(m_n) ∪ Orb(m_n′).

*Proof (i = 1).* In the basis (x, t = yx) of F, w₁ = X·xT·xx·xT is conjugate to x³t⁻², so G_{w₁} = ⟨x,t | x³ = t²⟩, with centre ⟨x³⟩ and quotient C₃ * C₂ = ⟨a⟩ * ⟨t⟩, x ↦ a, y = tx⁻¹ ↦ tā (ā = a⁻¹), Y ↦ at; abelianization x ↦ 2, t ↦ 3, y ↦ 1. By Lemma J0 two elements are conjugate iff their cyclically reduced syllable necklaces in C₃ * C₂ agree and their abelianizations agree. The image of m_n = XyⁿxY^{n+1} is ā(tā)ⁿa(at)^{n+1} = (āt)ⁿ(at)^{n+1}: cyclically reduced, 4n+2 syllables, a-exponent necklace (−1)ⁿ(+1)^{n+1}, abelianization −1; for m_n⁻¹: necklace (−1)^{n+1}(+1)ⁿ, abelianization +1.

Let W have n_x letters x^{±1} and n_y letters y^{±1}. (1) ab(W) = 2e_x + e_y = ±1, so n_y is odd. (2) Displacement (Lemma 3 with ω = (1,2)): n_x + 2n_y ≥ 4n+2, and the cyclic syllable length of the image of W is n_x + 2n_y minus the number of syllables lost in reduction, so the *loss* is exactly n_x + 2n_y − (4n+2). Assume |W| = n_x + n_y ≤ 2n+3. Then n_y ≥ 2n−1, so (n_x, n_y) is one of (0, 2n+3), (0, 2n+1), (1, 2n+1), (2, 2n+1), (4, 2n−1) (n_y odd; n_x ≥ 4n+2−2n_y). The first two are powers of y with |ab| ≥ 7. (1, 2n+1) is x^{±1}y^k with |k| = 2n+1 and ab = ±2 + k ≠ ±1. So |W| ≥ 2n+3, and at equality (n_x, n_y) ∈ {(2, 2n+1), (4, 2n−1)}.

*(4, 2n−1): loss 0.* Then no two syllables merge. xx gives a·a, so x-letters are isolated; y·x^{±1} gives ā·a^{±1} and x^{±1}·Y gives a^{±1}·a, so every x-letter is preceded by Y and followed by y. But the y-run following an x-letter is followed by another x-letter, which is then preceded by y: contradiction.

*(2, 2n+1): loss 2.* If the two x-letters are adjacent, W = x^{±2}y^k, |k| = 2n+1, ab = ±4 + k ≠ ±1 for n ≥ 3. Otherwise W = x^{ε₁}y^{k₁}x^{ε₂}y^{k₂}, and the loss is the sum of the losses at the two x-letters, computed from the last syllable of the run on the left (ā after y, t after Y) and the first syllable of the run on the right (t before y, a before Y); writing (sign of left run, ε, sign of right run): (+,+,−): āa = 1, loss 2. (+,−,+): āā = a, loss 1. (+,−,−): āāa = ā, loss 2. (−,+,+) and (−,−,+): loss 0. (−,+,−): aa = ā, loss 1. (+,+,+) and (−,−,−): after āa = 1 (resp. āa = 1 between X and Y) the two exposed t's cancel as well, loss ≥ 4. In the admissible cases only the x-syllable, the last syllable of the left run and the first syllable of the right run are involved, so the two junctions do not interact. Total loss 2 leaves: both runs positive and ε₁ = ε₂ = −1 (ab = 2n−3 ≠ ±1); both negative and ε₁ = ε₂ = +1 (ab = 3−2n ≠ ±1); or runs of opposite signs, W = x^{ε₁}y^kx^{ε₂}Y^l with k, l ≥ 1, k + l = 2n+1 (losses 0 + 2 for all four sign choices). In the last case the image is a^{ε₁}(tā)^{k−1}t(at)^l if ε₂ = +1 and a^{ε₁}(tā)^kt(at)^{l−1} if ε₂ = −1: a two-block necklace with N₋ = k − [ε₂ = +1] + [ε₁ = −1] entries −1, and ab(W) = 2(ε₁+ε₂) + 2k − (2n+1). W ~ m_n iff (N₋, ab) = (n, −1); W ~ m_n⁻¹ iff (N₋, ab) = (n+1, +1). Both systems reduce to −(ε₁+ε₂) − [ε₂ = +1] + [ε₁ = −1] = 0, i.e. ε₁ = −ε₂, with k = n, resp. k = n+1. The four solutions are XyⁿxY^{n+1} = m_n, xyⁿXY^{n+1} (a rotation of (m_n′)⁻¹), xy^{n+1}XYⁿ (a rotation of m_n⁻¹) and Xy^{n+1}xYⁿ = m_n′. ∎

(So m_n′ ~ m_n⁻¹ in G_{w₁}: the pair m_n, m_n′ plays the role of research-a's p_n, q_n.)

**Theorem C (plateau; proved here).** For n ≥ 3 and i = 1, 2, every ACM path from MS(n,w_i) = (m_n, w_i) along which the total cyclic length never increases stays, up to order, in (Orb(m_n) ∪ Orb(m_n′)) × Orb(w_i). In particular MS(n,w_i) is a local minimum of the ACM graph, all states of the plateau have length 2n+8, and every ACM path from it to (x,y) — hence every ordinary AC trivialization, measured by cyclic length — contains a state of total length ≥ 2n+9. This bound is attained by a first exit: (m_n, b) is an ACM neighbour of MS(n,w₁) of length 2n+9.

*Proof.* Induction along the path exactly as in research-a: with the first relator in Orb(m_n) (resp. Orb(m_n′)) frozen, the group is BS(n,n+1) (resp. BS(n+1,n)) and Theorem A keeps the second relator in Orb(w_i); with w_i frozen, Theorem B keeps the first in Orb(m_n) ∪ Orb(m_n′) (the conjugacy class of (m_n′)^{±1} in G_{w_i} is that of m_n^{∓1}). The exit is the last sentence of Theorem A. ∎

**Proposition D (the saddle between the two plateaus; proved here).** For every n ≥ 1, m_n is conjugate to p_n in B = G_b. Hence AK(n) = (p_n, b) → (m_n, b) → (m_n, w₁) = MS(n,w₁) is a path of two ACM moves whose middle state has length 2n+9, the smallest value allowed by research-j Theorem J1 (n ≥ 3) for any path leaving AK(n)'s plateau.

*Proof.* With research-a's map x ↦ T²S, y ↦ ST², X ↦ ST, Y ↦ TS to C₂ * C₃: m_n ↦ (ST)(ST²)ⁿ(T²S)(TS)^{n+1} = ST(ST²)^{n−1}·STS·(TS)^{n+1}. Cyclically the last S cancels the first, merging the two neighbouring T's into T²: the T-exponent necklace is 2ⁿ1^{n+1}, that of p_n, and ab(m_n) = −1 = ab(p_n) (x, y ↦ 1). Lemma J0 gives m_n ~ p_n in B; Theorem A gives b ~ w₁ in BS(n,n+1) (its last sentence only needs Q = P+1). ∎

Proposition D is a two-line conceptual reason for research-c's theorem MS(n, y⁻¹x²y⁻¹) ~AC AK(n) (each ACM move is an AC composite by research-g Lemma G0); no new AC equivalence is claimed and none needs a new certificate. (TH07's draft Theorem A lists all length-(2n+3) words conjugate to p_n^{±1} in B; m_n, m_n′ are among them.)

## 5. The 34 benchmark members, and where the method stops (T6, T7)

**Proposition E (exact computation with the slide lattice; `--ms`).** The second relators of the 34 Miller–Schupp benchmark presentations certified in research-d §4.3 (first relator in Orb(m₃)) all have Britton length 3 and minimal BS(3,4)-conjugate length 5, and they lie in exactly **two** conjugacy classes of BS(3,4) up to inversion: 17 in the class of w₁ (the only one of length 5 is line 898, YXYxx ∈ Orb(w₁)) and 17 in the class of w₂ (line 939, YXXYx). Consequently each of the other 32 presentations (lengths 15–17) is a single m₃-frozen ACM move from MS(3,w₁) or MS(3,w₂), has a strictly shorter ACM neighbour, and is not a local minimum; the 34 "members" are two nodes of the ACM graph, exchanged by ι (y ↦ y⁻¹) up to Orb. For comparison: w₁ has 10 words of length 5 in its class up to inversion (= Orb(w₁)) and 36 of length 6 (among them b); m₃ has 36 conjugates of length 9 and 180 of length 10 in G_{w₁}.

**P_3 = (c, k₃) and Q = (q₁, q₂): the method gives nothing, and why (T7).** All four frozen groups are free-by-cyclic: G_c ≅ F₂ ⋊ ℤ (research-o Thm O1, monodromy matrix A = [[0,1],[1,−1]]); G_{q₁} ≅ F(a₀,a₁) ⋊ ℤ with y = xa₀, a_i = xⁱa₀x⁻ⁱ and a_{i+2} = a_i a_{i+1} (proved here by the argument of research-o Thm O1: x²y = y²x reads a₃x³ = a₁a₂x³, so the kernel of the degree map is free on a₀, a₁ and x acts by a₀ ↦ a₁, a₁ ↦ a₀a₁), monodromy [[0,1],[1,1]]; G_{k₃} ≅ F₃ ⋊ ℤ (research-o §3), and q₂ is a rotation of θ(k₃⁻¹) for θ: x ↦ x⁻¹ (direct check), so G_{q₂} ≅ G_{k₃}. For such a group the only action on a tree coming from the HNN structure is the action on a line through the degree, Lemma 3 gives only |degree|, and the analogue of the slide lattice is (1 − M)ℤ^k for the monodromy matrix M, which is *all* of ℤ^k: det(1 − M) = ±Δ(1) = ±1 (for G_c: 1; for G_{q₁}: −1; for G_{k₃}: Δ = t³ + t − 1). In BS(P,Q) the rigidity of Theorem A came from the proper edge groups Pℤ, Qℤ (residues of the exponents); in a free-by-cyclic group there are no residues at the abelian level. So for P_3 and Q the question "is it a local minimum?" is a genuine twisted-conjugacy question in a free group (research-o §2.2), outside the reach of displacement/slide arguments.

*Exact reformulation.* P_3 is a local minimum iff no cyclically reduced word of length ≤ 6 is conjugate to k₃^{±1} in G_c or to c^{±1} in G_{k₃}; Q is a local minimum iff no word of length ≤ 6 is conjugate to q₂^{±1} in G_{q₁} and no word of length ≤ 5 to q₁^{±1} in G_{q₂}. Since every balanced presentation of the trivial group of total length ≤ 12 is AC-trivial (externally reported: Havas–Ramsay; Miasnikov–Myasnikov) and AK(3) ~AC Q (research-d), **a shorter ACM neighbour of Q would trivialize AK(3)**; so "Q is a local minimum" is a *necessary* condition for AK(3) to be a counterexample, of the same kind as research-a's theorem for AK(3) itself, and it is not proved.
