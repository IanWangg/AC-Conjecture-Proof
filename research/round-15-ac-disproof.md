# Two independent conjugation phases at the external energy-24 pair

2026-11-19. Set $H=\langle \texttt{XXYxYXXYxxy},\texttt{XXYXYxxyXyxxy}\rangle$ in $F(x,y)$. For arbitrary $t,s\in F(x,y)$, put $J_t=\langle H,tHt^{-1}\rangle$ and $K=\langle J_t,s\rangle$. Then $\langle\!\langle J_t\rangle\!\rangle_K$ contains no primitive of $F(x,y)$. Consequently two independent conjugation phases cannot expose a primitive from the energy-24 pair, even with arbitrary Nielsen operations before, between and after the phases. The same statement for one phase fails at AK(2), where a 20-instruction ordinary path exposes a primitive. [Finite audit and strict control](../results/round15_ac_disproof_two_phases.json).

An independent conjugation phase conjugates the two rows by arbitrary words. One common conjugation lets us write its output as $(u,tvt^{-1})$ with $u,v\in H$. Initial Nielsen operations preserve $H$. Nielsen operations between phases preserve the subgroup generated by the outputs and thus keep the rows in $J_t$. Gauging the second phase the same way puts its output into the asserted normal closure in $K$. A Nielsen suffix keeps both rows there. The argument also permits arbitrary initial elements of $H$, independently of whether they form a basis. The theorem bounds this phase pattern. Round 15 independently gives a strict stable path to this pair at cyclic peak 35. [Stable replay](round-15-stable-proof.md).

## Finite reduction for a gapless first word

Round 13 supplies two inputs. Adjoining any word outside $H$ produces a proper rank-three subgroup. The normal closure of $H$ in every proper overgroup contains no ambient primitive. These already exclude one independent conjugation phase with any first word $t$ because $J_t$ lies in the normal closure of $H$ in $\langle H,t\rangle$. [Single-word ranks and proper overgroups](round-13-ac-disproof.md).

A word is gapless in a based folded core when its maximal readable prefix and maximal inverse suffix meet or overlap. Then it lies in the double coset of the difference $p_a p_b^{-1}$ of the two based tree paths to the split vertices. Adding that word has the same effect as identifying vertices $a,b$ and folding. Left multiplication of $t$ by an element of $H$ conjugates $J_t$ by that element, right multiplication leaves $J_t$ unchanged, and $J_{t^{-1}}=t^{-1}J_tt$. We can thus use the 136 unordered vertex pairs of the 17-vertex source core for all gapless $t$ outside $H$. If $t\in H$, the one-phase argument applies. [Gap criterion](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word).

For fixed $t$, a second word $s$ with a nonempty gap in the core of $J_t$ attaches a path with one new cycle after folding. The character killing $J_t$ and assigning one to a new chord has zero value on its normal closure. A sign-pure cyclic loop in the kernel cannot use that chord, because any nonzero edge count would require both orientations to cancel. Once the chord is removed, any added tree edges are bridges, which likewise cannot be used by a sign-pure cyclic loop. The loop stays in the core of $J_t$, where the one-phase result applies. This argument also handles a stem and circuit attached at a single vertex. If $s$ is gapless, one of the finitely many distinct-vertex identifications suffices, or $s\in J_t$. The associated overgroup $\langle H,t,s\rangle$ is computed by identifying the images of the same two vertices in the core of $\langle H,t\rangle$.

## Reduction for a first word with a nonempty gap

The initial core of $J_t$ consists of two disjoint copies of the source core joined by the gap path of $t$. To see this, read the prefix in the first copy and the inverse suffix in the second. At the endpoints of the gap, the missing directions leave the new path folded. The path from the first root to the second has label $t$. Attach an $s$-loop at the first root. The resulting graph has topological rank at most five and its fold represents $K$. Follow the images $D_1,D_2$ of the two copies through individual elementary folds, based at the images of their respective roots.

As long as $D_1,D_2$ are disjoint, their topological ranks add to at most five, and each rank is at least two because its labelled subgroup contains $H$. The labelled subgroup of $D_i$ changes only when a fold with at most one edge in $D_i$ identifies two distinct vertices of $D_i$. A fold with both edges in $D_i$ preserves its labelled subgroup. In the changing case the effect on $D_i$ is one vertex identification, so its new labelled subgroup is obtained by adjoining one word. Any strict enlargement from $H$ has rank three by Round 13. After that, a further change would require another vertex identification, raising the topological rank of $D_i$ to at least four while the other copy still has rank at least two. This exceeds the total rank bound. Thus each labelled subgroup stays $H$ or is generated by $H$ and one additional word. At most one copy can enlarge strictly before the first meeting.

If no meeting occurs, choose a spanning tree of the final folded graph extending spanning trees of both copies. Every non-tree edge outside the copies gives a signed count that vanishes on the normal closure, since the two generating copies can be transported to their roots along tree paths. Consequently an accepted sign-pure cyclic loop in the normal closure uses none of those outside non-tree edges. The remaining edges outside the copies are bridges of the remaining graph. The loop stays in one copy. Retracting to that copy's free factor puts the loop, based at its root by a path in the copy, in the normal closure of the corresponding copy of $H$. That subgroup is excluded by the proper-overgroup theorem, since the copy's labelled subgroup is $H$ or a proper rank-three enlargement.

If there is a first meeting, the fold identifies one vertex of each disjoint copy. It cannot merge an edge from one copy with an edge from the other, because their common initial vertex would already have been a meeting. The connected union therefore has topological rank equal to the sum of the two pre-fold ranks. Choose preimages $a,b$ in the original source copies of the two identified vertices, and let $q=p_a p_b^{-1}$. In the partially folded graph, both $q$ and $t$ label paths from the first root to the second root. Hence $tq^{-1}\in K$, and the normal closures of $J_t$ and $J_q$ in $K$ agree.

If neither labelled subgroup enlarged strictly, the union generates $J_q$. The union has topological rank at least four, so a tree extension of that connected subgraph shows that $K$ is generated by $J_q$ and at most one more word. If one labelled subgroup enlarged strictly, the union itself has topological rank five. No further independent loop is needed to generate $K$, so $K$ is again generated by $J_q$ and at most one additional word, supplied by the enlarged copy. All subsequent folds preserve the subgroup of $F(x,y)$ represented by the whole graph. This reduces the first meeting case to the gapless first word $q$ and an arbitrary second word, already covered by the finite census. The same reduction includes $q\in H$.
