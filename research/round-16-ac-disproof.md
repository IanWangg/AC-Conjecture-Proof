# Two conjugation phases at literal AK(3) and four external projections

2026-11-19. Let $H$ be the subgroup generated by the literal AK(3) rows or by one of the four energy-23 external pairs below. For arbitrary ambient words $t,s\in F(x,y)$, put $J_t=\langle H,tHt^{-1}\rangle$. The normal closure of $J_t$ in $\langle J_t,s\rangle$ contains no primitive of $F(x,y)$. Thus a path from any of these five markings to a primitive coordinate needs at least three independent conjugation phases, even with arbitrary Nielsen operations before, between and after the phases. The result extends the previous energy-24 theorem to the original AK(3) presentation. [Literal AK(3) replay](../results/round16_ac_disproof_literal.json), [four projection replays](../results/round16_ac_disproof_projections.json), [energy-24 theorem](round-15-ac-disproof.md).

## Proof inputs and finite cases

A common conjugation makes the first phase's two outputs elements of $J_t$. Applying the same gauge at the second phase puts both outputs in the normal closure of $J_t$ in $\langle J_t,s\rangle$. The intermediate Nielsen operations preserve the subgroup of the pair and the final ones preserve membership in the normal closure.

The geometric proof from Round 15 needs two types of proper subgroup checks. The first excludes primitives from the source and from its normal closure in each single-word enlargement. Every first vertex identification at the five source cores has rank three, with agreement between two folding implementations. The gap criterion then gives rank three for every outside first word. For an unread first gap, the new chord has signed count zero on the source normal closure. Its removal leaves the new tree edges as bridges, so a sign-pure cyclic loop in that normal closure would stay in the original core. All gapless first words are covered by the finite rank-three checks.

The second type of check excludes primitives from the normal closure of $H$ in the proper cumulative subgroups $\langle H,t,s\rangle$ arising in the finite case. A gapless $t$ lies in a double coset represented by a difference of source vertex paths. Reversing the pair conjugates the two-copy subgroup. A second word with a gap in the $J_t$ core can again be handled by the chord argument. For the remaining second words, identify two vertices of that core and their images in the core of $\langle H,t\rangle$. The latter fold gives the proper cumulative subgroup or the ambient rose. Each of the proper graphs so obtained has rank three or four. The no-contact case for a first word with a gap uses only the single-word exclusions above. The first-contact case reduces to a gapless first word and an arbitrary second word, so this finite check also gives the arbitrary-word theorem. [First-contact proof](round-15-ac-disproof.md#reduction-for-a-first-word-with-a-nonempty-gap), [one-word gap argument](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word).

## AK(2) two-phase positive control

Start from $(\texttt{yyXXy},\texttt{xyxYXY})$, the AK(2) marking for which Round 11 proved primitive exclusion in every proper overgroup. Conjugate row 0 by $y$ and multiply row 1 on the right by row 0. Conjugate row 1 by $\texttt{xYX}$ and multiply row 0 on the right by row 1. Expanding the three-letter conjugation gives six strict instructions with first and second phase lengths one and three. The new first row is $\texttt{yyXyyXyX}$, which matches the rank-two primitive conjugacy class with signed counts $(3,5)$ on $(X,y)$. The target and primitive class both replay. For a separate check of the subgroup method, $t=X$ and $s=Y$ give a literal ambient rose for $\langle J_t,s\rangle$ at this same AK(2) marking.
