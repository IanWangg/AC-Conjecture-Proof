# Restrictions on five ordinary AK(3) conjugations

2026-11-19. Exactly **396 of 1,024** signed schedules survive the necessary tests for a five-conjugation ordinary trivialization. Every surviving schedule needs multiplication in at least three of the four intervening gaps, including the last. The tests allow Nielsen intervals of arbitrary length. [Exact census and replay](../results/round21_ac_disproof_five_conjugations.json), [prior lower bound](round-20-ac-disproof.md).

## The marked prefix

Let $H=\langle p,b\rangle$ be the literal AK(3) relator subgroup. Its two generators are an intrinsic free basis because their ambient exponent matrix has determinant of absolute value one. Before the first conjugation, the unchanged row $u$ and the receiving row $v$ form another basis of $H$. Their two homology vectors in the marked $(p,b)$ basis are a basis modulo two.

Write $a,c,d,e$ for the first four signed conjugating letters. After the first conjugation, both actual rows belong to $\langle u,v^a\rangle$, where $w^g=gwg^{-1}$. Inductively, immediately before the fifth conjugation both rows belong to the group generated by $u^g$ and $v^{ga}$ for every ordered subproduct $g$ of the letters $e,d,c$, including the empty product. This follows by retaining each transported copy and its previous untransported copy at each conjugation. The containing group $K$ used for calculation replaces the two initial rows by the whole of $H$ in each copy. The homology of each actual row in $H_1(K;\mathbb F_2)$ still lies in the span of the separately marked conjugates of $u$ and $v$. Thus six ordered choices of distinct nonzero coefficient vectors cover the allowed spans, rather than allowing every coefficient in each copy independently.

An ambient primitive belonging to $K$ has a cyclic representative which can be read as a loop at some vertex of the Stallings core of $K$. A path to that vertex and the return path contribute zero combined homology voltage. The checker tracks partial vertex maps decorated with the sums of chord voltages modulo two and with the ambient parity of the word. The two standard positive basis substitutions give a finite saturation on each of four signed alphabets, as in the rank-two primitive census. This yields every possible pair of homology voltage and ambient slope parity of an ambient primitive in $K$. A unimodular pair of slopes must give two different nonzero ambient parity vectors in the same permitted source span. [Rank-two primitive census](round-20-ac-disproof.md#all-relevant-primitive-classes-in-the-prefix-cores).

The ordinary final group must be the rose. Before the last conjugation both actual rows must already be primitive, since the pair just after it is a basis, and conjugation preserves primitivity and ambient slope. The first two rows of the table enforce these facts using unmarked envelopes. In the proper prefix cases the two directional completing-word tests are necessary, since enlarging either the stationary row or the conjugated row to its whole containing core still gives the rose. The integral incidence from Round 20 handles $xy$ and $yx$ followed by two conjugations along the first generator, since both later conjugators lie in the same marked subgroup $K$ used there. The new binary test handles the other proper prefix cases passing the directional tests.

For each of the four prefixes starting with `Xy` and followed by two letters from `x,X`, none of its six permitted source spans contains the voltage of even one ambient primitive. Either final `y` or final `Y` is therefore excluded. [Source-span and signed-map receipts](../results/round21_ac_disproof_five_conjugations.json).

## Intervening multiplication

After at most three single-generator conjugations with arbitrary intervening multiplications and inversions, the two actual rows cannot both be primitive with ambient determinant of absolute value one. For three conjugations, the first envelope census leaves eight primitive-admitting prefixes. The homogeneous-prefix character argument rules out every primitive in four of them. For the other four, the integral incidence argument rules out a primitive pair with unimodular slopes. Conjugation and inversion alone preserve each row's primitivity and the absolute determinant. Hence a path with five conjugations requires a multiplication between the fourth and fifth. [Primitive-admitting envelopes](round-06-stable-disproof.md#three-necessary-original-letter-conjugations-in-the-free-group), [homogeneous-prefix exclusion](round-19-ac-disproof.md#equal-first-letters), [integral incidence](round-20-ac-disproof.md#keeping-both-source-rows).

A binary mask records which of the four gaps allow multiplications. Each group of consecutive conjugations separated only by inversions has one exact accumulated conjugator per row. Enumerating the row receiving each letter determines both conjugators. A reduction of the total conjugator length would give a path with fewer than five single-generator conjugations, excluded in Round 20. At a permitted gap, the folded subgroup generated by the two transported copies is a necessary containing group. At the last block, the directional completing-word tests use the relative word between the two transports. The receipt lists all signed schedules and allowed masks that pass these necessary tests. [Product-free blocks and last-row test](round-19-ac-disproof.md#products-between-conjugations).
