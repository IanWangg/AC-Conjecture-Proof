# Proper-overgroup tests at both energy-20 markings

2026-11-19. The row subgroup of each new energy-20 AK(3) marking has primitive-free normal closure in every proper overgroup in \(F(x,y)\). The first marking has two exceptional principal images. An affine quotient settles the rank-three case in which all abelian characters fail, and three integer characters settle the rank-four cover. A separate exact replay identifies the first full ambient subgroup on a 687-move route after coalescing conjugations between products. ## Complete principal-fringe classifications

Write \(H\) for the subgroup generated by a marked pair. The proper-overgroup theorem from Round 10 reduces the primitive-in-normal-closure problem to all proper principal images of the based core of \(H\). For each image reached by identifications that increase rank by one, the two source rows form part of a free basis. In this basis the quotient killing the source rows is a free group on the other generators. The replay constructs an inverse marking, checks the row images, and saturates the free-word identity relation in each of the eight signed double-free cyclic automata. A primitive loop must occur in at least one of these languages. [Proper-overgroup reduction and exact quotient test](round-10-ac-disproof.md).

## The six-vertex affine quotient

In the first exceptional image a spanning-tree chord basis is \(a,b,c\), with ambient words `yX, xxy, YxxYXXXy`. The source rows read \(bc^{-1}\) and \(b^2ab^{-1}a^{-1}\). Their quotient therefore has \(b=c\) and \(aba^{-1}=b^2\). Use the homomorphism to the dyadic affine group given by \(a(t)=2t\) and \(b(t)=c(t)=t+1\).

Every cyclic strongly connected component in each of the eight automata consists of a single oriented cycle. A nonempty closed path runs around one such cycle one or more times. The replayer uses the actual chord words on the cycle to compute its affine map \(t\mapsto 2^k t+q\), with \(k\in\mathbb Z\) and \(q\) dyadic rational. Across all eight automata there are twelve cyclic components. Each of their maps is nonidentity. If \(k\ne0\), every positive power has dilation different from one. If \(k=0\), a nonidentity affine map translates by \(q\ne0\) and its positive powers translate by nonzero integer multiples of \(q\). Either way there is no nonempty cyclic path in the kernel. A change of the starting vertex around the cycle conjugates its image and preserves this conclusion.

The abelian relation kills \(b\) and \(c\), so every integer character killing the source rows vanishes on the cyclic labels `xxy` and `xy` in their indicated components. These are ambient primitives. Their chord values are respectively \(b\) and \(c^{-1}\), with affine translations \(1\) and \(-1\). Thus even assigning different abelian characters to different signed languages would leave these two primitives, while the affine quotient excludes them from the actual normal closure. [All cyclic components and affine images](../results/round12_ac_disproof_energy20.json).

## The index-three cover

The rank-four exception has chord basis `XX, yX, xyy, Yxy`. The characters \((-1,0,0,1)\), \((-2,-2,-2,1)\), and \((-1,-2,-2,0)\) each kill both source rows. The first character handles the same-sign languages when the \(y\)-letter double is forbidden, the second handles all opposite-sign languages, and the third handles the same-sign languages when the \(x\)-letter double is forbidden. The independent reachability verifier checks the cyclic components and 72 integer potential inequalities. This completes primitive exclusion in all proper overgroups of both pairs. ## The first full relative-conjugator subgroup on a strict route

Between successive product blocks, coalesce the conjugations of each row into ordinary words \(g_0,g_1\). The row inversions commute with conjugation and can be moved to the beginning of that block. With the running common conjugation \(C\) on the input tuple, replace it by \(C'=C g_0^{-1}\) at the block end. The first row has then undergone only its prescribed inversions, while the second undergoes its inversions and conjugation by \(t=Cg_0^{-1}g_1C^{-1}\). At product steps retain the common marking, since multiplication commutes with simultaneous conjugation. This gives an ordinary path by expanding each \(t\) into its single-letter conjugations. The group cut uses the **whole words \(t\) at block boundaries**.

The cumulative overgroup is unchanged by anchoring the second row instead. In one block this replaces \(t\) by \(t^{-1}\). The resulting gauges differ by multiplication on the left by \(t^{-1}\), inside the updated cumulative group. At later blocks this difference changes relative conjugators by conjugation inside that group. The cut is also independent of the choice of \(g_i\) taking a signed row between these endpoints. Each row has primitive abelianization because the relator exponent matrix remains unimodular, so it is no proper power. Its centralizer is its cyclic subgroup. Thus replacing \(g_i\) by another valid word right-multiplies it by a power of the input row. The new relative word is in the same double coset of the preceding cumulative group. The gauge changes by multiplication on the left by an element of that group. The same argument applies inductively along the specified blocks.

Identifying vertices 7 and 0 folds directly to the ambient rose. The gauged prefix replays in 53 strict operations. Both possible row anchors give the same three subgroup graphs at the same block boundaries. For the adjacent AK(2) control, the cut occurs in the block ending at original instruction 17, and the whole verified path reaches the positive basis in 46 instructions. These cuts identify where the proper-overgroup theorem ceases to constrain the specified paths.
