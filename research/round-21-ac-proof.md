# Degree-six restrictions on even exponents in the width-two Wirtinger branch

2026-11-19. In the aligned width-two Wirtinger repair, a degree-six Magnus test in characteristic two restricts the outer block with donor $C$ at both even residues $2,6\pmod 8$. It checks **4,096** class-three cases, leaves two of the eight second-degree choices for each residue, and imposes one binary linear relation on the third-degree coordinates in each surviving class. The restriction applies for arbitrary free-word length. [Earlier exponent bounds](round-20-ac-proof.md), [exact finite receipt](../results/round21_ac_proof_wirtinger_even.json).

## Integral normalization

Use $b^{-1}$ as stable letter in the free-by-cyclic $C$ quotient, with free basis $u,v,w$, shift $\Psi$, and $A=A_0b^{-1}$. The normalized intermediate is $h=V^\alpha b^{-1}$. The Laurent formula in Round 19 determines the abelianization of $V$ before the deletion quotient, up to sign and a shift. Inverting $V$ and negating $\alpha$ if needed leaves its power unchanged. Conjugating by a power of the stable letter then makes the abelianized kernel vector $(T-I)w=(-1,1,1)$, where $T$ is the linear part of $\Psi$. Hence in the free kernel write $V=u^{-1}vw V'$ with $V'\in\gamma_2$. This uses the **integral** coordinate and fixes the generator powers, including their higher Magnus coefficients. The necessary outer equation remains

$$
k V^\alpha=A_0\Psi(k).
$$

[Outer fibre and Laurent constraint](round-19-ac-proof.md), [first use of this normalization in characteristic three](round-20-ac-proof.md#third-powers-in-canonical-coordinates).

## Finite degree-six test

Write $V=1+n$ and $k=1+k_1+\cdots+k_6$ in the truncated noncommutative Magnus algebra over $\mathbb F_2$. The group of series with constant coefficient one has exponent dividing eight. For residues $r=2,6\pmod 8$ the power $P_r$ has the following form through degree six:

$$
P_2=1+n^2,\qquad P_6=1+n^2+n^4+n^6.
$$

In both cases $P_r$ has zero degree-one coefficient, as does $A_0$. The degree-one equation forces $k_1=0$ since $I-T$ is invertible. The degree-two equation determines $k_2$ by the invertible operator $I-T^{\otimes2}$, and its right side depends only on $n_1$. Once $k_2$ is computed, put $k_*=1+k_2$. The remaining coefficients of $k$ are allowed to be arbitrary associative coefficients. With $n_1,n_2$ fixed, their linear columns in degrees three through six are

$$
A_0\Psi(m)-m(P_r)_{\le 3},\qquad 3\le\deg m\le 6.
$$

The power through degree three depends only on $n_1,n_2$, so these columns are shared by every choice in degree three and above of $V$. The exact checker has rank **972** for the conjugator columns. The calculation is over the associative algebra, which enlarges the set of possible conjugators.

For $V$ itself we retain the free-group constraints. Use the three basic commutators $[u,v],[u,w],[v,w]$ in that order after $u^{-1}vw$. Their exponents modulo two give every degree-two choice. The degree-three variation is generated over $\mathbb F_2$ by commutators of weight three, obtained by bracketing the basic commutators with $u,v,w$. Their degree-three components span an eight-dimensional space. Each of its 256 binary combinations is checked for each of the eight degree-two choices. Choosing a representative with these coefficients fixes $n_1,n_2,n_3$.

All remaining changes to $n$ through degree five are generated by appending the squares of the basic weight-two commutators and the commutators of weights four and five. To see this, use lower central collection in order of increasing weight. Adjusting a weight-two exponent by two changes the expansion first in degree four. Its square commutes through other weight-two factors modulo degree six and can be moved to the end. The weight-three terms with a given leading component differ, after rearrangement, by weight-four and weight-five terms and invisible even powers of weight-three terms. In weight three the generating brackets have the antisymmetry and Jacobi relations of the free Lie ring. In rank three the three brackets on three distinct generators have the single Jacobi relation, while the six brackets using just two distinct generators are independent. Thus a vanishing characteristic-two component can be eliminated through even powers and this integral relation. A change of weight at least six cannot affect either power $P_2$ or $P_6$ through degree six. Appended changes are additive in degrees four and five, since every product of two starts at degree eight. Multiplying by the base series before appending only requires its degree-one part. Thus the possible changes $\delta$ to $n$ through degree five lie in the span computed from the indicated commutators, with the exact degree-one prefix.

For such $\delta$, the change to $P_r$ through degree six is

$$
(n_1+n_2)\delta+\delta(n_1+n_2).
$$

Every term in the change to $n^4$ has degree at least seven, and the same holds for $n^6$. The extra columns in the outer equation are obtained by multiplying this displayed variation by $k_*$. The combined column rank is **988**. Of the 4,096 residuals of $A_0\Psi(k_*)-k_*P_r$, exactly 512 lie in that span. For each residue, the only admissible second-degree choices are $(1,0,1)$ and $(1,1,1)$ in the ordered basis above. In each of these four cases, the same binary equation $d_3+d_4+d_6+d_7=0$ holds in the ordered weight-three basis recorded in the receipt. Its 128 solutions pass this necessary condition. The absolute exponent menu remains $1,2,3,6$. The replayer also accepts a manufactured instance with an actual weight-four correction and a nontrivial free conjugator, constructed by setting the target equal to $kV^6\Psi(k)^{-1}$. ## A common formulation of the remaining middle condition

For a survivor write $X=x_0^{-1}$, $Z=y_0$, and $S=Tb^{-1}$ in the abstract intersection basis, with $T$ here the exceptional generator of the intersection. In that basis let $G(t)=s t^j$ be the Laurent coordinate of the middle conjugator $\eta$. Left multiply both $X,Z$ by $b^j$ and substitute $S\mapsto b^{-j}S b^j$ in $\eta$. This keeps the two free images of $\eta$ unchanged and sends $G$ to the constant $s$. Right multiply each of the new $X,Z$ by the power of $b$ that sets its total exponent to zero. This keeps both images of $S$ unchanged, gives $V=X^{-1}Z$, and has conjugated the original $V$ by a power of $b$. In this simultaneous normalization, its integral abelianization in the $C$ fibre is $s(-1,1,1)$, so here keep the sign $s$ rather than inverting $V$ separately.

Set $f(W)=\eta(b,[W,b])$. The two middle representatives are $g_L=f(X)$ and $g_R=f(XV)$. The exact free-word middle equation gives $g_L C=B g_R$, since $Qx^{-1}=C$, $Px^{-1}=B$, and $x^{-1}g_R x=\sigma(g_R)$. It follows that any remaining exponent needs the same two values in the following necessary outer equations:

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
F/N_C\colon & f(X)f(XV)^{-1}=B,\\
F/N_B\colon & f(X)^{-1}f(XV)=C.
\end{array}
$$

In each equation the two evaluations of $\eta$ share the same correction in the derived subgroup of the kernel of $\exp_b$ on $F(b,S)$. This formulation can now be applied to both odd exponents and to the restricted even-coordinate cases. The cyclic-power branch is separate from this degree-six test.
