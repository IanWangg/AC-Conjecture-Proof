## What the results establish together

- Any ordinary AC path reaching a primitive AK(3) relator has cyclic peak at least **29**. The [full independent cap-27 audit](round-05-cyclic27.md) checks 37,055,512,096 candidate products. The [compact cap-28 audit](round-05-ak3.md) independently checks all 8,772,461,272 orbit-level products, and every signed ambient orbit is represented by an ordinary path. The new energy-16 escape representatives remain two-phase excluded over the tested bridge families.
- The rank-three waypoint with a non-obvious primitive has two projections of energy 22. The literal $x$ chart stays in the cap-27 component, while the intrinsic chart lies outside cap 28. Eight defining exchanges prove stable reachability of the latter with maximum rank at most four. The expanded strict continuation was not counted. This separates bounded ordinary reachability from stable reachability and provides a new source for constructive searches. [Rank-four transport](round-05-stable-chart-transport.md).
- The proof-track criterion reduces a common-lift problem to Nielsen equivalence in a single joint quotient. The forced donor example shows that a one-donor correction cannot suffice for every chart switch. Alongside the first rank-three state with no primitive row, this identifies concrete cases that an unrestricted stable-to-ordinary projection would have to handle. [Moving-chart proof and controls](round-05-ac-proof.md).
- The surviving restrictions on the third phase depend both on the first source and on the unread middle residual. Beginning instead with every zero-gap envelope for an arbitrary initial Nielsen prelude can create a primitive in the enlarged graph. This makes the intrinsic-basis conditions used in the constructive track relevant to improving the disproof track's scope. [Third-phase scans and countercontrol](round-05-ac-disproof.md).
- Once AK(3) reaches the marked basis modulo the braid relation module, ordinary equivalence survives all finite derived-series layers of the braid kernel and all simultaneous finite markings. The lifting theorem uses finite normal chief factors in any finite rank and a solvable-kernel argument at rank two. The first solvable correction can itself be made in the free group. After that correction the frozen first row defines a one-relator quotient with nontrivial perfect derived subgroup, explaining why the remaining one-sided correction requires unbounded length across the derived layers. [Kernel lifting and free-group obstruction](round-05-finite-kernel.md).
- In the degree-seven stable quotient, the exact distance 14 holds for both ordered and unordered bases. The small projected exclusions also give a practical way to screen positive padding in the next degree before searching the full higher-rank quotients. [Padding retractions](round-05-stable-disproof.md).
