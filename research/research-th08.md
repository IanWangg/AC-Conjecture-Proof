- **Wording (S2), README closing summary: a sentence that is not what H proves.** The summary lists, among the places where an obstruction cannot live, "no quotient with an abelian normal subgroup inside its derived subgroup or a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup". *Exact failing step:* H's Theorem 2 is an equivalence — Q obstructs U iff Q/(N ∩ [Q,Q]) (resp. Q/N) does — and yields "Q does not obstruct" only when the smaller quotient is already known not to obstruct (as in Corollary 3, where the descent ends at Q_ab). What follows in general is Theorem 4: a *just-obstructing* quotient has no such subgroup. The README sentence is strictly stronger. Indeed, for a fixed U, the statement (*) "no quotient of F with a nontrivial abelian normal subgroup inside its derived subgroup obstructs U" is **equivalent to "no proper quotient of F obstructs U"**: if F/K obstructs with K ≠ 1, put K₁ = K ∩ [F,F] ≠ 1 (a nontrivial normal subgroup of F₂ meets [F,F]: [k,g] ∈ K ∩ [F,F]); then Q = F/[K₁,K₁] maps onto F/K, hence obstructs U, and K₁/[K₁,K₁] ≠ 1 is an abelian normal subgroup of Q inside [Q,Q] = [F,F]/[K₁,K₁]; conversely F itself has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup. Nothing in the repository proves that no proper quotient obstructs AK(3) (that would say: if AK(3) is not AC-trivial, then F is its own just-obstructing quotient). (*) cannot be refuted either — it holds vacuously if AK(3) is AC-trivial — so this is an unproved sentence, not a false theorem; no theorem of H is affected. Suggested wording: "an obstruction, if one exists, descends through abelian normal subgroups inside the derived subgroup and through nonabelian minimal normal subgroups; a minimal (just-obstructing) quotient has neither". The README's own later paragraph on H is worded correctly.
- **Wording (S3), H §7.1.** "A quotient-based proof … must use a group with no minimal normal subgroups" is slightly stronger than Theorem 4(c),(d): a just-obstructing Q may still have central minimal normal subgroups of prime order meeting [Q,Q] trivially (the "abelian fibre product over an infinite base" case that H §6 itself lists as open). *Update:* §3.2 below proves that this case does not occur (Theorem TH08-C, Corollary C2), so the sentence is true after all; it just does not follow from H's Theorem 4 as proved.

## 3. Results that fell out of the audit (proved here; short; they strengthen H's Theorem 2(i) and need a second reader; novelty not established)

Notation as in H: π: F → Q onto, (a,b) = (π(x),π(y)), ρ: ℤ² = F_ab → Q_ab the induced surjection; "Q obstructs U" means π(U) ≁_Q (a,b). A pair P = (s,t) of Q is *admissible* if (ab s, ab t) = (ρ(v₀), ρ(v₁)) for a basis (v₀,v₁) of ℤ² (H's condition "abelianization Nielsen-equivalent to that of (a,b)"). Every pair AC-equivalent in Q to π(U) is admissible, because AC moves act on abelianized pairs through GL₂(ℤ) and (e(u₀), e(u₁)) is a basis. A *class pair* of U is any (u₀,u₁) ~AC U in F; V_i := ⟨⟨u_i⟩⟩_F, G_i := F/V_i.

### 3.1 The core lemma

**Lemma TH08-A (abelian-cokernel coordinate).** Let P = (s,t) be an admissible pair of Q. If Q/⟨⟨t⟩⟩ is abelian, then P ~_Q (a,b). Hence, if Q obstructs U, then for **every** pair (s,t) in the Q-class of π(U) both Q/⟨⟨s⟩⟩ and Q/⟨⟨t⟩⟩ are nonabelian.
*Proof.* Let (v₀,v₁) be as in the definition and let (p,q) be a basis of F with e(p) = v₀, e(q) = v₁ (Nielsen). (B) ab π(p) = ρ(v₀) = ab s, so s⁻¹π(p) ∈ [Q,Q] ⊆ ⟨⟨t⟩⟩; multiplying coordinate 0 by this element gives (s,t) ~ (π(p), t). (C) Q is generated by π(p), π(q), so Q/⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ is cyclic and [Q,Q] ⊆ ⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩; ab π(q) = ρ(v₁) = ab t, so t⁻¹π(q) ∈ ⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ and (π(p),t) ~ (π(p),π(q)). (D) (π(p),π(q)) is the image of a basis of F, Nielsen-equivalent to (a,b). ∎
Special cases: H's Theorem 1 (Q/⟨⟨π(c)⟩⟩ = 1); a coordinate equal to π(p) with p primitive (Q/⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ cyclic) — the quotient-level form of D's Lemma D3; H's Lemma 2.2.

### 3.2 Lifting through every abelian normal subgroup

**Theorem TH08-C (H's Theorem 2(i) without the restriction to [Q,Q]).** Let N ⊴ Q be abelian. An admissible pair P of Q is standardizable iff its image in Q/N is. In particular **Q obstructs U iff Q/N does**.
*Proof.* "Only if" is clear. *Central case.* Let N ≤ Z(Q) and let the image of P in Q/N be standardizable. Lifting the path (conjugators lift), P ~_Q (aν₀, bν₁) with ν₀, ν₁ ∈ N. In Q/⟨⟨bν₁⟩⟩ the image of b equals that of ν₁⁻¹, which is central; the quotient is generated by the image of a and a central element, so it is abelian. Lemma TH08-A applies to the admissible pair (aν₀, bν₁). *General case.* Put Q₁ = Q/[N,Q]; N/[N,Q] is central in Q₁ and Q₁_ab = Q_ab, so by the central case the image of P is standardizable in Q₁. Lifting that path to Q gives P ~ (an₀, bn₁) with n₀, n₁ ∈ [N,Q], and H's Lemma 2.1 removes them. ∎
(H's proof needed n_i ∈ [Q,Q] because Lemma 2.2 returns to (a,b) itself; returning to the image of another basis of F, as in H's own Theorem 1, removes the restriction.)

**Corollary C1.** (i) For every solvable normal subgroup S ⊴ Q: Q obstructs U iff Q/S does (derived series of S; each term is characteristic in S). (ii) For every finite normal subgroup M ⊴ Q, and more generally every normal M with a solvable Q-normal subgroup of finite index: Q obstructs U iff Q/M does (chief series as in H's Corollary 3; abelian factors by Theorem TH08-C, nonabelian ones by H's corrected Theorem 2(ii)). (iii) If R is the union of all solvable normal subgroups of Q (a directed union), Q obstructs U iff Q/R does (a trivializing path in Q/R has two discrepancies, which lie in one solvable normal subgroup).

**Corollary C2 (structure of just-obstructing quotients; sharpens H's Theorem 4(b)–(d)).** If Q is just-obstructing for U, then Q has **no nontrivial abelian, solvable or finite normal subgroup**; in particular Z(Q) = 1 and Q has **no minimal normal subgroup at all** (a minimal normal subgroup is abelian or nonabelian-minimal). So the sentence of H §7.1 that I flagged in F8 is in fact true, by this corollary rather than by H's Theorem 4.

**Corollary C3 (fibre products; closes the case H §6 lists as open).** Let 𝒦(U) := {K ⊴ F : F/K does not obstruct U}. If K ∈ 𝒦(U) and F/L is virtually solvable, then K ∩ L ∈ 𝒦(U). In particular, for **every** quotient H = F/K, the abelian fibre product H ×_{H_ab} ℤ² = F/(K ∩ [F,F]) obstructs U iff H does; moreover (H's Prop. 5.1 formalism) the linear AC-monodromy at the standard pair is the full congruence group, Λ_H = Γ_H, for every H, finite or not.
*Proof.* Q = F/(K∩L), M = K/(K∩L) ≅ KL/L is a normal subgroup of the kind in C1(ii), and Q/M = F/K. For L = [F,F], M is central and the argument is just Lemma TH08-A: coordinate 1 = (b; r₁) has abelian cokernel. Λ_H = Γ_H: for γ ∈ Γ_H the pair ((π_H q', π_H p'); I), (q',p') a basis of F with exponent matrix γ, is admissible in Q and standardizable in H, hence in Q, by a path of monodromy I; composing with the Nielsen map gives a loop at (a,b) of monodromy γ. ∎
So H's Theorem 5 needs no finiteness, and the test cases proposed in H §7.2 (H = SL₂(ℤ), B₃, triangle groups, PSL₂(ℤ[1/2]) with a ℤ² fibre) carry no information beyond the base H itself. This concerns one pair against the standard pair; N's two-pair criterion N2.6 is not affected.

### 3.3 The product of the two one-relator groups

**Theorem TH08-P1.** Let (u₀,u₁) be a class pair of U. Then F/(V₀ ∩ V₁) ≅ G₀ × G₁ does not obstruct U. Consequently (Corollary C1) no quotient Q of F in which [⟨⟨π(u₀)⟩⟩, ⟨⟨π(u₁)⟩⟩] is solvable — in particular none in which the two relator normal closures commute — obstructs U.
*Proof.* F = V₀V₁ gives F/(V₀∩V₁) ≅ G₀ × G₁ via w ↦ (wV₀, wV₁). Write A = G₀, B = G₁, k' = u₁V₀ ∈ A (a normal generator of A), k = u₀V₁ ∈ B (a normal generator of B); then π(u₀) = (1,k), π(u₁) = (k',1). B_ab = ℤ²/⟨e(u₁)⟩ ≅ ℤ is generated by k̄. Choose a primitive p ∈ F with (e(p), e(u₁)) a basis of ℤ²; its image b_p ∈ B has b̄_p = j·k̄ with j = ±1; let a_p be its image in A.
Step 1: ⟨⟨(k',1)⟩⟩ = A × 1, so coordinate 0 can be multiplied to (α,k) with α := (k'⁻¹a_p)^j.
Step 2: ⟨⟨(α,k)⟩⟩ contains (α,k)^j and the commutators [(α,k),(1,h)] = (1,[k,h]), hence 1 × [⟨⟨k⟩⟩,B] = 1 × [B,B]. Multiply coordinate 1 by (α,k)^j·(1,d), d := k^{−j}b_p ∈ [B,B]: it becomes (k'α^j, k^j d) = (a_p, b_p) = π(p).
Step 3: Q/⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ is cyclic; Lemma TH08-A.
For the consequence: M := [⟨⟨π(u₀)⟩⟩,⟨⟨π(u₁)⟩⟩] is normal and solvable, so Q obstructs iff Q/M does (C1(i)); in Q/M the closures commute, so their intersection is central, and modulo it Q/M is a quotient of G₀ × G₁ (C1(i) again). ∎

*Example (AK(3)).* u₀ = x³y⁻⁴, u₁ = b = xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹, p = y, j = −1, α = y⁻¹b ∈ T(3,4): after step 1 the pair is ((y⁻¹b, x³y⁻⁴), (b,1)); multiplying coordinate 1 by coordinate 0⁻¹ and by (1, x³y⁻³) gives (y,y) = π(y); coordinate 0 has abelianization e(x) − 2e(y) and is moved to π(xy⁻²). So **AK(3) is AC-trivial modulo ⟨⟨x³y⁻⁴⟩⟩ ∩ ⟨⟨xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹⟩⟩**, and neither T(3,4) × B₃ nor (C₃ * C₄) × PSL₂(ℤ) obstructs it. `experiments/th08_product_quotient.py` replays the three steps with explicit elements in the finite image S₄ × SL(2,5) (order 2880), checking every normal-closure membership by brute force (a sanity check of the steps, not a proof). Not covered: G_c × G_{c'} for coordinates of two *different* class pairs.

### 3.4 A remark on the two-pair problem

**Lemma TH08-S (where a central two-pair obstruction of N §2.3 can live).** Let N ≤ Z(Q) ∩ [Q,Q], let (s,t) normally generate Q, n₀, n₁ ∈ N, Q̄ = Q/N. If H₂(Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩; ℤ) = 0 then N ⊆ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩, so (sn₀, tn₁) ~ (s, tn₁); if also H₂(Q̄/⟨⟨s̄⟩⟩; ℤ) = 0 then (sn₀, tn₁) ~ (s,t).
*Proof.* A' := Q/⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩ is a central extension of A := Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩ with kernel N₁ = N/(N ∩ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩), and N₁ ⊆ [A',A'] because N ⊆ [Q,Q]. In the five-term sequence H₂(A) → N₁ → H₁(A') → H₁(A) → 0 the map N₁ → H₁(A') is zero, so H₂(A) → N₁ is onto; H₂(A) = 0 gives N ⊆ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩. Symmetrically for s. ∎
H's Lemma 2.2 is the case where both quotients are cyclic. Theorem TH08-C does **not** extend to two merely normally generating pairs (its proof uses that the target pair generates Q); for two pairs, a central obstruction must be carried by the Schur multiplier of one of the one-normal-generator groups Q̄/⟨⟨s̄⟩⟩, Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩ (for pairs coming from F these are quotients of the one-relator groups G₀, G₁, whose own H₂ vanishes since their relators are not in [F,F]).

## 6. Synthesis (one page)

**Where the project stands, in one sentence.** Every result is either an exact statement about *one* generalized move (G2, O6), a certified equivalence that changes coordinates (C, K1, G1, Lemma S, the Aut-realizations), or a no-go theorem for a class of quotients (B, E, H, N1, and §3 above); nothing separates AK(3) from (x,y), and nothing connects them.

**Lines that are closed (do not reopen).**
1. *Quotient obstructions with any "small" normal structure.* Finite, solvable, virtually solvable, simple quotients; quotients where a class coordinate normally generates (B, E, H). With §3: an obstruction always descends modulo **every** abelian, solvable or finite normal subgroup and modulo nonabelian minimal normal subgroups, so abelian fibre products H ×_{H_ab} ℤ² are useless over every base (not only finite ones), and G₀ × G₁ is useless for every class pair. What an obstructing quotient Q must look like after descent (just-obstructing): infinite, not simple, **no nontrivial solvable or finite normal subgroup, trivial centre, no minimal normal subgroup**; for every pair (s,t) in the Q-class of π(U) the groups Q/⟨⟨s⟩⟩ and Q/⟨⟨t⟩⟩ are **nonabelian**; for every class pair the mutual commutator [⟨⟨π(u₀)⟩⟩,⟨⟨π(u₁)⟩⟩] is not virtually solvable; if Q is just-infinite it is residually finite with unbounded AC distances over its finite quotients (H 4(e)).
2. Chirality invariants attached to the presentation complex (N1); monotone quantities on reversal-closed move sets (K2); the correction-cost program (K3).
3. One-move primitive exits: from AK(n), n ≥ 3, with either relator frozen (G2); from P_n, n = 3,…,8, with c frozen (O6). Defect-2 torus states are trivializations for all coprime torus types (G §2.4(b); F5), but none has been seen.
4. Further directed brute force (M: cap 29 done, cap 30 not affordable).

**Three theorem-level targets, ranked by expected yield.**

*T1. The synchronization problem: is 𝒦(U) = {K ⊴ F : F/K does not obstruct U} closed under finite intersections?* Under the AC conjecture it is (𝒦 is everything), so each instance is a provable-or-refutable weakening, and a single failure would be an obstruction, i.e. a counterexample for that U. Known instances: K ∩ L with F/L virtually solvable (Corollary C3, which contains H's Theorem 5), and V₀ ∩ V₁ for one class pair (P1). All of these go through one mechanism: produce a coordinate with abelian cokernel (Lemma TH08-A). **Missing:** any way to synchronize two trivializations when no such coordinate is visible. The general criterion available now is: K ∈ 𝒦(U) as soon as some class pair has a coordinate t with F/(⟨⟨t⟩⟩K) abelian. First instances that this does **not** decide, with exact tools already in the repository: the images of F in T(3,4) × ⟨x,y | k₃⟩ and in B₃ × G_{c_K} (coordinates of AK(3) and of P₃ without a visible common partner; torus normal forms from G, twisted-conjugacy normal forms from O), and more generally products or fibre products of two non-obstructing quotients that are both infinite and radical-free (if one factor is finite or solvable the instance is closed by C1). A general positive theorem would say that obstructions are "prime"; a single negative instance would be a disproof of the conjecture for that U.

*T2. Exact move theorems in the P_n coordinates (constructive side).* (a) O6 for all n ≥ 3 (TH03): **missing** an invariant uniform in n — the candidate is the exact holonomy of the figure-eight subgroup H in PSL₂(ℤ[ω]): tr ρ(k_n²) and tr ρ((xˢy)²) satisfy linear recurrences in n and s, equality of translation lengths bounds |s| − n, and the finitely many remaining patterns are killed by O's sieve; finite quotients alone cannot do it (k_{n+N} ≡ k_n). (b) The k_n-frozen half (O's Q2) — **missing** any separating quotient of ⟨x,y | k₃⟩ ≅ F₃ ⋊ ℤ; until then "no one-move primitive exit from P₃" is only half proved (F2). (c) Rigidity of AK(3)'s torus island (TH04): **missing** a Lemma-G4-type finite reduction for torus-type (not only primitive) neighbours, i.e. a lower bound on the syllable length of θ_M(x^{p'}y^{q'}) in C_p * C_q in terms of |M|. (d) The first two-move statement: enumerate the c-frozen neighbours k' ∈ R(a³BA) whose one-relator group has decidable conjugacy and decide the second move exactly; a hit is a trivialization of AK(3) by certificate, a complete negative is out of reach (R(a³BA) is infinite). None of (a)–(c) bears on AC-triviality; their value is exact machinery and an honest closure of F2.

*T3. The stabilizer Ŝ(C4) (counterexample side).* The dichotomy "exactly D₆ or infinite" is proved. **Missing for "infinite":** one certified element of infinite order — a finite computation (N's orbit-coincidence method has not been run on M's cap-34 forward set; it costs minutes) or a uniform Lemma-S-type construction for T(a;p,q) (TH02) explaining why a = 2 (X = T(2;4,9)) has an infinite stabilizer. **Missing for "exactly D₆" (which would disprove the conjecture):** any candidate invariant at all. By N1 it is not topological; a separating quotient obstructs C4 or its image, so it has all the properties in closed line 1; Theorem TH08-C does not extend to two merely normally generating pairs, and by Lemma TH08-S a central two-pair obstruction must be carried by the Schur multiplier of a one-normal-generator quotient. The two-pair abelian lifting problem (TH01) has two distinct parts (F4): (α) non-central corrections in [N,Q] ∖ (D₀ × D₁), (β) the central step. I would not assign more than one agent to the "exactly D₆" side until a candidate invariant exists.

**What is missing for the main problem, stated plainly.** For non-triviality: an AC-invariant of pairs that survives closed line 1; the only quotients left are infinite and radical-free (no solvable or finite normal subgroups, no minimal normal subgroups), with every coordinate cokernel Q/⟨⟨s⟩⟩ nonabelian — e.g. suitable lattices or hyperbolic-type quotients (TH05) and infinite Burnside-type quotients (TH10), where nothing is known. For triviality: a path through cyclic length ≥ 15 (J) that leaves the directed cap-29 forward set (M); the exact one-move theorems say that it cannot end with a primitive relator after a single generalized move from AK(3), or from P₃ with c frozen.
