- **Wording (S2), README closing summary: a sentence that is not what H proves.** The summary lists, among the places where an obstruction cannot live, "no quotient with an abelian normal subgroup inside its derived subgroup or a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup". *Exact failing step:* H's Theorem 2 is an equivalence — Q obstructs U iff Q/(N ∩ [Q,Q]) (resp. Q/N) does — and yields "Q does not obstruct" only when the smaller quotient is already known not to obstruct (as in Corollary 3, where the descent ends at Q_ab). What follows in general is Theorem 4: a *just-obstructing* quotient has no such subgroup. The README sentence is strictly stronger. Indeed, for a fixed U, the statement (*) "no quotient of F with a nontrivial abelian normal subgroup inside its derived subgroup obstructs U" is **equivalent to "no proper quotient of F obstructs U"**: if F/K obstructs with K ≠ 1, put K₁ = K ∩ [F,F] ≠ 1 (a nontrivial normal subgroup of F₂ meets [F,F]: [k,g] ∈ K ∩ [F,F]); then Q = F/[K₁,K₁] maps onto F/K, hence obstructs U, and K₁/[K₁,K₁] ≠ 1 is an abelian normal subgroup of Q inside [Q,Q] = [F,F]/[K₁,K₁]; conversely F itself has no nontrivial abelian normal subgroup. Nothing in the repository proves that no proper quotient obstructs AK(3) (that would say: if AK(3) is not AC-trivial, then F is its own just-obstructing quotient). (*) cannot be refuted either — it holds vacuously if AK(3) is AC-trivial — so this is an unproved sentence, not a false theorem; no theorem of H is affected. Suggested wording: "an obstruction, if one exists, descends through abelian normal subgroups inside the derived subgroup and through nonabelian minimal normal subgroups; a minimal (just-obstructing) quotient has neither". The README's own later paragraph on H is worded correctly.
- **Wording (S3), H §7.1.** "A quotient-based proof … must use a group with no minimal normal subgroups" is slightly stronger than Theorem 4(c),(d): a just-obstructing Q may still have central minimal normal subgroups of prime order meeting [Q,Q] trivially (the "abelian fibre product over an infinite base" case that H §6 itself lists as open).

## 3. Three small results that fell out of the audit (proved here; short; need a second reader; novelty not established)

Notation as in H: π: F → Q onto, (a,b) = (π(x),π(y)); "Q obstructs U" means π(U) ≁_Q (a,b). A *class pair* of U is any pair (u₀,u₁) ~AC U in F; V_i := ⟨⟨u_i⟩⟩_F, G_i := F/V_i (the two one-relator groups of the pair).

**Lemma TH08-L (primitive-image coordinate; the quotient-level form of D's Lemma D3).** If some pair AC-equivalent in Q to π(U) has a coordinate equal to π(p) with p primitive in F, then Q does not obstruct U.
*Proof.* Let the pair be (s, π(p)) and let (q,p) be a basis of F. AC moves act on abelianized pairs by GL₂(ℤ), so (s̄, π̄(p)) = M·(π̄(q), π̄(p)) in Q_ab² for some M ∈ GL₂(ℤ). Let (q',p') be a basis of F whose exponent vectors are the rows of M applied to (e(q),e(p)) (Nielsen). Then π(q') ≡ s and π(p') ≡ π(p) modulo [Q,Q]. (1) Q/⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ is generated by the image of π(q), hence cyclic, so [Q,Q] ⊆ ⟨⟨π(p)⟩⟩ and s can be multiplied to π(q'). (2) Q/⟨⟨π(q')⟩⟩ is generated by the image of π(p'), so [Q,Q] ⊆ ⟨⟨π(q')⟩⟩ and π(p) can be multiplied to π(p'). (3) (π(q'),π(p')) is the image of a basis, Nielsen-equivalent to (a,b). ∎
(H's Theorem 1 is the case where π(p) is manufactured from a coordinate that normally generates Q.)

**Theorem TH08-P1 (the product of the two one-relator groups never obstructs).** Let (u₀,u₁) be a class pair of U. Then
(a) F/(V₀ ∩ V₁) ≅ G₀ × G₁ does not obstruct U;
(b) F/[V₀,V₁] does not obstruct U; hence **no quotient of F in which the normal closures of the images of u₀ and u₁ commute elementwise obstructs U**;
(c) for K := [V₀,V₁] and every n ≥ 0, F/K⁽ⁿ⁾ (derived series of K) does not obstruct U.
*Proof.* (a) F = V₀V₁ gives F/(V₀∩V₁) ≅ G₀ × G₁ via w ↦ (wV₀, wV₁) (Chinese remainder). Write A = G₀, B = G₁, k' = image of u₁ in A (a normal generator of A), k = image of u₀ in B (a normal generator of B). Then π(u₀) = (1,k), π(u₁) = (k',1). B_ab = ℤ²/⟨e(u₀)⟩ ≅ ℤ is generated by k̄; choose a primitive p ∈ F whose image b_p ∈ B has b̄_p = j·k̄ with j = ±1 (complete e(u₀) to a basis of ℤ² and lift), and let a_p be the image of p in A.
Step 1: ⟨⟨(k',1)⟩⟩ = A × 1, so coordinate 0 can be multiplied to (α, k) with α := (k'⁻¹a_p)^j.
Step 2: ⟨⟨(α,k)⟩⟩ contains (α,k)^j and, being normal, the commutators [(α,k),(1,h)] = (1,[k,h]), hence 1 × [⟨⟨k⟩⟩,B] = 1 × [B,B]. Multiply coordinate 1 by (α,k)^j·(1,d) with d := k^{−j}b_p ∈ [B,B]: it becomes (k'α^j, k^j d) = (a_p, b_p) = π(p).
Step 3: Lemma TH08-L.
(b) [V₀∩V₁, F] ⊆ [V₀,V₁] (write g = v₀v₁) and V₀ ∩ V₁ ⊆ [F,F] (exponent vectors in ℤe(u₀) ∩ ℤe(u₁) = 0), so (V₀∩V₁)/[V₀,V₁] is a central subgroup of F/[V₀,V₁] inside its derived subgroup; H's Theorem 2(i) lifts (a). (Recalled, not used: for a balanced presentation of the trivial group V₀ ∩ V₁ = [V₀,V₁], because π₂ of the contractible presentation complex vanishes.)
(c) K⁽ⁿ⁾/K⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ is an abelian normal subgroup of F/K⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ inside the derived subgroup (K ⊆ [F,F]); apply H's Theorem 2(i) inductively. ∎

*Example (AK(3)).* With u₀ = x³y⁻⁴, u₁ = b = xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹, p = y, j = −1: α = y⁻¹b ∈ T(3,4); after step 1 the pair is ((y⁻¹b, x³y⁻⁴), (b,1)); multiplying coordinate 1 by coordinate 0⁻¹ and by (1, x³y⁻³) gives (y,y) = π(y); coordinate 0 has abelianization e(x) − 2e(y) and is moved to π(xy⁻²). So **AK(3) is AC-trivial modulo [⟨⟨x³y⁻⁴⟩⟩, ⟨⟨xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹⟩⟩]**, and neither T(3,4) × B₃ nor (C₃ * C₄) × PSL₂(ℤ) obstructs it. `experiments/th08_product_quotient.py` replays the three steps with explicit elements in the finite image S₄ × SL(2,5) (order 2880) and checks every normal-closure membership by brute force (a sanity check of the steps, not a proof).

**Corollary TH08-P2 (H's open fibre products over an infinite base, for bases that kill a class coordinate).** Let c be a coordinate of a class pair of U and let H be any quotient of F/⟨⟨c⟩⟩. Then the abelian fibre product H ×_{H_ab} ℤ² = F/(ker(F → H) ∩ [F,F]) does not obstruct U.
*Proof.* ker ∩ [F,F] ⊇ ⟨⟨c⟩⟩ ∩ [F,F] ⊇ ⟨⟨c⟩⟩ ∩ ⟨⟨w⟩⟩ for the partner w of c, so the group is a quotient of G_c × G_w. (Directly: π(c) = (1; e(c)) is central and can be multiplied by ([H,H]; 0) ⊆ ⟨⟨π(w)⟩⟩ to π(p) for a primitive p with e(p) = e(c); then Lemma TH08-L.) ∎
This turns the heuristic of H §7.2 ("one needs H in which neither relator dies") into a theorem and closes the proposed test cases H = B₃, SL₂(ℤ), PSL₂(ℤ) (b is a class coordinate of AK(n)), every quotient of T(3,4), of BS(3,4), of G_c = ⟨x,y | xyxy⁻²x⁻¹y⟩, and of the other 160 one-relator groups of H §5.3. What P1 does **not** cover: products G_c × G_{c'} for coordinates c, c' of *different* class pairs, and fibre products over bases in which no class coordinate dies.

**Remark TH08-R (relative form of H's Corollary 3).** If M ⊴ Q, M ⊆ [Q,Q], and M has a solvable Q-normal subgroup S with M/S finite, then Q obstructs U iff Q/M does. (Same proof as Corollary 3, descending to Q/M instead of Q_ab: a chief series of Q/S through M/S, then the derived series of S; only 2(i) with N ⊆ [·,·] and corrected 2(ii) are used.) Consequently the set 𝒦(U) := {K ⊴ F : F/K does not obstruct U} satisfies: K ∈ 𝒦(U), K ⊆ [F,F], F/L virtually solvable ⇒ K ∩ L ∈ 𝒦(U) (take M = K/(K∩L) ≅ KL/L).

**Lemma TH08-S (where the central two-pair obstruction of N §2.3 can live).** Let N ≤ Z(Q) ∩ [Q,Q], let (s,t) normally generate Q, n₀, n₁ ∈ N, Q̄ = Q/N. If H₂(Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩; ℤ) = 0 then N ⊆ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩, so (sn₀, tn₁) ~ (s, tn₁); if also H₂(Q̄/⟨⟨s̄⟩⟩; ℤ) = 0 then (sn₀, tn₁) ~ (s,t).
*Proof.* A' := Q/⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩ is a central extension of A := Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩ with kernel N₁ = N/(N ∩ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩), and N₁ ⊆ [A',A'] because N ⊆ [Q,Q]. In the five-term sequence H₂(A) → N₁ → H₁(A') → H₁(A) → 0 the map N₁ → H₁(A') is zero, so H₂(A) → N₁ is onto; H₂(A) = 0 gives N ⊆ ⟨⟨tn₁⟩⟩. Symmetrically for s. ∎
H's Lemma 2.2 is the case where both quotients are cyclic. So a counterexample to the central case (β) of the two-pair problem needs a normally generating pair for which one of the one-normal-generator groups Q̄/⟨⟨s̄⟩⟩, Q̄/⟨⟨t̄⟩⟩ has nonzero Schur multiplier *and* the stem class of N survives every reachable change of base pair; for pairs coming from F these groups are quotients of the one-relator groups G₀, G₁, whose own H₂ vanishes (their relators are not in [F,F]).
