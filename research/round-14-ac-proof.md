# Width-two Wirtinger repairs through degree four

2026-11-19. For the aligned same-row Wirtinger repair, a width-two singleton shift of determinant two has eight explicit congruences on its Hall coefficients. These give the complete fourth-degree condition in the free nilpotent quotient, including the previous mod-5 test. In determinant zero or two, the free middle equation also forces at least four occurrences of the input letter sent to the unknown shift word. This second obstruction allows arbitrarily long intervening powers of the other letter. [Round 13 reduction](round-13-ac-proof.md), [exact residue and free-word receipts](../results/round14_ac_proof_wirtinger_degree4.json).

## The middle equation

Keep the notation $a,b,P,Q$ from the adjacent-index expansion, so $B=Px^{-1}$ and $C=Qx^{-1}$ and

$$
P=a^{-1}bab^{-2},qquad Q=a^{-2}ba^2b^{-2}.
$$

The height-normalized middle equation is $gQ=P\sigma(g)$ in the Magnus base group. Suppose the width is two and the deleted extreme occurs once with sign $\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}$. In the upper-singleton convention the base is freely generated by $a,b$, and the positive shift is $\theta(a)=b$, $\theta(b)=W$. In the lower-singleton convention the indices are positioned so the same named $a,b$ remain in the base, and the descending shift is $\theta(a)=W$, $\theta(b)=a$. Round 13 proves $g\in[F(a,b),F(a,b)]$ in the free base, including determinant zero. The two forms of the exact required equality are

$$
\theta(P^{-1}gQ)=g\quad\text{(upper)},\qquad
P^{-1}gQ=\theta(g)\quad\text{(lower)}.
$$

Write $C_2=[a,b]$, $C_{3,a}=[C_2,a]$, $C_{3,b}=[C_2,b]$ with $[u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$. The $C_2$ coefficient of $PQ^{-1}$ is $1$. In determinant two the equation forces the $C_2$ coefficient of $g$ to be $2$ for the upper singleton and $-1$ for the lower singleton.

## The complete degree-four condition in determinant two

In this case the exponent matrix $T$ of $\theta$ has determinant $2$ and trace $L=3+\epsilon$. The following convention fixes the signs of the three unknowns $r,s,t$ in the shift word $W$, modulo the fourth lower central subgroup:

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=a^{-2}b^L C_2^r C_{3,a}^s C_{3,b}^t &&\text{(upper)},\\
W&=a^L b^{-2} C_2^r C_{3,a}^s C_{3,b}^t &&\text{(lower)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\epsilon=-1$, retain $r\equiv1\pmod5$ from Round 13. The following rows give one further congruence $s+\lambda t\equiv f(r)\pmod p$ for each stated modulus.

| Singleton | $\epsilon$ | $p$ | $\lambda$ | $f(r)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Upper | $-1$ | 3 | 2 | $1$ |
| Upper | $-1$ | 17 | 14 | $11r^2+4r+13$ |
| Upper | $1$ | 3 | 1 | $r^2+2r+1$ |
| Upper | $1$ | 7 | 5 | $5r^2+5r+3$ |
| Lower | $-1$ | 3 | 2 | $r+1$ |
| Lower | $-1$ | 17 | 11 | $15r^2+16r+10$ |
| Lower | $1$ | 3 | 1 | $2r^2+2r+2$ |
| Lower | $1$ | 7 | 3 | $6r^2+r+5$ |

The truncation needs the shift word through degree three because each argument of $\theta$ in the displayed equations lies in the commutator subgroup. Replacing a generator series by a perturbation of degree three changes its commutator series first in degree four, linearly in the perturbation. The three fourth-degree corrections to $g$ are likewise linear. For each residue $r$ and prime $p$, the replay uses the degree-three equation to determine the two degree-three coordinates of $g$, then eliminates its three degree-four coordinates from the sixteen degree-four Magnus coefficients. It checks all $r$ and verifies the displayed quadratic form of the remaining row. The monomials of the three fourth-degree basic commutators are independent modulo each of these primes. [Hall's basic commutator basis](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1950-0038336-7), [replay](../experiments/round14_ac_proof_wirtinger_degree4.py).

To see completeness at this nilpotent degree, let $\alpha,\beta$ be the eigenvalues of $T$. The eigenvalues on degree three are $2\alpha,2\beta$. On degree four they are $2\alpha^2,4,2\beta^2$, from the two-generator Hall commutators of multidegrees $(3,1),(2,2),(1,3)$. The determinants of $I-\theta$ at these degrees are therefore $9-2L$ and $-3(25-2L^2)$, respectively. These are $5,-51$ at $L=2$ and $1,21$ at $L=4$. At each degree the cokernel is finite of squarefree order, so checking the listed primes is equivalent to membership in the integral image, given the previous degree's unique coordinates. Thus the congruences characterize existence in the free nilpotent quotient of class four. The free-group equation remains open even when all the congruences pass. The upper sign-$-1$ case with $s\equiv t\pmod3$ is one completely excluded family, allowing arbitrary higher commutators and arbitrary middle conjugators.

## At least four occurrences in the shift input

For the upper singleton put $u=P^{-1}gQ$. The equation is $\theta(u)=P u Q^{-1}$. For the lower singleton use $g$ directly. Denote by $d$ the determinant of the shift. In the unresolved cases $d=0,2$, the class-two coordinate of $u$ is $d-1$ in the upper case, and that of $g$ is $1-d$ in the lower case. Each is $\pm1$. These follow by comparing the coefficient of $C_2$ on both sides of the exact equation.

If upper $u$ has two occurrences of $b^{\pm1}$, it is $a^k U a^{-k}$ with $U=[a^\eta,b^\delta]$ and $\eta,\delta\in\{1,-1\}$. Indeed the opposite signs of the two $b$ letters and the zero $a$ exponent give that form initially with an arbitrary $a$ power in the commutator. The class-two coefficient $\pm1$ forces that power to be $\pm1$. The analogous form for lower $g$ with two $a$ letters is $b^k U b^{-k}$. Substituting in the upper equation and moving the first $b^\eta$ of $[b^\eta,W^\delta]$ to the other side implies that

$$
a^k U a^{-k}Q^{-1}b^{-\eta}P
$$

is conjugate to $b^{-\eta}$. For the lower equation the analogous necessary condition is that $b^k U b^{-k}Qa^\delta P^{-1}$ is conjugate to $a^\delta$.

In each case write the target as $c^k U c^{-k} V$ with $c=a$ or $b$. Both $U,V$ contain the other generator. Strip maximal initial and terminal $c$ powers, writing $U=c^iU_0c^j$, $V=c^vV_0c^w$. The target is cyclically conjugate to

$$
c^{k+i+w}U_0c^{-k+j+v}V_0.
$$

This word is cyclically reduced and longer than one whenever both displayed powers are nonzero. For each of the eight sign and determinant choices, the replay checks the at most two seam-zero exponents and finds cyclic length greater than one there as well. Zero occurrences are excluded by the nonzero class-two coordinate, while odd counts are impossible for a zero exponent vector. Thus upper $u$ has at least four $b$ occurrences and lower $g$ at least four $a$ occurrences. The result is independent of the lengths and higher coefficients of $W$.
