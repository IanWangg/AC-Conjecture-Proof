## 1. Theorem N1: chirality is invisible to every invariant of the presentation complex (proved here)

Recall the standard definitions (externally reported, classical: Hog-Angeloni–Metzler, *Two-dimensional homotopy and combinatorial group theory*, Ch. I; Wright 1975). The **Q**-moves** on a presentation (x,y | r₁,r₂) are the ordinary AC moves together with a change of generators by any φ ∈ Aut(F₂); two presentations are **Q**-equivalent** iff one is obtained from the other by a finite sequence of such moves. The Q**-class of U is therefore exactly the union ⋃_{φ∈Aut(F₂)} φ([U]_AC) of the AC classes of the automorphic images of U. The presentation complex K(U) is the one-vertex 2-complex with 1-cells e_x, e_y and 2-cells attached along r₁, r₂; a 3-deformation is a finite sequence of elementary expansions and collapses through complexes of dimension ≤ 3.

**Lemma 1.1.** For every signed letter permutation σ (the eight maps generated by τ, ε_x, ε_y) and every presentation U, the complexes K(σU) and K(U) are homeomorphic (indeed CW-isomorphic).
*Proof.* Relabelling the two 1-cells (τ) or reversing the orientation of one 1-cell (ε_x, ε_y) is a cellular homeomorphism of the 1-skeleton; a 2-cell attached along the loop that reads r_i in the old labelling reads σ(r_i) in the new one. So the identity map of the underlying space is a CW isomorphism K(U) → K(σU). ∎

**Lemma 1.2.** For every φ ∈ Aut(F₂), K(φU) and K(U) are 3-deformation equivalent (classical).
*Proof sketch, for completeness.* It suffices to treat the Nielsen generators. For τ, ε_x, ε_y use Lemma 1.1. For φ = (x ↦ xy, y ↦ y): expand K(U) by a 1-cell e_z and a 2-cell attached along z⁻¹xy (an elementary expansion, since z occurs once); in the resulting presentation (x,y,z | r₁, r₂, z⁻¹xy) replace each r_i(x,y) by r_i(zy⁻¹, y), which differs from r_i by a product of conjugates of (z⁻¹xy)^{±1} — this is a sequence of AC moves, i.e. 3-deformations (each "multiply a relator by a conjugate of another" is realized by sliding a 2-cell across another, a 3-deformation); now x occurs only in z⁻¹xy, once, so collapse e_x together with that 2-cell. The result is (z,y | r_i(zy⁻¹,y)) ≅ K(φ⁻¹U) after renaming z to x. The same for φ⁻¹. ∎

**Theorem N1 (proved here).** Let I be any function on balanced presentations of the trivial group that factors through (a) the homeomorphism type of K(U), or (b) the 3-deformation type of K(U), or (c) the Q**-class of U, or more generally (d) through any equivalence relation generated by the ordinary AC moves together with *some* automorphisms of F₂ that, together with Inn(F₂), generate a subgroup A ≤ Aut(F₂). Then I(σU) = I(U) for every σ ∈ A (in cases (a), (c): for every signed permutation σ; in (b), (c): for every φ ∈ Aut(F₂)). In particular **no invariant of type (a)–(c) can separate C4 from any σ(C4), nor from any φ(C4), φ ∈ Aut(F₂).**
*Proof.* (a) and (b) by Lemmas 1.1–1.2; (c) and (d) because φ(U) is Q**-equivalent (resp. A-equivalent) to U by definition. ∎

**What this removes.** Every invariant of the 2-complex: homotopy type (contractible anyway), Whitehead torsion / simple homotopy type, the 3-deformation type and its quantum (Bobtcheva–Quinn) or TQFT-type refinements, thickenability (F) and all 3-manifold data derived from thickenings, the "torus-knot-pair / BS-type" structure of F (which is Aut-invariant by construction, F §3), the presented group and every group-theoretic datum of it, and every "generalized AC" class that admits basis changes. None of this can see chirality. This complements B/E/H (which remove finite, solvable and the listed infinite quotients) and K (monotone quantities) and sharpens I's remark that a chirality invariant "cannot factor through the group".

**What survives, and the precise shape it must have.** Aut(F₂) acts on the set 𝒞 of AC classes (Lemma D2 of research-d, for the identity U ~ V ⇒ φU ~ φV), and the stabilizer of a class is a subgroup S([U]) ≤ Aut(F₂) containing Inn(F₂); write Ŝ(U) = μ(S([U])) ≤ GL₂(ℤ). A chirality invariant of U is a function on 𝒞 that is not constant on the orbit Aut(F₂)·[U]; it exists iff Ŝ(U) ≠ GL₂(ℤ). By Theorem N1 it must be an invariant of the *framed* object (F with its basis (x,y), U), i.e. of the 𝒜-orbit of U in F², that is not invariant under the normalizing action of Aut(F₂) on 𝒜-orbits. Concretely: a quotient π: F → Q whose kernel is not characteristic (§2), or a function of the pair of words that uses the labels x, y asymmetrically. In the terminology of the AC group of Gilman–Myasnikov (externally reported; not consulted), it is a function on F² constant on 𝒜-orbits but not on ⟨𝒜, Aut(F₂)⟩-orbits.

Two elementary facts about Ŝ that are used in §3: (i) Ŝ(φU) = μ(φ) Ŝ(U) μ(φ)⁻¹, so the question "Ŝ(U) = GL₂(ℤ)?" is a class invariant; (ii) if both relators of U are *cyclic palindromes* (each word read backwards is a rotation of itself; true for x^p y^q, for x^a y x⁻¹ y and for the braid relator), then ι(U) ≡_Σ U⁻¹ ≡_Σ U, hence −I ∈ Ŝ(U); this applies to AK(n), C3, C4, and to every T(a;p,q). So for these classes the chirality question lives in PGL₂(ℤ) = GL₂(ℤ)/{±I}.

## 2. Quotients that compare two pairs: what changes relative to H (proved here)

H's framework compares a pair with the *standard* pair (a,b) = (π(x),π(y)), which generates Q. A chirality question compares π(U) with π(σU), two pairs that only *normally* generate Q. This section records exactly which of H's tools survive. Throughout, π: F → Q is an epimorphism, "~_Q" is AC equivalence in Q (inversion, right multiplication by the other coordinate or its inverse, conjugation by arbitrary elements of Q, swap), and Q **separates** U from V (two pairs normally generating F) if π(U) ≁_Q π(V). If U ~ V in F no quotient separates them; U ≁ V iff the identity quotient separates them.

### 2.1 Reduction to invariant kernels

**Theorem N2.1 (proved here).** Let φ ∈ Aut(F₂) and suppose Q = F/N separates U from φ(U). Put N' = ⋂_{i∈ℤ} φⁱ(N). Then Q' = F/N' separates U from φ(U), φ induces φ̄ ∈ Aut(Q') with π'∘φ = φ̄∘π', and the separation says precisely that the AC class of π'(U) in Q' is **not fixed by φ̄**. The standard class [(π'x, π'y)] is fixed by every automorphism of Q' induced from Aut(F₂).
*Proof.* N' ⊆ N, so Q' maps onto Q and AC equivalence passes to quotients: π'(U) ~ π'(φU) would give π(U) ~ π(φU). φ(N') = N' by construction, so φ̄ exists. φ̄(π'x, π'y) = π'(φx, φy) is the image of a basis of F, which is Nielsen-equivalent to (x,y); Nielsen moves are AC moves. ∎

So a chirality obstruction is always an Aut(Q)-action on the set of AC classes of pairs in Q that fixes the standard class but moves the class of π(U). (Finite Q: impossible, BLM; solvable Q: impossible, Guyot — both for pair-vs-pair comparisons, since those theorems compare arbitrary normally generating pairs with Nielsen-equivalent abelianizations.)

### 2.2 Two-pair lifting through nonabelian minimal normal subgroups

**Theorem N2.2 (proved here; H's Theorem 2(ii) with the coordinator's corrected hypothesis, in two-pair form).** Let N be a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup of Q and let P = (p₀,p₁), P' = (s,t) be pairs each normally generating Q. If the images of P and P' in Q/N are AC-equivalent, then P ~_Q P'. Hence Q separates U from V iff Q/N does.
*Proof.* Lift a path from P̄ to P̄' to Q: P ~ (s n₀, t n₁) =: P'' with n_i ∈ N; P'' normally generates Q because AC moves preserve normal closures. Minimality gives N ∩ ⟨⟨g⟩⟩ ∈ {1, N} for every g, and (†): if (u,v) normally generates Q and ⟨⟨u⟩⟩ ∩ N = 1 then N ⊆ ⟨⟨v⟩⟩ — for [N,⟨⟨u⟩⟩] ⊆ N ∩ ⟨⟨u⟩⟩ = 1, so if also N ∩ ⟨⟨v⟩⟩ = 1 then Q = ⟨⟨u⟩⟩⟨⟨v⟩⟩ centralizes N and N is abelian. Now:
1. If ⟨⟨t n₁⟩⟩ ∩ N = 1, then N ⊆ ⟨⟨s n₀⟩⟩ by (†) applied to P''. Pick m, z ∈ N with [m,z] ≠ 1 and multiply coordinate 1 by m (allowed: m lies in the normal closure of coordinate 0). Since t n₁ centralizes N, [t n₁ m, z] = [m,z] ≠ 1 lies in ⟨⟨t n₁ m⟩⟩ ∩ N, so N ⊆ ⟨⟨t n₁ m⟩⟩. Rename n₁ := n₁ m.
2. Multiply coordinate 0 by n₀⁻¹ ∈ N ⊆ ⟨⟨t n₁⟩⟩: the pair is (s, t n₁).
3. If N ⊆ ⟨⟨s⟩⟩, remove n₁ directly. Otherwise ⟨⟨s⟩⟩ ∩ N = 1, s centralizes N; as in step 1 replace s by s m' (m' ∈ N ⊆ ⟨⟨t n₁⟩⟩, [m',z'] ≠ 1), get N ⊆ ⟨⟨s m'⟩⟩, remove n₁, obtaining (s m', t). Finally P' = (s,t) normally generates Q and s centralizes N, so (†) gives N ⊆ ⟨⟨t⟩⟩ and m' can be removed. ∎

### 2.3 Two-pair lifting through abelian normal subgroups: the correction modules and where H's argument stops

Let N ⊴ Q be abelian, written additively as a right ℤ[Q]-module, n·g := g⁻¹ n g. For a subgroup L ≤ Q put [N,L] := Σ_{g∈L} N(g−1); when L is normal this is a ℤ[Q]-submodule, and [N,Q] = [N,⟨⟨s⟩⟩] + [N,⟨⟨t⟩⟩] whenever Q = ⟨⟨s,t⟩⟩ (from n(gh−1) = n(g−1)h + n(h−1)).

**Theorem N2.3 (proved here).** Let P' = (s,t) normally generate Q, and let n₀, n₁ ∈ N. Put
    D₀ := N(s−1) + [N,⟨⟨t⟩⟩],   D₁ := N(t−1) + [N,⟨⟨s⟩⟩].
Then (s n₀, t n₁) ~_Q (s(n₀+d₀), t(n₁+d₁)) for all d₀ ∈ D₀, d₁ ∈ D₁. Consequently (s n₀, t n₁) ~_Q (s,t) whenever n₀ ∈ D₀ and n₁ ∈ D₁; and if ⟨s,t⟩ = Q then D₀ = D₁ = [N,Q] (H, Lemma 2.1), recovering H's Theorem 2(i).
*Proof.* Conjugating coordinate 0 by m ∈ N replaces n₀ by n₀ + m(1−s), giving N(s−1). For q ∈ Q and m ∈ N, the five moves "conjugate coordinate 1 by q; multiply coordinate 0 by coordinate 1; conjugate coordinate 1 back; conjugate coordinate 0 by m; multiply coordinate 0 by the inverse of the q-conjugate of coordinate 1 (conjugate, invert, multiply, invert, conjugate back)" leave coordinate 1 unchanged and replace n₀ by n₀ + m·(q⁻¹tq)⁻¹ − m·s = n₀ + m((q⁻¹tq)⁻¹ − 1) − m(s−1), exactly as in H's "multiply trick" with the donor conjugated first (the verification is H's computation with t replaced by q⁻¹tq; N abelian is used to commute n₁ past m). Since N(g⁻¹−1) = N(g−1) for every g, this gives N(q⁻¹tq − 1) = N(t−1)·q for every q, whose sum over q is [N,⟨⟨t⟩⟩]. Coordinate 1 is symmetric. ∎

**Where H's argument stops (proved here).** The corrections available *without changing the base pair* are exactly D₀ × D₁ by this mechanism. When (s,t) only normally generates Q, D₀ can be a proper submodule of [N,Q]: for N central, D₀ = D₁ = 0 while N ∩ [Q,Q] may be nonzero. H's Lemma 2.2 closes this gap for the standard pair by using that Q/⟨⟨b⟩⟩ is cyclic, hence abelian, so [Q,Q] ⊆ ⟨⟨b⟩⟩; for a merely normally generating pair, Q/⟨⟨t⟩⟩ is a group with one normal generator and need not be abelian. So **the two-pair analogue of H's Theorem 2(i) is not proved by H's method, and I could not prove it by any other** (open; see 2.4 for the exact residual statement). In the finite and solvable cases it is true by BLM and Guyot; a counterexample would have to be an infinite non-solvable Q with an abelian normal subgroup inside [Q,Q].

### 2.4 The exact residual statement: affine monodromy

For N abelian normal, every AC move acts on the N-components of a pair *linearly over ℤ[Q]*, with coefficients depending on the current base pair: (p₀n₀)⁻¹ = p₀⁻¹·(−n₀·p₀⁻¹); (p₀n₀)(p₁n₁) = p₀p₁·(n₀·p₁ + n₁); q⁻¹(p₀n₀)q = (q⁻¹p₀q)·(n₀·q). Hence for a move sequence m and a pair P, m(P·n) = m(P)·(A_m(P) n) with A_m(P) ∈ GL₂(ℤ[Q]) (right-module endomorphisms) and n ∈ N². For a pair P' put Π(P') := {m : m(P') ≡ P' mod N²} and write m(P') = P'·ζ(m), ζ(m) ∈ N². The maps α_m: n ↦ A_m(P') n + ζ(m), m ∈ Π(P'), form a group 𝓜(P') of affine transformations of N² (composition: ζ(m'∘m) = ζ(m') + A_{m'}(P')ζ(m)), the **affine AC-monodromy** at P'. Then:

**Proposition N2.4 (proved here).** (i) E(P') := {n ∈ N² : P'·n ~_Q P'} is the orbit 𝓜(P')·0 of the origin. (ii) If P̄ ~ P̄' in Q/N via m₀, so that m₀(P) = P'·ζ₀, then P ~_Q P' iff ζ₀ ∈ E(P'). (iii) E(P') ⊇ D₀ × D₁ (Theorem N2.3), and H's Theorem 2(i) is the statement E((a,b)) ⊇ (N ∩ [Q,Q])² for the generating pair (a,b).
*Proof.* (i) P'·n ~ P' iff some m has m(P'·n) = P', i.e. m ∈ Π(P') and A_m n + ζ(m) = 0, i.e. α_m(n) = 0, i.e. n = α_m⁻¹(0) ∈ 𝓜(P')·0. (ii) m₀(P) = P'·ζ₀ ~ P iff ... P ~ P' iff P'·ζ₀ ~ P' iff ζ₀ ∈ E(P'). (iii) is N2.3. ∎

For N central and Q̄ = Q/N finite, E(P') ⊇ ... and in fact everything is standard (H Cor. 3(b), since [Q,Q] is then finite); H's linear monodromy Λ_G is the image of 𝓜 in GL₂(ℤ) for the fibre products. So **a quotient-type chirality invariant, if one exists, must appear as an affine-monodromy orbit E(π σU) ⊂ N² missing the element ζ₀ produced by a path in Q/N, for a central or abelian N inside [Q,Q] of an infinite non-solvable Q; or in a Q with no abelian or nonabelian minimal normal subgroups at all (H's Theorem 4 situation).** This is the complete list, by N2.1–N2.4 and H's Theorems 1–4. Nothing in the repository, or known to me, exhibits a single instance of the first kind; the second kind is as hard as the conjecture itself.

**Corollary N2.5 (just-separating quotients; proved here).** If U ≁_AC σ(U), then (Zorn, exactly as in H's Theorem 4) some Q just-separates them: Q separates and no proper quotient does. Such a Q has no nonabelian minimal normal subgroup (N2.2), is infinite and not virtually solvable (BLM, Guyot, H Cor. 3 — all valid for two pairs), and for every abelian normal N ⊴ Q the obstruction is located in the fibre N² as in N2.4(ii) with ζ₀ ∉ D₀(πσU) × D₁(πσU).

### 2.5 Angle 2 of the brief: fibre products over an infinite base

**Proposition N2.6 (proved here; H's Prop. 5.1(3) for two pairs and any base).** Let H be a quotient of F, Q := H ×_{H_ab} ℤ² = F/(ker π_H ∩ [F,F]), and let M_U ∈ GL₂(ℤ) be the exponent matrix of U (rows = exponent vectors). For U, V normally generating F: π_Q(U) ~_Q π_Q(V) iff there is a move sequence m with m(π_H U) = π_H V in H and μ(m)·M_U = M_V. Equivalently: π_H(U) ~_H π_H(V), and M_V M_U⁻¹ ∈ μ(m₀)·Λ_H(U) for one (any) m₀ with m₀(π_H U) = π_H V, where Λ_H(U) := μ(Stab_𝒜(π_H U)) is the linear monodromy of AC loops at π_H(U).
*Proof.* Q ⊆ H × ℤ² and every AC move acts diagonally; conjugation acts trivially on the ℤ² factor; the ℤ²-component of π_Q(U) is M_U. ∎

For V = σ(U) the required matrix is M_{σU}M_U⁻¹ = M_U μ(σ)ᵀ M_U⁻¹. Three remarks. (a) The invariant is nontrivial only if Λ_H(U) is a *proper* subgroup of its congruence envelope Γ_H(U) = {M : M·M_U ≡ M_U over H_ab}; H proved Λ = Γ for every finite H at the standard pair, and Theorem N2.2/N2.3 do not decide it for infinite H at a non-generating pair. (b) H must not kill a class coordinate of U or of σ(U) (H Cor. 1.1 applied to each pair separately; for C4 the known class coordinates include x⁸y⁵, x⁴yx⁻¹y, xy³xy⁻², from I's certificate `i_c4_to_T4_8_5.json`). (c) Lower bounds on Λ_H(U) are cheap (exhibit loops) and *kill* the candidate; an upper bound is an obstruction and is as hard as the problem. I did not run this computation (it needs a decision procedure for AC paths in H; for H a torus-knot group G's conjugacy normal forms would be the starting point).

## 3. The realized part of GL₂(ℤ) on the class of C4 (proved here by certificate; experiment for the negative part)

### 3.1 Method

Let P₀ be a presentation and Fwd(P₀, C) the forward-reachable set of D's engine (every member is ordinary-AC-equivalent to P₀). If two *different* Σ-states S, S' ∈ Fwd(P₀, C) lie in one Out(F₂)-orbit, S' ≡_Σ φ(S), then φ(P₀) ~ φ(S) ≡_Σ S' ~ P₀, so μ(φ) ∈ Ŝ(P₀). I's test (research-i §5) asked only whether the seven Nielsen-generator images of the representative P₀ itself lie in Fwd(P₀, C). The test here uses *all* pairs of states, i.e. it reads the stabilizer off the orbit structure of the whole forward set: `experiments/n_autstab.py` groups a state dump by D's Whitehead canonical form (`d_whitehead.aut_canon`, imported read-only), recovers an explicit automorphism for every coincidence not explained by ι (BFS through minimal pairs with the four type-II Whitehead maps and the three signed-permutation generators), and records its matrix modulo ±I. With `--explain` it then generates the group ⟨ι, recovered automorphisms⟩ (matrices mod ±I) and checks that every multi-member orbit is accounted for by that group alone. Self-test: 141 planted automorphisms recovered; the closure of the two automorphisms of 3.2 with ι has order 6 mod ±I; the closure of x ↦ xy alone is reported as infinite.

### 3.2 Result for C4 = (x⁴yx⁻¹y, xy³xy⁻²) at cap 28

## 4. Honest limits

- Theorem N1 is elementary; its value is that it closes a whole category of candidate chirality invariants at once (everything attached to K(U), including all 3-manifold/thickening data and all generalized-move classes). It says nothing about AK(3) itself, whose class is Aut-closed.
- Theorems N2.1–N2.3 and Proposition N2.4 are proved as stated; the two-pair analogue of H's Theorem 2(i) (abelian normal subgroups inside [Q,Q] are invisible) is **open** — I could neither prove it nor exhibit a counterexample. Everything in §2 is a necessary-condition/structure result; no obstruction is constructed, and no quotient is shown to separate anything.
- Propositions N3 (Ŝ(C4) ⊇ D₆) and N4 (Ŝ(X) infinite) rest only on the four certificates and on D's Lemma D2 (Aut-equivariance of ~); they do not depend on the correctness of the Whitehead canonical form, which was used only to *find* the coincidences. The negative statement ("nothing beyond D within cap 28") depends on D's `aut_canon` and on the cap; it is a bounded observation. Automorphic images not in D may well appear at higher caps (M's cap-34 forward set of C4 is the obvious next input).
- The dichotomy "Ŝ(C4) = D or infinite" is a trivial consequence of maximality; it does not say which case holds. The second case is the AC-conjecture-compatible one.
- Literature: arXiv was blocked; Q**-equivalence/3-deformation facts are standard (Hog-Angeloni–Metzler; Wright) and quoted from memory; the finite subgroups of GL₂(ℤ) likewise. Whether the D₆-symmetry of the C4 class is known (e.g. to the authors of the nahomar/andrews-curtis-solver classification, who work modulo signed permutations only) could not be checked. Novelty is not established for anything.
- Resource use: all runs single-core, niced, < 400 MB; the Python canonicalization of a 216K-state dump takes ≈ 70 s. D's Whitehead closure has no size cap and hangs on some energy-28 states of Y; `n_autstab.py` now caps it (overflowing states get a unique key, so coincidences can be missed but never invented; 0 overflows occurred in the reported C4, C6, AK3 and X runs).
- The bounded GL₂(ℤ) closure test for X (entries ≤ 12, depth 6) is a search, not a membership proof; Ŝ(X) ∌ swap is *not* claimed.

## 5. Advice to the coordinator (ranked)

1. **Run `n_autstab.py --explain` on every large forward set that already exists** (M's cap-34 dump of C4, cap-32 of C6, the cap-28/29 dumps of AK3, P1, P2, C3, X, Y). Conversely, if ever a forward set of C4 of size ≫ 10⁷ still shows exactly D₆, that is the first quantitative "chirality signal" in the project — still no proof, but a sharp, falsifiable statement.
2. **Redirect the chirality target.** Not "C4 vs σ(C4) for a signed permutation" in general: the three reflections and the order-3 element of D are realized, and −I is trivially realized on every cyclic-palindromic pair. The open cosets are those of τ (swap), ε_x, ε_y and the unipotents; the cheapest single test is whether any state of Fwd(C4, C) is a τ-image of another state (the engine's `--trivial-targets` matches only against *given* targets, so use `n_autstab.py`, which compares all pairs).
3. **Theory problem worth one agent (sharp and self-contained):** prove or refute the two-pair analogue of H's Theorem 2(i): *if N ⊴ Q is abelian with N ⊆ [Q,Q] and two normally generating pairs become AC-equivalent in Q/N, are they AC-equivalent in Q?* True for finite and solvable Q (BLM, Guyot). A proof pushes every quotient-type chirality obstruction into H's "no abelian normal subgroups in [Q,Q]" territory; a refutation with an explicit infinite Q would be the first pair-separating mechanism in the project and the natural candidate for a chirality obstruction (Prop. N2.4 says exactly what to compute: the affine monodromy orbit of 0 in N²).
4. **Stop looking for chirality in topology.** By Theorem N1 nothing attached to the presentation complex, its thickenings, 3-deformations, TQFT/quantum invariants or any generalized-move class can see it. Chirality lives in the framing, i.e. in non-characteristic (or characteristic but basis-sensitive) quotients and in nothing else.
5. **Fibre products over infinite H (angle 2):** Prop. N2.6 gives the exact criterion; the only computation worth doing is a *lower bound* on Λ_H(C4) for a torus-knot group H (G's conjugacy normal forms make AC loops in H checkable) — it kills the candidate if Λ_H = Γ_H, and the upper-bound direction is the problem itself. Low priority.
6. **Uniform structure question for I/O:** is the hexagonal symmetry a property of the whole shared-vertex family T(a;p,q) (C4 ~ T(4;8,5))? The order-3 element R permutes a Farey triangle; the three vertices x, xz, z of T(a;p,q) also form one. A Lemma-S-style explicit cyclic-product sequence realizing an order-3 automorphism uniformly on T(a;p,q) would be a theorem. §3.3 shows that the picture is not uniform: X = T(2;4,9) (level 1 of I's T(2;·) series, a = 2) already has an *infinite* stabilizer with an infinite-order element realized at barrier 24, while C4 ~ T(4;8,5) (a = 4) shows only the finite D₆ at cap 28. Understanding why the a = 2 members (C3, X) carry infinite symmetry — C3's class is even Aut-closed — and whether the a = 4 members (C4, Y) do, is the sharpest structural question this note leaves.
