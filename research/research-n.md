## The theorem

Put F=F(x,y), X=x^-1, Y=y^-1, and define

    p_n=x^n Y^(n+1),   b=xyxYXY,
    B=<x,y | b=1>,   pi:F -> B.

**Theorem.** For every n>=2 and every c in B, the entire subgroup

    pi^-1(c <pi(p_n)> c^-1)

contains no primitive element of the ambient free group F.

Here primitive means belonging to a free basis of F. There is no word
length bound, no bound on c, and no finite-rank assumption on the
preimage subgroup.

In particular, for arbitrary q,r in F,

    <q p_n q^-1, r b r^-1>

contains no ambient primitive element. The same holds if the second
displayed generator is replaced by **any collection of words in the
normal closure of b**. It also holds after replacing p_n by any word s
whose image in B is conjugate to p_n or its inverse.

Thus all the power-length-nine departure tuples in the coordinating
agent's catalogue are primitive-free even with arbitrary independent
conjugating tails. Their images satisfy exactly this B-conjugacy
condition. This conclusion does not require separately understanding the
six one-relator deletion models. It does not, by itself, cover the
braid-length-eight departures: the coordinating agent's complementary
torus representation supplies that different argument for AK(3).

## 1. A determinant obstruction to primitive proper-power images

We use the standard representation of B in SL(2,Z):

    U=rho(x)=[[1,1],[0,1]],
    V=rho(y)=[[1,0],[-1,1]].

Direct multiplication gives UVU=VUV and

    trace([U,V])=3,

where [A,B]=ABA^-1B^-1. If u is primitive, choose a free complement v.
The commutator of a free basis of F2 is conjugate to [x,y] or its inverse.
Consequently

    trace([rho(u),rho(v)])=3.                         (1)

This standard Nielsen invariant is reviewed in the primary article
[McCullough–Wanderley, *Nielsen equivalence of generating pairs of
SL(2,q)*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017089512000675). Only the elementary
rank-two commutator fact is used here; no finite-field conclusion from
that paper is imported. It can also be checked directly on the Nielsen
generators: interchange, inversion, and multiplication send the
commutator to a conjugate of itself or its inverse.

For arbitrary A,B in SL(2,Z),

    (AB-BA) A^-1 B^-1 = [A,B]-I.

Taking determinants yields the exact identity

    det(AB-BA)=2-trace([A,B]).                        (2)

Thus (1) forces det(AB-BA)=-1 when A=rho(u) and B=rho(v).

Now let C be any integral determinant-one matrix with trace T>=3. Define
integers f_j(T) for every integral j by

    f_0=0, f_1=1, f_(j+1)=T f_j-f_(j-1), f_(-j)=-f_j.

Cayley–Hamilton gives, for all integral m,

    C^m=f_m(T) C-f_(m-1)(T) I.                      (3)

The scalar term commutes with B. Hence

    det(C^m B-B C^m)
       =f_m(T)^2 det(CB-BC).                         (4)

If |m|>=2, the coefficient satisfies |f_m(T)|>=T>1. For positive m this
follows from f_2=T and the strictly increasing recurrence; negative m
follows by the sign rule. Equation (4) therefore cannot equal -1.

**Proper-power lemma.** In this braid-matrix representation, a primitive
free word cannot have image conjugate in SL(2,Z) to C^m when T>=3 and
|m|>=2. It also cannot have matrix image I, because its commutator with
every matrix would have trace two.

Conjugation causes no denominator issue: a conjugate of C by an
SL(2,Z) matrix is another integral matrix with the same trace, and (3)–(4)
apply to it directly. More generally, in any integral representation of
F2, (4) says that the square f_m(T)^2 must divide
2-trace([rho(x),rho(y)]) for a primitive proper-power image. Here that
constant is exactly -1.

## 2. Apply the lemma to all powers of the AK power relator

The power matrix is

    C_n=rho(p_n)=[[n(n+1)+1,n],[n+1,1]],
    trace(C_n)=n(n+1)+2.

For every n>=2 this trace is at least eight. If pi(u) is conjugate in B
to pi(p_n)^m, then rho(u) is conjugate to C_n^m. The proper-power lemma
excludes |m|>=2; the identity case excludes m=0.

The cases m=+1 and m=-1 are exactly J's existing theorem: no primitive
free word has image conjugate to p_n or its inverse in B for n>=2. For
completeness, here is the short uniform argument, with J credited for
its derivation.

The braid character e(x)=e(y)=1 sends p_n to -1. After inverting a
candidate primitive if necessary, its total exponent sum is one. Every
such primitive is freely conjugate to

    v_k=(xY)^k x,   k in Z.

Indeed (xY,x) is a free basis, so v_k is primitive with exponent vector
(k+1,-k), and Nielsen's rank-two primitive conjugacy uniqueness covers
all primitive words with this vector. A primary proof is
[Clay–Conant–Ramasubramanian, Corollary 4.4](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf).

For k>=0 the trace s_k=trace(rho(v_k)) satisfies

    s_0=2, s_1=4, s_(k+2)=3s_(k+1)-s_k.

For k<0 it is s_(-k-1), because swapping x and y is inner in B. In the
quotient C2*C3 of B, put S=xyx and T=xy. Then

    x=T^2 S,  Y=T S.

The cyclic syllable length of p_n is 4n+2, while that of v_k is 4j+2,
where j=k for k>=0 and j=-k-1 otherwise. Conjugacy therefore forces j=n.
But

    s_n > n(n+1)+2  for every n>=2.

The induction starts at s_2=10>8, and s_(n+1)>2s_n makes the inequality
strict at the next n. This contradicts equality with trace(C_n). It
excludes both remaining powers. For AK(3), trace(C_3)=14 lies strictly
between s_2=10 and s_3=26; the free-product step is needed for a uniform
all-n proof because a trace collision occurs at n=56.

Combining all m proves that no primitive u maps into any conjugate of
the cyclic subgroup <pi(p_n)> in B. This proves the theorem.

## 3. Why the arbitrary-tail consequence is exact

Let q,r be arbitrary free words. In B the braid generator vanishes, so

    pi(q p_n q^-1)=pi(q) pi(p_n) pi(q)^-1,
    pi(r b r^-1)=1.

Every word in these generators therefore has B-image in the one
displayed conjugate cyclic subgroup. The preimage theorem excludes an
ambient primitive at **every word length**. No graph folding, tail bound,
finite quotient sieve, or assumption that a subgroup primitive maps to
p_n rather than p_n^m is required.

Arbitrary Nielsen moves preserve the generated subgroup, so they cannot
produce a primitive waypoint from any of these tuples. This includes
arbitrary long Nielsen preludes after the independent initial
conjugations, and arbitrary powers or products of the generators.

This is stronger than testing the finite literal catalogue, but remains
a restricted regime. An individual conjugation of a later Nielsen word
can change its braid image from a power in one common cyclic subgroup
to a power in a different conjugate cyclic subgroup. Both resulting
images can be nontrivial, and their generated image need no longer be
cyclic. The preimage theorem does not constrain that new regime. It is
therefore **not an invariant under all AC moves**.

The n=2 case is a useful check on that logical boundary: the theorem
holds for n=2, but AK(2) has the exact ordinary certificates already
published here. The n=1 exception is also necessary: p_1=xYY is itself
primitive, and therefore lies in its own cyclic preimage.

## 4. Exact checks and reproducibility

`experiments/cyclic_braid_preimage.py` uses Python 3 and only its standard
library. Run

```sh
python3 experiments/cyclic_braid_preimage.py \
  --output results/cyclic_braid_preimage_results.json
```

The saved run passes:

- 1,225 exact Cayley–Hamilton coefficient identities for n=2,...,50 and
  m=-12,...,12, including negative and zero powers;
- 19,481 exact determinant/commutator/divisibility comparisons against
  all freely reduced test words of length at most four in the stated
  matrix family;
- 2,000 free-basis Nielsen updates, checking commutator trace three with
  signed, long primitive words;
- the uniform trace recurrence and C2*C3 cyclic syllable formulas,
  the n=1 primitive exception, and the n=56 trace-collision control.

For AK(3), the first coefficients f_0,...,f_5 are

    0, 1, 14, 195, 2716, 37829.

Thus already a square of 14 divides the required determinant for a
square power image, whereas a primitive basis requires determinant -1.
The finite checks validate exact formulas and conventions; the theorem
at unbounded n, m, tail length and subgroup word length follows from the
proof, not extrapolation of these checks.

The coordinating agent separately observed that the same proper-power
lemma applies to an integral representation of T(3,4) with x of order
three and y of order four. Combined with J's m=+1/-1 torus theorem, that
provides the complementary arbitrary-tail result for the braid-length-eight
catalogue. It is a separate coordinating-agent extension, not an outcome
of this report's B3 representation.

This investigation closes a specific unbounded gap in the primitive-free
subgroup analysis. It supplies no complete free-basis path and no
obstruction preserved by unrestricted AC moves. None of the four full
breakthrough objectives has been achieved.
