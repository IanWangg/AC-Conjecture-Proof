# Noninvertible substitutions on the once-stabilized orbit

2026-11-19. A complete menu of 432 short single-generator substitutions was applied to all 11,951 signed rank-three orbits of cyclic energy at most 16 in the generated cap-22 excursion catalogue. None of the 5,162,832 images has a primitive coordinate after any available primitive-row deletion. [Generation, exact chart screen and positive control](../results/round09_stable_substitutions.json).

By the [once-stabilized endomorphism argument](round-09-stable-proof.md#endomorphisms-preserving-the-component), replacing one generator $g$ by any ambient word of $g$-exponent one and fixing the other two generators sends every triple in the ordinary stabilized orbit to a triple in that same orbit. Independent cyclic reduction of its rows is allowed by conjugation. The scan takes every freely reduced replacement of length at most five with $g$-exponent one and at least two positive occurrences of $g$, for each of the three generator choices. Every word in this menu has precisely three occurrences of $g^{\pm1}$.

These replacements are noninvertible. In the free splitting over the other letters, trim those letters from the beginning and end of the replacement. The trimmed word begins and ends with a $g$-letter and still contains at least three such letters. To fold the subgroup generated by the fixed letters and this word, attach its based loop to the rose on the fixed letters. The loop folds to its usual stem and cyclic circuit, with no further folds into the fixed rose since its initial $g$ directions are absent there. The resulting graph has no based single-letter loop reading $g$. Thus the images generate a proper subgroup. The normal-generation property still holds for the substituted relator tuple by the ordinary orbit argument.

## Restrictions on what a substitution can achieve

The [primitive-projection argument](round-09-stable-proof.md) explains the negative result for every endomorphism of unimodular abelianization. A primitive image of a row implies that the row was already primitive, and the induced endomorphism between the two primitive deletions gives the same implication for each surviving coordinate.

Geometricity also descends along an injective homomorphism of finite-rank free groups. Suppose $\phi(T)$ is represented by pairwise disjoint simple closed curves on the boundary of a handlebody $V$. In the cover $\widetilde V$ corresponding to $H=\phi(F)$, choose a closed lift of each curve by choosing its based conjugating path so that the lift represents the specified conjugacy class in $H$. These lifts remain embedded and disjoint. Model $V$ by thickening a rose. Its cover is assembled by thickening the covering graph, whose finite core carries $H$. Choose a finite connected subgraph containing the core together with every handle piece touched by the finitely many compact lifts. Enlarge it past the handle cuts that meet those lifts. The thickening is a compact handlebody of fundamental group $H$, and its boundary contains the chosen multicurve. Transporting the marking by the isomorphism $F\to H$ gives a geometric realization of $T$. [Geometric multiwords and elevations, Cashen and Manning](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157015000108).

Both implications continue to apply after any ordinary suffix that uses only conjugators in $H$. Pull the suffix back along $F\to H$ and apply the primitive or geometric argument to its endpoint. A substitution can change which ambient outside conjugators are available on a short route, so further moves involving those conjugators remain a separate case.
