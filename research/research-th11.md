## 2. Theorem T1: Out(G_c) and the stabilizer of c (proved here)

**Lemma 2.1.** (i) F = [G_c, G_c] and G_c^{ab} = ℤ. (ii) The centralizer of A in GL₂(ℤ) is {±A^k}, and A is not conjugate to A⁻¹ in GL₂(ℤ). (iii) No power A^k, k ≠ 0, has an eigenvalue ±1. (iv) C_{G_c}(x) = ⟨x⟩, and x is not conjugate to x⁻¹ in G_c.

*Proof.* (i) det(A − I) = det[[−1,1],[1,−2]] = 1, so the coinvariants of φ on F^{ab} vanish and G_c^{ab} = ℤ, with kernel F. (ii) e₁ is a cyclic vector (Ae₁ = e₂), so a matrix commuting with A is p + qA; ℤ[A] ≅ ℤ[t]/(t²+t−1) is the ring of integers of ℚ(√5), whose units are ±ε^k, and A corresponds to a root of t²+t−1, i.e. to ε⁻¹ or −ε: the units are ±A^k. tr A = −1 ≠ 1 = tr A⁻¹. (iii) The eigenvalues of A are (−1±√5)/2, of absolute value ≠ 1. (iv) (v,m)(a,0)(v,m)⁻¹ = (vφ^m(a)v⁻¹, 0); if this is (a^{±1},0), abelianizing gives A^m e₁ = ±e₁, so m = 0 by (iii) and the sign is +; then v ∈ C_F(a) = ⟨a⟩. ∎

**Theorem T1.** (a) θ₀ induces an automorphism θ₀* of G_c with θ₀*(u,0) = (ι(u),0), θ₀*(1,1) = (b,1), hence θ₀*(u,1) = (ι(u)b, 1). (b) Out(G_c) = {1, [θ₀*]} ≅ ℤ/2. (c) If θ ∈ Aut(F₂) and θ(c) is conjugate to c^{±1}, then θ is inner or θ₀ times inner; the sign is + in the first case and − in the second.

*Proof.* (a) θ₀(c) = X·yx·X·XY·XY·x·yx = XyXYXYxyx (free reduction), whose cyclic reduction yXYXYxy is a rotation of c⁻¹ = YxyyXYX. So θ₀ descends to G_c. θ₀(a) = x⁻¹ = a⁻¹, θ₀(b) = yx·X·XY = yXY = b⁻¹, θ₀(y) = yx = (yxY)y = (b,1).
(b) Let α ∈ Aut(G_c). By 2.1(i) α preserves F; let α₀ = α|_F with matrix N, and α(y) = (w, ε), ε = ±1. Applying α to y u y⁻¹ = φ(u) gives w φ^ε(α₀(u)) w⁻¹ = α₀(φ(u)), and abelianizing, A^ε N = N A, i.e. N A N⁻¹ = A^ε. By 2.1(ii) ε = 1 and N commutes with A, so N = ±A^k. Composing α with conjugation by y^{−k} (which acts on F by φ^{−k}) we get N = ±I. By Nielsen's theorem (an automorphism of F₂ acting trivially on F₂^{ab} is inner) α₀ is inner or ι times inner; composing with conjugation by an element of F, α₀ ∈ {id, ι}. If α₀ = id: w φ(u) w⁻¹ = φ(u) for all u, so w = 1 and α = id. If α₀ = ι: for u = a, w b⁻¹ w⁻¹ = b⁻¹, so w = b^j; for u = b, w a⁻¹b w⁻¹ = b a⁻¹, i.e. b^j a⁻¹ b^{1−j} = b a⁻¹, so j = 1 and α = θ₀*. Finally θ₀* is not inner: inner automorphisms act on F^{ab} by powers of A, and −I is not a power of A (2.1(iii)).
(c) θ induces an automorphism of G_c; by (b), after composing θ with an inner automorphism of F₂ and possibly with θ₀, we may assume θ induces the identity on G_c: θ(x) ≡ x, θ(y) ≡ y modulo ⟨⟨c⟩⟩. The exponent vector of c is (1,0), so the matrix of θ is [[1+k, l],[0,1]] with 1+k = ±1 =: ε. By Nielsen θ = inn_h ∘ ν with ν = (x ↦ x^ε, y ↦ x^l y). Then in G_c: h̄ x^ε h̄⁻¹ = x, so ε = 1 and h̄ = x^j by 2.1(iv); and x^j·x^l y·x^{−j} = y, i.e. (a^{j+l}b^{−j}, 1) = (1,1), so j = l = 0 and θ is inner. Since θ₀(c) ~ c⁻¹ and c is not conjugate to c⁻¹ in F₂ (exponent vector (1,0) ≠ 0), the signs are as stated. ∎

Consequences. θ₀(xˢy) = x⁻ˢyx ~ x^{1−s}y (so θ₀ exchanges the two cusp classes {x⁻¹y, y} and {xy, x²y} of O4); cyc θ₀(k_n) = X^nYxyxy = x⁻ⁿy⁻¹xyxy, with normal form class (a⁻ⁿbab, 1) (the coordinator's formula; `o_gc.nf("XXXYxyxy")` = (a⁻²ba, 1), which is a⁻¹-twisted-conjugate to it). θ₀(P_n) is, up to conjugating and inverting the first relator, **P_n′ := (c, x⁻ⁿy⁻¹xyxy)**, of total length n + 12.

Independent machine check of (c): `experiments/th11_stab.py` (next section) returns for c exactly: Stab(c) = {I}, {M : θ_M(c) ~ c⁻¹} = {[[−1,1],[0,1]]} — the matrix of θ₀.

## 3. Proposition T2: stabilizers of b and p_n (proved modulo Whitehead's theorem)

*Recalled (Whitehead 1936; Higgins–Lyndon 1974; Lyndon–Schupp I.4):* if a cyclic word w has minimal length in its Aut(F)-orbit and θ(w) has the same length, then θ is a product of Whitehead automorphisms τ_k⋯τ_1 such that every intermediate cyclic word τ_i⋯τ_1(w) has that same length. In rank 2, modulo inner automorphisms, the Whitehead automorphisms are the 8 signed permutations and the four maps y ↦ yx^{±1}, x ↦ xy^{±1}. Since Out(F₂) = GL₂(ℤ), the stabilizer of the cyclic word w^{±1} is the set of matrices labelling paths from w to w^{±1} in the finite graph of minimal words; `experiments/th11_stab.py` computes it by breadth-first search over pairs (cyclic word, matrix), after a greedy Whitehead reduction when w is not minimal (c reduces to xxYYXy, length 6).

**Proposition T2.** (a) Stab(b) = {I, −swap}, and the classes sending b to b⁻¹ are {−I, swap}. (b) For every n ≥ 3: Stab(p_n) = {I}, and the only class sending p_n to p_n⁻¹ is −I. (c) For n = 2 there are in addition the classes ±[[1,0],[−3,−1]] (x ↦ xy⁻³, y ↦ y⁻¹: (xY³)²y³ = xY³x ~ x²Y³).

*Proof.* (a), (c): the exact search (16 minimal words in each case). (b), uniformly in n: the four type-II maps send p_n = xⁿY^{n+1} to cyclic words of lengths 3n (y ↦ yx: x^{n−1}Y(XY)ⁿ), 3n+2 (y ↦ yX), 3n−1 (x ↦ xy: (xy)^{n−1}xYⁿ) and 3n+1 (x ↦ xY), all > 2n+1 for n ≥ 3. Signed permutations normalize the set of type-II maps modulo inner automorphisms, so the same holds for the 8 signed-permutation images of p_n; hence the minimal words are exactly these 8, every edge of the graph is a signed permutation, and the stabilizer consists of the signed permutations σ with σ(p_n) ~ p_n^{±1}: only id and ι = (x ↦ X, y ↦ Y), since n ≠ n+1. The script confirms n = 3, …, 12. ∎

Remarks. (1) swap induces an inner automorphism of the trefoil group F/⟨⟨b⟩⟩ (conjugation by xyx, since xyx·x = y·xyx there), so Stab(b^{±1}) → Out(F/⟨⟨b⟩⟩) is not injective, in contrast with c (T1(c)). (2) For AK(n) = (p_n, b), n ≥ 3, the common stabilizer is {±I}: besides the trivial symmetry ι, **no automorphism maps both relators of AK(n) to conjugates of themselves or their inverses** — every other AC-realization of an automorphism on AK(3) (D6) must move a relator off its Σ-class. (3) For P_n the common stabilizer of c^{±1} and k_n^{±1} is trivial: by T1 the only candidate is θ₀, and θ₀(k_n) is not even conjugate to k_n^{±1} modulo c (T3). (4) Stab(k_2^{±1}) contains −swap (YYxyXX = k_2⁻¹) and Stab(k_n^{±1}) is trivial for n = 3, …, 6 (script).

## 4. Theorem T3: the mirror invariant κ (proved here, by hand)

Let z := [a,b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹. Facts: (F1) γ₂/γ₃ ≅ ℤ is generated by z and is central in F/γ₃; an automorphism with matrix M acts on it by det M, so φ(z) ≡ z⁻¹ and ι(z) ≡ z (mod γ₃). (F2) I − A = [[1,−1],[−1,2]] is invertible over ℤ.

**Lemma 4.1.** Every u ∈ F is φ-twisted conjugate modulo γ₃ to z^k for some k ∈ ℤ, and z^k, z^{k′} are twisted conjugate modulo γ₃ iff k ≡ k′ (mod 2). Hence κ(u) := k mod 2 is a well-defined, complete invariant of φ-twisted conjugacy in F/γ₃, and κ(g) := κ(u) is an invariant of the conjugacy class of g = (u,1) in G_c.

*Proof.* Choose v ∈ F with (I − A)v̄ = −ū (F2); then v u φ(v)⁻¹ ∈ γ₂, so it is ≡ z^k. If v z^k φ(v)⁻¹ ≡ z^{k′}, abelianizing gives (I − A)v̄ = 0, so v ≡ z^m (mod γ₃) and v z^k φ(v)⁻¹ ≡ z^{m+k+m} by (F1). Conversely z^m realizes every shift by 2m. ∎

(κ factors through a finite quotient of G_c: it is already a complete invariant of twisted conjugacy in the group F/γ₃F⁴ of order 32 (a^i b^j z^k with i, j mod 4, k mod 2), by the same proof. This is a finite 2-group-by-cyclic quotient; such quotients carry no AC invariant (B, E, H) but they do separate conjugacy classes of G_c.)

**Theorem T3.** For every degree-1 element g of G_c, κ(θ₀(g)) = κ(g) + 1. Consequently θ₀(g) is not conjugate to g, and not to g⁻¹ (degree −1). In particular, for all n, s ∈ ℤ: θ₀(k_n) ≁ k_n^{±1} and xˢy ≁ x^{1−s}y in G_c; no conjugacy class of degree-1 elements is θ₀-invariant.

*Proof.* Let g = (u,1) and v u φ(v)⁻¹ ≡ z^k (mod γ₃). θ₀* is an automorphism of G_c preserving F and γ₃, so it maps the conjugate elements (u,1), (z^k,1) of G_c/γ₃ to conjugate elements: θ₀(g) = (ι(u)b, 1) ~ (ι(z)^k b, 1) = (z^k b, 1) modulo γ₃ (T1(a), F1). If w b φ(w)⁻¹ ≡ z^{k₀}, then w z^k b φ(w)⁻¹ ≡ z^{k+k₀} because z is central modulo γ₃; so κ(θ₀(g)) = κ(g) + κ(b). Finally κ(b) = 1: take w = a⁻¹b⁻¹; φ(w) = b⁻¹(b⁻¹a)⁻¹ = b⁻¹a⁻¹b, so w b φ(w)⁻¹ = a⁻¹b⁻¹·b·b⁻¹ab = a⁻¹b⁻¹ab = [a⁻¹,b⁻¹] ≡ z (mod γ₃), the commutator being bilinear modulo γ₃. ∎

**Values (by hand for s ≥ 0).** In F/γ₃ write elements as a^i b^j z^k with b^j a^i = a^i b^j z^{−ij}. For u = aˢ take v = a^{−2s}b^{−s} ((I−A)v̄ = (−s,0)). Then φ(v)⁻¹ = (b⁻¹a)ˢ b^{2s}, (b⁻¹a)ˢ = (ab⁻¹z)ˢ = aˢb⁻ˢ z^{s(s+1)/2}, and v aˢ φ(v)⁻¹ = a^{−2s}b⁻ˢ a^{2s} b^{s} z^{s(s+1)/2} = z^{2s² + s(s+1)/2}. So **κ(xˢy) = C(s+1,2) mod 2**: κ = 0 for s ≡ 0, 3 and κ = 1 for s ≡ 1, 2 (mod 4) — consistent with O4 (y ~ x⁻¹y: κ = 0; xy ~ x²y: κ = 1) and with s ↦ 1−s flipping κ. Likewise κ(k_n) = C(n+1,2) mod 2 (script, all n by Lemma 5.3): κ(k_3) = 0, κ(θ₀k_3) = 1.

**Corollary 4.2 (answer to task (2) of the brief; TH03's open question).** θ₀(k_n) is a genuinely new killer class of G_c for every n ≥ 1: (c, k_n) and (c, θ₀(k_n)) = P_n′ are never one c-frozen ACM move apart. In TH03's census of c-frozen neighbours of P₃ (their T14), the 11 words "in the mirror class" are therefore *not* neighbours of P₃.

## 5. Theorem T4: the degree-4 integer invariant Ĵ (proved here; closed forms by exact computation)

Facts (free Lie ring, GL₂-equivariance: γ₃/γ₄ ≅ det⊗V, γ₄/γ₅ ≅ det⊗Sym²V with V = F^{ab}): (F3) L₃ := γ₃/γ₄ ≅ ℤ² with basis [z,a], [z,b]; φ acts by −A, and I + A = [[1,1],[1,0]] is invertible over ℤ. (F4) L₄ := γ₄/γ₅ ≅ ℤ³ with basis e₁ = [[z,a],a], e₂ = [[z,a],b], e₃ = [[z,b],b] ↔ a², ab, b²; φ acts by Φ₄ = −Sym²(A), with columns (0,0,−1), (0,−1,1), (−1,2,−1); the form ℓ(c₁,c₂,c₃) := 2c₁ − c₂ − 2c₃ satisfies ℓ∘Φ₄ = ℓ, and ℓ induces an isomorphism L₄/(I − Φ₄)L₄ ≅ ℤ (the image of I − Φ₄ = [[1,0,1],[0,2,−2],[1,−1,2]] has rank 2, lies in ker ℓ, and its 2×2 minors have gcd 1). ι acts trivially on L₄ ((−1)² from Sym², +1 from det). All four facts are re-derived by the script in the truncated Magnus algebra ℤ⟨X,Y⟩/(degree ≥ 5) (`th11_nil.py`: `PHI4`, `ELL`).

The eigenvalue 1 of φ on γ₄/γ₅ is what makes an integer invariant appear: 1 is an eigenvalue of φ on γ_k/γ_{k+1} only when 4 | k (the eigenvalues are ±λ^i μ^{k−i} with λμ = −1).

**Theorem 5.1.** Every u ∈ F is φ-twisted conjugate modulo γ₅ to z^κ·w with κ ∈ {0,1} and w ∈ γ₄. The pair (κ, J(u)), J(u) := ℓ(w mod γ₅) ∈ ℤ, depends only on the twisted class of u modulo γ₅, and is a complete invariant of φ-twisted conjugacy in F/γ₅. Hence κ and **Ĵ := J − κ** are invariants of conjugacy classes of degree-1 elements of G_c.

*Proof.* Existence: Lemma 4.1 gives a twisted conjugate ≡ z^κ (mod γ₃) (use z^m to reduce k modulo 2); then twisted conjugation by v ∈ γ₃ changes the L₃-component by (I + A)v̄ (v is central modulo γ₄), so by (F3) we reach z^κ w with w ∈ γ₄. Uniqueness: let g z^κ w φ(g)⁻¹ ≡ z^{κ′} w′ (mod γ₅). Then (I − A)ḡ = 0, so g ∈ γ₂, g ≡ z^m (mod γ₃), κ′ = κ + 2m, so m = 0 and g ∈ γ₃; modulo γ₄, (I + A)ḡ = 0, so g ∈ γ₄, which is central modulo γ₅; hence w′ − w = (I − Φ₄)g and ℓ(w′) = ℓ(w). Completeness: conversely, if ℓ(w′) = ℓ(w) then w′ − w ∈ (I − Φ₄)L₄ by (F4). ∎

**Proposition 5.2.** Ĵ(θ₀(g)) = Ĵ(g) for every degree-1 g (while κ flips).

*Proof.* As in T3: if g ~ (z^κ w, 1) modulo γ₅, then θ₀(g) ~ (ι(z)^κ ι(w) b, 1) = (w·ι(z)^κ b, 1), and since w is central modulo γ₅, J(θ₀ g) = ℓ(w) + J(ι(z)^κ b). The two constants are J(b) = 1 and J(ι(z)b) = J(a⁻¹b⁻¹ab·b) = −1 (script), so Ĵ(θ₀g) − Ĵ(g) = J(ι(z)^κ b) − 1 + 2κ = 0 in both cases. ∎

**Lemma 5.3 (polynomiality).** Fix w ∈ F. On each residue class of n modulo 4, κ(aⁿw) is constant and J(aⁿw) is a polynomial in n of degree ≤ 4.

*Proof.* Call a family of truncated Magnus series S(n) = Σ_m S_m(n)·m *weighted* if each coefficient S_m is a polynomial in n of degree ≤ deg m. Weighted families (with constant term 1) are closed under product, inversion and the substitution induced by φ (which sends a monomial of degree d to a constant combination of monomials of degree ≥ d); and if B = 1 + Q is a *fixed* series with Q of order ≥ d and e(n) is a polynomial of degree ≤ d, then B^{e(n)} = Σ_j C(e(n), j) Q^j is weighted (C(e(n), j) has degree ≤ dj, and Q^j has order ≥ dj). μ(aⁿw) = (1+X)ⁿμ(w) is weighted. The reduction of Theorem 5.1 uses the conjugators a^{e₁}b^{e₂} (e_i linear in n; d = 1), z^{−(k−κ)/2} (k = coefficient of XY, of degree ≤ 2; d = 2) and [z,a]^{e₃}[z,b]^{e₄} (e_i = integer combinations of degree-3 coefficients; d = 3). k(n) is an integer-valued polynomial of degree ≤ 2, so k mod 2 has period 4 and (k−κ)/2 is a polynomial on each class. Hence the final series is weighted on each class, and J is an integer combination of its degree-4 coefficients. ∎

**Theorem 5.4 (closed forms).** For all n, s ∈ ℤ:

## 6. Applications (proved here)

### 6.1 Theorem T5: the primitive classes

**Theorem T5.** xˢy ~ x^{s′}y in G_c iff s = s′, or {s,s′} = {−1,0}, or {s,s′} = {1,2}.

*Proof.* "If" is O3/O4 (y ~ x⁻¹y, xy ~ x²y). "Only if": with m = s² − s, 24·C(s+1,4) = m(m−2), so Ĵ(xˢy) = Ĵ(x^{s′}y) iff m′ = m or m + m′ = 2. The values of m are 0, 2, 6, 12, …, so m + m′ = 2 forces {m,m′} = {0,2}, i.e. s, s′ ∈ {−1,0,1,2}; and m′ = m forces s′ ∈ {s, 1−s}. In the second case κ differs (T3) unless s′ = s. Among s ∈ {−1,0,1,2}, κ = 0,0,1,1. ∎

So the one-parameter family of primitive exits (c, xˢy) of Prop. O2 consists of pairwise distinct killer classes apart from the two cusp classes.

### 6.2 Theorem T6: second proof of the uniform O6

**Lemma 6.1.** For n, s ∈ ℤ put G := n² − 5n − 7 and W := s² − s − 1. Then Ĵ(k_n) = Ĵ(xˢy) iff W² = G² − 48(n+1). The integer solutions are exactly: n ∈ {−1, 0} with s ∈ {−1,0,1,2}; n ∈ {1, 2} with s ∈ {−2, 3}; n = 9 with s ∈ {−4, 5}.

*Proof.* 24Ĵ(xˢy) = −(s²−s)(s²−s−2) = 1 − W², and n⁴ − 10n³ + 11n² + 22n = G² − 48n − 49, so 24Ĵ(k_n) = −G² + 48n + 49; equality means W² = G² − 48(n+1). G and W are odd. If n ≠ −1 then |G| ≠ |W|, so ||G| − |W|| ≥ 2 and 48|n+1| = |G² − W²| ≥ 4|G| − 4. But 4G − 4 − 48(n+1) = 4(n² − 17n − 20) > 0 for n ≥ 19, and 4G − 4 + 48(n+1) = 4(n² + 7n + 4) > 0 for n ≤ −7. So −6 ≤ n ≤ 18, and the 25 cases are checked directly (G² − 48(n+1) is negative for 3 ≤ n ≤ 8; it is a square only for n = −6 (61², but s² − s − 1 = 61 has no integer root), −1, 0 (1), 1, 2 (25) and 9 (19²)); `th11_nil.py` repeats the enumeration. ∎

### 6.3 Theorem T7: relations among the k_n and their mirrors

## 7. Task (3): the trace coincidences at n = 4, 5 (identity proved by exact computation; source open)

Let N(g) = tr ρ(g²) as in §6.2 (a rational integer for all elements below). Exact values (`th11_trace.py`): N(k_n) = 2, 2, 8, 8, 20, 74, 218, 632, 1880, 5588, 16562 for n = −1, …, 9 and N(xˢy) = 2, 2, 2, 2, 8, 26, 74, 218, 650, 1928, 5714 for s = −1, …, 9, with N(xˢy) = N(x^{1−s}y). The coordinator's coincidences are N(k_4) = N(x⁵y) = N(x⁻⁴y) = 74 and N(k_5) = N(x⁶y) = N(x⁻⁵y) = 218 (and N(k_1) = N(k_2) = N(x⁻²y) = N(x³y) = 8, which are genuine conjugacies or their mirrors).

**Proposition T8 (reflection identity).** For all n ∈ ℤ: N(k_n) + N(k_{4−n}) = N(x^{n+1}y) + N(x^{n−4}y).

*Proof.* k_n² = aⁿ·b⁻¹a⁻¹·bⁿ·a⁻¹·y² and (xˢy)² = aˢbˢy² (O §6.3), so both N(k_n) and N(xⁿy) are of the form tr(AⁿC₁BⁿC₂) with fixed C₁, C₂. A and B are diagonalizable with eigenvalues λ^{±1}, λ′^{±1} (tr A = 1−ω, tr B = 2+ω ≠ ±2), so every such sequence is a combination of the four exponentials (λ^{±1}λ′^{±1})ⁿ. This 4-dimensional space is invariant under shifts and, because the set of four bases is closed under inversion, under n ↦ −n. Hence the difference of the two sides lies in it, and it vanishes for the 23 consecutive values n = −9, …, 13 (exact computation), so it vanishes identically. ∎

*Explanation of the coincidences.* At n = 4 the identity reads N(k_4) − N(x⁵y) = N(y) − N(k_0), and k_0 = YXyy is a conjugate of x⁻¹y ~ y; at n = 5 it reads N(k_5) − N(x⁶y) = N(xy) − N(k_{−1}), and k_{−1} = (a⁻¹b⁻¹a⁻¹, 1) is twisted-conjugate to the class {x⁻¹y, y} (explicit conjugator a·b·a·b·a applied letter by letter; (κ,Ĵ) = (0,0)), all of these being parabolic with N = 2. At n = 2 it gives 2N(k_2) = 2N(x³y). So **the n = 4, 5 coincidences are the reflections n ↦ 4 − n of the peripheral conjugacies of k_0 and k_{−1}**, and the difference N(k_n) − N(x^{n+1}y) (= 6, 0, −6, 0, 0, −18, −48, −126, … for n = 1, 2, 3, …) is odd under n ↦ 4 − n. No further zero occurs for |n| ≤ 13 (TH03 proves there is none at all).

*What is and is not explained.* The reflection is not induced by an automorphism of G_c (Out(G_c) = ⟨θ₀⟩ and θ₀ preserves each N-value), and it does not map conjugacy classes to conjugacy classes: Ĵ(k_4) = 5 ≠ −15 = Ĵ(x⁵y), Ĵ(k_5) = 10 ≠ −35 = Ĵ(x⁶y). So the coincidences yield **no exact relation usable for AK(4) or AK(5)**: by T6 neither P_4 nor P_5 has a one-move primitive exit, and nothing here produces a two-move exit. Whether the identity comes from a symmetry of the figure-eight complement that does not descend to the Gieseking manifold, from a hidden symmetry (the figure-eight complement is arithmetic), or from a 2×2 Hermitian-form accident, I could not decide: the identity is equivalent to one equation between two explicit Hermitian forms on ℂ² (those giving N(k_n) and N(xⁿy) in terms of Aⁿ = pA + q), and I did not find a structural reason for it. *Open.*
