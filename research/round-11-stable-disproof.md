# Activated intervals in the one-padding budget-20 branch

2026-11-19. At stable budget 20, a shortest candidate can use at most two added pairs in degree ten and in the free group. A shortest one-padding candidate would have an 18-instruction degree-nine retraction with at least one interval of three nonidentity rows. Every such interval needs at least four instructions from entry to return. If it is the only interval, it needs five. Separately, erasing the added variable while retaining the original degree forces some interval of length at least six at every budget in the free group and in Magnus degree at least eight. This holds for any balanced normally generating rank-two tuple. ## Retraction and marked coefficients

Normalize a shortest candidate and absorb swaps into the row labels. In the case of one added variable $z$, erase $z$ and truncate to degree nine. Write $I$ for an unordered vertex with an identity row and call consecutive instructions from $I$ back to $I$ an excursion if every interior vertex has three nonidentity rows. There is at least one excursion, because a shortest stable path must import an original row into the spare at some crossing. The first import into an identity spare has a nonidentity original donor. The round-ten saturation shows that every retracted path of at most 17 instructions consists of vertices with an identity row. A projected wait could be deleted, so the candidate at budget 20 has exactly 18 effective instructions in this retraction. [Round-ten saturation and directional cut](round-10-stable-disproof.md)

Keep the full mod-two exponent vectors of the same labelled rows before erasure. They always form a basis of $\mathbb F_2^3$. At an idle vertex $I$ the identity row projects to the zero vector and has $z$ coefficient one. The other two rows have independent projected exponent vectors. Along effective instructions staying in the idle sector, the identity row is unchanged, and inversion, conjugation and products between the active pair act invertibly on their vector of two $z$ coefficients. A product using the identity as donor would be a projected wait. In particular an idle section continuing from the start or reaching the terminal basis has zero $z$ coefficients on both active rows.

## Two- and three-instruction excursions

The first instruction of an excursion is a right product importing a signed copy of one active row $a$ into the identity. The resulting projected tuple is $(a,b,a^\epsilon)$, up to row order, where $a,b$ have independent projected mod-two exponent vectors and $\epsilon=\pm1$. Any return to the idle sector must multiply a row by a signed donor to kill it, so those two rows are equal up to inversion immediately before the return.

A two-instruction excursion returns to the pair $(a,b)$ or to $(a^{-1},b)$, with no regard to their order. A three-instruction excursion has just one internal step. If it changes $b$ by inversion, conjugation or a product by a copy of $a$, its new exponent vector is independent of that of the two copies of $a$. The final cancellation can only discard one of those copies. The resulting pair is obtainable by that same change to $b$ and possibly inversion of $a$. If the internal step inverts or conjugates one copy of $a$, the last cancellation forces it still to equal the other copy up to inversion. The pair then only needs a possible inversion of $a$. If the step multiplies a copy of $a$ by $b^{\pm1}$, the three projected exponent vectors are nonzero and pairwise distinct. If it multiplies one copy by the other, the receiver has zero projected exponent vector and is nonidentity at this interior tick. In either case no two nonidentity rows have the same projected exponent vector, so the final cancellation is impossible.

Thus the two- and three-instruction forms have idle replacements of at most one and two instructions respectively. Replacing either excursion leaves a retracted path of at most 17 steps, and the saturation certifies that all its vertices are idle. There was exactly one excursion in the original path. The shortening saves exactly one move, since a retracted path of at most 16 moves is impossible. Both outside idle sections connect to a marked endpoint and therefore have zero $z$ coefficients on their active rows.

The imported copy of $a$ carries $z$ coefficient one, while the original copy carries zero. In the two-instruction form, deleting the imported copy leaves the original pair unchanged and would save two moves. If the original copy is deleted, the remaining $a$ row carries $z$ coefficient one. In the three-instruction form, the internal step must change $b$. A step modifying a copy of $a$ would need only one replacement move and save at least two. When $b$ changes, deleting the imported copy leaves one ordinary change to $b$ and again saves at least two. Deleting the original retains the row with $z$ coefficient one. The final idle section cannot remove a nonzero vector of active $z$ coefficients. This excludes both forms.

Independently, a complete local enumeration finds nine possible windows of length two and six of length three over the 53-vertex saturated degree-nine geodesic corridor. For each window it lifts every allowed instruction simultaneously in the degree-nine rank-two quotient and the rank-three degree-one quotient, keeping their row alignment. No window reaches the joint endpoint. Degrees two through four reproduce the exclusion. [Window enumerator and joint lift](../experiments/round11_stable_disproof_short_excursions.py)

## A long excursion at any budget

Every four-instruction excursion can be replaced by at most four idle rank-two instructions. At its first active vertex, use formal letters $a,b$ for the two incoming active rows and the tuple $(a,b,a^\epsilon)$ for the two rows and the imported copy. Use two more independent formal letters $x,y$ as conjugators. Enumerate two internal ordinary instructions in this formal free group. If an internal row is formally empty, it must also be empty after evaluation, so that intermediate tuple cannot be part of an excursion. In all other cases, the final cancellation forces equality of the formal $a,b$ parity vectors of its recipient and donor, since the original active rows have independent mod-two exponent vectors. The $x,y$ counts in these symbolic rows stay zero in abelianization because they enter only by conjugation.

There are 1,296 distinct triples of removed formal row, signed cancelling donor and surviving unordered pair passing those conditions. Starting from the formal pair $(a,b)$, a complete four-instruction ball has 14,284 unordered pairs. Only eight triples have a surviving pair outside the ball. In those eight, replace the retained signed donor by the removed row, which equals it by the hypothesized last cancellation. This leaves a pair of formal words attained within two rank-two moves. The finite replay imposes no other equality on the actual words. [Symbolic enumerator](../experiments/round11_stable_disproof_symbolic4.py) · [All exceptional triples](../results/round11_stable_disproof_symbolic4.json)

Consider a shortest stable path to the unordered positive basis in the free group or a characteristic-two Magnus quotient of degree at least one, using one added pair. Erase the added variable at the original degree, or erase it in the free group. An idle vertex has exactly one identity row, because the remaining projected degree-one vectors must have rank two. If all excursions had length at most four, replace them with the idle paths just proved and remove the resulting identity slot. This gives an ordinary path using at most as many moves as the ordinary section of the stable path. It saves the insertion and deletion, contradicting shortestness. Hence this same-degree erasure has at least one excursion of length at least five, with no restriction on the total instruction budget. For the further truncation from degree ten to degree nine, use the separate budget-20 conditions above and below.

For a five-instruction excursion the symbolic ball contains 129,940 pairs, and there are 19,766 candidate terminal triples after checking three internal moves. Substituting the removed word for the retained cancelling donor covers 997 additional triples. The remaining 47 have impossible cancellation equalities in the free group or in degree at least eight. Of these, 45 have different $a,b$ exponent vectors modulo 16. The actual exponent vectors of $a,b$ form an invertible matrix modulo 16 since they are independent modulo two. Erasing one variable at a time gives the required exponent readings in the cyclic degree-eight quotients, each of order 16. The last two triples both require $ab^{-1}a^{-1}=b^{-1}$. The independent linear parts $A,B$ force a nonzero homogeneous degree-two commutator part $AB+BA$, excluding that equality. Thus every excursion of at most five instructions has a replacement of no greater length in these groups. The same shortestness argument raises the unrestricted bound to at least six. ## An isolated four-instruction excursion at budget 20

Assume there is just one excursion and that it has four instructions. As above, at entry and after return the active $z$ coefficients are zero. Denote the physical row originally containing the spare by $j$. Immediately after the birth product the support of the $z$ coefficient vector is $\{j\}$. Immediately before return it is a singleton at the row about to be killed. Indeed all other rows survive unchanged with zero $z$ coefficient, and the returning identity must have coefficient one while its donor has zero.

There are two interior steps. A product whose donor has $z$ coefficient one toggles the coefficient of its recipient. Other instructions preserve the support. To go from a singleton to a singleton, either neither interior step toggles or both do.

If neither toggles, the spare stays in row $j$ and never changes a surviving row as donor. After erasing $j$, its own updates disappear and the other two rows can be obtained with at most the two interior moves. If both toggle the same other row $i$, their donor is still row $j$, whose projection remains $a^\epsilon$. For $i=b$, the same two right multiplications act on $b$ at rank two. For $i=a$, the row after the two products is one of $a^{-1},a,a^3$. The power $a^3$ cannot kill the remaining spare, since killing it requires a donor equal to $a^{\pm1}$. The element $a$ has order at least eight in the degree-nine quotient. Erasing the other marked variable gives an odd power of one generator in the rank-one degree-nine quotient, where that generator has order 16. Thus the surviving change to $a$ costs at most an inversion. In all these cases the idle replacement saves at least two moves.

The remaining support transition transfers the singleton from $j$ to $i$ by two products, first from $j$ to $i$, then from $i$ back to $j$. The final return would have to kill $i$. If $i$ was the original copy of $a$, its projected exponent vector is zero after these products and the two other projected vectors are nonzero. If $i$ was the row of $b$, its projected vector is $a+b$ in additive mod-two notation, while the other vectors are $a$ and $b$. In either case no donor can kill $i$. This excludes a single four-instruction excursion. Several longer excursions can transfer and later clear the $z$ coefficients, so the argument does not exclude a four-instruction excursion among other excursions.

## Erasing an identity in a larger projected tuple

In any retracted ordinary graph with extra identity rows, delete one identity from each unordered vertex containing one. Along any edge with an identity at both ends, the two vertices have a common unchanged identity slot, since an ordinary instruction changes at most one slot after row swaps are absorbed. Deleting this slot gives an edge or a wait in the smaller graph. Hence a path confined to vertices with an identity projects to a path of no greater length with one fewer slot, even when no particular physical identity slot persists throughout.

The earlier three-slot degree-nine retraction excludes length 16, so the two-padding branch at stable budget 20 must visit a degree-nine retracted vertex with all four rows nonidentity. Entering that region imports a signed duplicate, and the exit requires a signed duplicate for cancellation. This gives an exact screening condition for the remaining two-padding branch. ## Complete three-padding retraction

The five-slot degree-eight erasure at middle budget 14 has disjoint balls at radii 7 and 7. The boundary-only run retains 15,631,448 and 74,695,954 tuples and checks 661,103,565 final transitions. This excludes the three-pair branch of stable budget 20 by projection. Together with the previous eliminations of four and five pairs and the two-tree bound for higher padding, it leaves only $k=0,1,2$. The unrestricted degree-ten ordinary search at budget 20 is still in progress.
