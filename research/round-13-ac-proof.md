# The Wirtinger conjugacy block and exceptional Magnus intersections

2026-11-19. The same-row Wirtinger repair with an outer-compatible middle relator of exponent vector $(0,-1)$ has no solution of Schreier width zero or one. At width at least two it requires an exceptional Magnus intersection. Width two with a singleton extreme has an additional determinant obstruction, including every case in which both extremes occur once. An explicit middle relator in $A[N_B,N_C]$ passes the two outer blocks and has a verified small-cancellation obstruction to the middle block. [Round 11 outer conditions](round-11-ac-proof.md) · [Exact word and strict replay receipts](../results/round13_ac_proof_wirtinger_hnn.json)

## Conjugacy gives a base-group equation

Let $(A,B)$ be the wrong Wirtinger pair and $(A,C)$ the corrected pair. Write $N_h$ for the normal closure of the middle relator in $F(x,y)$. The middle block requires $B$ and $C$ to be conjugate up to inversion in $F/N_h$. Here $h$ has exponent vector $(0,-1)$, as happens for every $h\in A[N_B,N_C]$. The height homomorphism $x\mapsto1$, $y\mapsto0$ descends through the quotient. Both donors have height $-1$, which fixes the sign in the conjugacy test.

Write $y_j=x^j y x^{-j}$. In the kernel of height on $F(x,y)$ these form a free basis. Cyclically reduce the Schreier relator for $h$ in that basis and translate its indices so the two extremes are $-2$ and $m-2$, where $m$ is its width. These operations conjugate $h$ in $F(x,y)$ and preserve the quotient. Direct rewriting, with $a=y_{-2}$ and $b=y_{-1}$, gives

$$
B=Px^{-1},\qquad C=Qx^{-1},\qquad
P=a^{-1}bab^{-2},\qquad Q=a^{-2}ba^2b^{-2}.
$$

For $m\ge1$ the quotient is the Magnus HNN extension with base

$$
H=\langle y_{-2},\ldots,y_{m-2}\mid h\rangle
$$

and associated subgroups $L=\langle y_{-2},\ldots,y_{m-3}\rangle$ and $M=\langle y_{-1},\ldots,y_{m-2}\rangle$. Both embed freely by the Freiheitssatz. Conjugation by $x^{-1}$ maps $M$ to $L$ by the index shift $\sigma(y_j)=y_{j-1}$. [Magnus, Freiheitssatz](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1930.163.141) · [Britton, The Word Problem](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970200)

If $gC=Bg$, multiplying $g$ on the right by a power of $C$ makes its height zero while preserving the equation. In the Bass–Serre tree, $B$ and $C$ each move the base vertex by one edge. They act as translations because their heights are nonzero. Thus their axes contain the base vertex and the iterates of that vertex give exactly one axis vertex at each integer height. A height-zero conjugator sends the base vertex on one axis to the base vertex on the other. Consequently $g\in H$. The equation then gives $x^{-1}gx=P^{-1}gQ\in H$. Britton's lemma forces $g\in M$ and

$$
gQ=P\sigma(g)\qquad\text{in }H.
$$

Conversely, such a $g\in M$ gives the desired conjugacy. The reduction places no bound on the original conjugator.

## Width and intersection obstructions

For $m=1$, $M$ is generated by $b$, so $g=b^n$. The base-group abelianization of $b^nQ=Pa^n$ gives $n([b]-[a])\in\mathbb Z[h]$. The sum of the two coordinates on the left is zero, whereas the sum for $[h]$ is $-1$. Therefore $n=0$. The middle block now requires $B=C$ in $F/N_h$. The width-one equality classification and outer permutation separator of Round 11 exclude this case. At width zero the relator is conjugate to $y^{-1}$ and the same outer separator applies. [Equality classification and separator](round-11-ac-proof.md#equality-in-the-middle-quotient)

For $m\ge2$, both $P$ and $Q$ lie in $L$. Thus $g=P\sigma(g)Q^{-1}\in L\cap M$. If the Magnus intersection is the ordinary overlap $\langle y_{-1},\ldots,y_{m-3}\rangle$, the equation holds in the free group $L$. Suppose the largest index $j$ in the reduced word for $g$ is at least zero. The right side $P\sigma(g)Q^{-1}$ uses only indices at most $\max(-1,j-1)$, a contradiction. Hence $g=b^n$. In the free group $L$, abelianization gives $n=0$, and $P\ne Q$. Every repair of width at least two therefore requires an exceptional intersection in the sense of Collins and Howie. [Collins, Intersections of Magnus subgroups](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/groups/intersections-of-magnus-subgroups-of-onerelator-groups/E31A6BB6F8B5A051D8D1DF60C923B168)

Even in the exceptional case the two representatives of $g$ must have zero exponent vector in their respective Magnus bases. For the representative in $M$, let its vector in $\mathbb Z^{m+1}$ be $v$, extended by zero at the lower extreme. The equation gives $v-\sigma v\in\mathbb Z[h]$ in the abelianization of $H$. Again the total exponent sum forces the multiple of $[h]$ to vanish. Equality with a strictly shifted finite vector gives $v=0$. The lower representative $P\sigma(g)Q^{-1}$ then has vector zero as well.

## A width-two class covering exceptional intersections

Suppose $m=2$ and one extreme occurs exactly once with sign $\epsilon$. Translate so that deleting that extreme leaves the adjacent generators $a,b$ in the donor coefficients. Eliminate the singleton generator. The quotient is an ascending HNN extension of the free group on $a,b$, with a stable letter $t$ equal to $x$ or $x^{-1}$. The other Magnus subgroup gives the injective endomorphism $\theta$ induced by $t$. In the ascending order of the basis, the exponent matrix $T$ of $\theta$ is a two-dimensional companion matrix. If the other extreme has signed exponent $e$, then

$$
d=\det T=e/\epsilon,\qquad
\det(I-T)=-1/\epsilon .
$$

For example, when eliminating the upper extreme, the exponent vector of the relator is $(e,f,\epsilon)$ and the columns of $T$ are $(0,1)^T$ and $(-e/\epsilon,-f/\epsilon)^T$. Eliminating the lower extreme gives the same formula in reverse index order.

Let $K$ be the height kernel. Its abelianization is the direct limit of $\mathbb Z^2$ under $T$. Since $I-T$ is unimodular and commutes with the transition maps, $I-\sigma$ is invertible on this limit. The equation for $g$ and the equality of the exponent vectors of $P,Q$ force $g\in[K,K]$. In the class-two quotient of $K$, this makes $g$ central. The class of $PQ^{-1}$ must therefore lie in the image of $I-\sigma$ on $[K,K]/\gamma_3(K)$.

The ascending HNN kernel is the increasing union of the free bases conjugated by $t^{-n}$. On their class-two commutator groups the transition acts by multiplication by $d$. The commutator quotient of the union is their direct limit, since the commutators defining both lower central subgroups involve only finitely many elements at a time. For $d\ne0$ it is $\mathbb Z[1/d]$, and $\sigma$ acts by multiplication by $d$ or by $d^{-1}$. The word $PQ^{-1}=a^{-1}[b,a^{-1}]a$ represents a generator of the base commutator group modulo $\gamma_3$. Thus repair requires $1\in(1-d)\mathbb Z[1/d]$. For $d=1$ the right side is zero. For $d\ne1$ it contains $1$ exactly when $|1-d|=1$, because $1-d$ and $d$ are coprime. This excludes every $d\notin\{0,2\}$, including $d=\pm1$ when both extremes are singletons. The case $d=0$ has a zero commutator direct limit and the case $d=2$ passes this necessary test.

When $d=2$ and $\epsilon=-1$, a further necessary condition comes from degree three modulo five. If the singleton is upper, the shift satisfies $\theta(a)=b$ and $\theta(b)=a^{-2}b^2[a,b]^r$ modulo $\gamma_3(F(a,b))$ for some integer $r$. If it is lower, the corresponding formulas are $\theta(b)=a$ and $\theta(a)=a^2b^{-2}[a,b]^r$ modulo $\gamma_3(F(a,b))$. The determinant of $T$ is nonzero, so the base abelianization injects into the kernel abelianization. The axis argument puts $g$ in the base and hence in its commutator group. Modulo class four it has the form $[a,b]^i[[a,b],a]^j[[a,b],b]^k$. The degree-three Magnus expansion of this word after substitution depends on the images of $a,b$ only through degree two, since the expansion of $g$ has zero degree-one part.

In the upper case apply $\theta$ to the equation to obtain $\theta(g)\theta(Q)=\theta(P)g$. In the lower case the equation is $gQ=P\theta(g)$. The degree-three Magnus algebra modulo five has exponent five on series with constant term one. The replay covers all 125 triples $(i,j,k)$ modulo five for every residue of $r$. The counts in both cases are $(0,5,0,0,0)$ in residue order. Thus $r\equiv1\pmod5$ is required. [Degree-three residues](../results/round13_ac_proof_wirtinger_hnn.json)

## A directly checkable nonexceptional class

There is a sufficient free-word test for an ordinary Magnus intersection. Assume the symmetrized Schreier relator satisfies $C'(1/6)$. In every cyclic window of length $\lfloor |h|/2\rfloor+1$ of the Schreier relator or its inverse, delete all letters except those on the two extreme indices and ignore their signs. Suppose the resulting sequence makes at least three changes between extremes in every window.

Let $u\in L$ and $v\in M$ represent the same element of $H$. Free and cyclic reduction of $uv^{-1}$ keeps its boundary a concatenation of at most two intervals, one with each extreme missing. Any cyclic interval of that boundary has at most two changes between extremes. If a nonempty freely and cyclically reduced boundary represented the identity in $H$, Greendlinger's lemma would give a subword of a cyclic relator longer than half a relator on the boundary. The required third change forbids it. Therefore $u=v$ already in the ambient free group on the indexed generators, and their common word belongs to the ordinary overlap. [Greendlinger, Dehn's algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160130108)

The replay constructs an explicit $h=A\prod_{i=1}^5[U_i,V_i]$, with $U_i$ a conjugate of $B$ and $V_i$ a conjugate of $C$. It expands both outer conversions into 1,284 and 1,260 strict ordinary instructions, respectively. The cyclically reduced Schreier relator has width four and 669 letters. Its longest piece has length 33. The shortest number of changes between extremes over all 1,338 oriented cyclic windows of length 335 is four. These verify the sufficient test and exclude all middle conjugators for this explicit outer-compatible relator. The proof leaves exceptional intersections at larger widths and the remaining width-two classes open.
