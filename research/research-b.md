# Research B: quotient certificates and the finite-quotient distance principle

Date: 2026-11-02. Status: checked partial results; no ordinary AC trivialization or counterexample is claimed. These deductions are recorded without a novelty claim.

## Prior work read and independent direction

The target repository was empty at the beginning of the investigation; I then read its initial README and the primary work of Panteleev–Ushakov, Borovik–Lubotzky–Myasnikov, and Guyot listed below. I chose to audit quotient obstructions and construct explicit low-nilpotency certificates. This direction complements searches for free-group move sequences: it identifies which obstructions are already ruled out, while retaining a precise possible use for quotient distances.

Let F=<x,y>, r_n=x^n y^(-n-1), and s=xyx y^-1 x^-1 y^-1. The target is the ordered pair (r_n,s), with n=3 for AK(3). All paths below use relator inversion, right multiplication by the other relator, conjugation C_i(g): r_i -> g^-1 r_i g, and relator interchange. No generator changes or stabilization are used. Quotient equalities are explicitly identified.

## 1. Known theorem: component obstructions in finite and solvable quotients vanish

Borovik–Lubotzky–Myasnikov prove that, for a finite group G and k>=max(w(G),2), two normally generating k-tuples are AC-equivalent precisely when their abelianized tuples are equivalent; here w(G) is its minimum number of normal generators. Guyot proves the corresponding assertion for every finitely generated solvable group. These are existing results, not discoveries of this investigation. See [BLM, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1295) and [Guyot, Proposition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06912).

For any surjection pi:F -> G to one of these groups, the images of (r_n,s) normally generate G, since the AK(n) presentation is trivial. Their exponent-sum matrix is

    M_n = [[n, -n-1], [1, -1]],   det M_n = 1.

Integer row operations therefore take this pair in Z^2 to its standard basis, and the same operations work in G_ab. Also w(G)<=2 because G is generated by pi(x),pi(y). The hypotheses of the cited theorems apply. Hence

    (pi(r_n),pi(s)) ~AC (pi(x),pi(y)).

Consequently an AC-invariant of the tuple in one fixed finite or solvable quotient cannot distinguish AK(n) from the standard tuple. The same conclusion covers an invariant factoring through finitely many finite quotients simultaneously: replace them by the finite image in their product. It does not cover metric divergence across an unbounded family of quotients; see Section 4.

For completeness, the standard proof of group triviality is short. In the presented group, z=x^n=y^(n+1) is central, since it commutes with both generators. The braid relation implies conjugacy of x and y: Delta=xyx=yxy satisfies Delta x=y Delta. Modulo <z>, we have x^n=1 and y^(n+1)=1; conjugacy also gives x^(n+1)=1, so x=1 and y=1 there. Thus the presented group is generated by the central element z and is abelian. The braid relation then gives x=y; the power relation gives x=1. This verifies normal generation, not AC triviality.

## 2. Uniform explicit class-2 certificate

Write N_2=F/gamma_3(F), c=[x,y]=x^-1 y^-1 xy. Every element has normal form x^a y^b c^k. Its multiplication rule is

    (a,b,k)(d,e,l)=(a+d,b+e,k+l-bd).

In this quotient s=x y^-1 c^-1. Put A=n(n-1)/2. Direct multiplication gives

    r_n s^-n = y^-1 c^-A.

Apply these operations to (r_n,s):

1. Replace r_n by r_n s^-n. Literally: invert s, multiply the first coordinate by it n times, and invert s back.
2. Invert the first coordinate, obtaining y c^A in N_2.
3. Conjugate it by x^A, obtaining y, because x^-1 y x=y c^-1.
4. Multiply the second coordinate by the first, obtaining x c^-1.
5. Conjugate the second coordinate by y, obtaining x.
6. Interchange coordinates.

If conjugations are expanded into conjugations by single letters, this is n+A+6 literal elementary moves. For AK(3), n=A=3 and the count is 12. This is a proof for every n>=1 in N_2, not a finite-range inference.

The accompanying program replays these moves as exact free words, then evaluates them independently in integral class-2 coordinates. It checks n=1,...,30 as implementation checks. For AK(3), the final free words are

    (YxyxYXYXXyxYXYxyxYXYxyxYXyyyy,
     XXyxYXYxyxYXYxyxYXyyy),

where capitals denote inverse letters. These words are not x,y. Thus the quotient certificate is explicitly not a free-group trivialization.

## 3. Degree-3 errors and a 48-move class-3 correction

Use the Magnus expansion x->1+X, y->1+Y, x^-1->1-X+X^2-X^3+..., and similarly for y. Let

    Lx=[X,[X,Y]]=X^2Y-2XYX+YX^2,
    Ly=[Y,[Y,X]]=Y^2X-2YXY+XY^2.

If (a,b) is the free endpoint of the 12-move AK(3) path, the residuals a x^-1 and b y^-1 have no degree-1 or degree-2 terms and have degree-3 terms

    (-2 Lx + Ly, -Lx + Ly).

In F/gamma_4(F), these are central degree-3 errors. They can be removed by the following completely explicit operations. Here c is always the fixed ambient word x^-1 y^-1 xy, not a relator.

- Conjugation of a coordinate with exponent vector x by c adds Lx to its error; conjugation of one with exponent vector y by c adds -Ly.
- To add -Ly to the x coordinate while leaving the y coordinate unchanged: multiply it by the y coordinate, conjugate by c, multiply back by the inverse y coordinate, and conjugate by c^-1. The intermediate exponent vector is x+y, so the two conjugations contribute (Lx-Ly)-Lx=-Ly.
- The same four operations with coordinates interchanged add Lx to the y coordinate: (Lx-Ly)-(-Ly)=Lx.

Starting after the 12-move path, first conjugate the x coordinate by c^2, then perform the -Ly correction. Next conjugate the y coordinate by c, then perform the Lx correction. Expand conjugation by c into four generator conjugations; implement multiplication by an inverse donor by donor-inversion, multiplication, and donor-inversion. The correction uses 36 further literal moves, for 48 total. This proves that this particular endpoint is (x,y) in F/gamma_4(F).

The code verifies the Magnus coefficients through degree 3 exactly. The free endpoint has word lengths (93,193), and nonzero degree-4 residuals remain. For example the second residual has degree-4 part

    -3 X^2Y^2 + 6 XYXY - 6 YXYX + 3 Y^2X^2.

This documents the first failure of this specific class-3 certificate to continue to class 4; it is not an obstruction to some other class-4 path. Existing solvable-group results already ensure quotient paths at every fixed nilpotency class.

## 4. Exact finite-quotient distance principle

**Proposition.** Fix a finite instruction alphabet consisting of coordinate inversions and swaps, multiplication of one coordinate by another or its inverse, and conjugation of one coordinate by a fixed free generator or its inverse. Let d_F(U,V) be its graph distance, with infinity for disconnected tuples. For every finite quotient pi:F -> Q, use the same instruction alphabet with the generators replaced by their images. Then, in the extended nonnegative integers,

    d_F(U,V) = sup_{finite quotients pi} d_Q(pi(U),pi(V)).

**Proof.** A free-group path projects to every quotient, giving the >= inequality. Conversely, suppose no free path of length at most L exists. There are only finitely many instruction sequences p of length <=L. For each, p(U) differs from V in at least one coordinate. Residual finiteness of the free group gives a finite quotient in which that coordinate difference remains nontrivial. Take the product of these finitely many quotient maps and restrict its target to the finite image Q. Every possible instruction sequence of length <=L still fails in Q, so its quotient distance exceeds L. Since this holds for every L smaller than d_F(U,V), the reverse inequality follows. If d_F is infinite, it holds for every finite L. QED.

The proof works for any finitely generated residually finite ambient group and any such finite alphabet of quotient-compatible moves. The finite-alphabet requirement is essential to this argument: arbitrary-word conjugation counted as one move gives infinitely many candidate instructions in a bounded-length ball.

For AK(3), the known finitary theorem says every distance on the right is finite. Therefore the proposition says that **AK(3) fails ordinary AC triviality if and only if these finite-quotient distances are unbounded**. This is a reformulation, not evidence that they are unbounded. Guyot explicitly points out the possible role of unbounded quotient reduction complexity in the discussion following Theorem 1.3. The proposition spells out the residual-finiteness argument for the finite instruction metric.

As small reproducible checks, breadth-first search gives distances 2,3,4 in S3,S4,A5 respectively, with generating pairs

    S3: x=(12),  y=(123)
    S4: x=(12),  y=(1234)
    A5: x=(123), y=(12345).

These use exactly 15 instructions: two inversions, one interchange, four signed right multiplications, and eight generator conjugations. Group orders are verified by closure. These three numbers are modest lower bounds for the same free-group metric and establish no growth theorem.

## Reproduction and limits

Run `python3 experiments/quotient_certificates.py` from the repository root. The script has no external dependencies, records the literal paths, checks class-2 coordinates, computes exact truncated noncommutative Magnus expansions, and exhaustively computes the three finite quotient distances. Its assertions pass in the author's execution.

The concrete contributions of this note are explicit quotient certificates, exposed lifting errors, and a fully proved metric clarification. No claim here settles any of the four target problems. The next useful quotient direction would require a proved lower bound tending to infinity for a family of finite quotient metrics, rather than disconnected components in one quotient. Success at each fixed nilpotency class, by itself, does not supply a single finite sequence in F.
