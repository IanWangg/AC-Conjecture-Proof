## 1. Rotation reduction: the important cases are complete

For cyclically reduced nonempty u,v in F(x,y), Q/R assert that an ambient
primitive can occur in some independently conjugated subgroup if and
only if it occurs in some subgroup generated by two cyclic rotations.
The axes proof handles both possibilities for the actual two elements:

- If their axes intersect, choose a common **vertex** and conjugate both
  elements to it. Both become cyclically reduced readings of their own
  axes, hence rotations of the original words. The resulting subgroup is
  a global conjugate of a tested rotation subgroup.
- If the axes are disjoint, their shortest connecting path is off each
  axis at its endpoint. The two word circles joined by that path are
  already folded, with a genuine separating bridge. A cyclic primitive
  in rank two uses at most one sign of each generator, so cannot cross
  an edge in both directions. It must stay in one circle and read a
  power of u or v. A positive instance therefore requires u or v itself
  primitive, in which case the original unrotated subgroup is already
  a positive instance.

The proof does not silently assume the input factors are nonprimitive:
the disjoint-axis primitive-factor case is explicitly accounted for.
Proper powers cause no exception. For negative statements, the premise
that every rotation subgroup is primitive-free already implies both
individual factors are nonprimitive.

Trimming the conjugating prefix/suffix of an accepted primitive based
loop leaves a cyclic closed loop at some graph vertex. This follows
from uniqueness of inverse-labelled paths in a folded inverse graph,
so a primitive need not initially be cyclically reduced at the chosen
basepoint. Testing loops at every vertex is essential and is correctly
used. The one-sign primitive fact is specific to ambient rank two; no
extension of this argument to higher free rank is asserted.

Independent inversion of either subgroup generator changes no subgroup.
Thus rotations of u and v suffice; inverse rotations introduce no
missing independent-tail case.

## 2. R's positive Nielsen semigroup really is complete

Starting from a signed ambient basis (a,b), the updates

    (U,V) -> (U,UV),       (U,V) -> (UV,V)

are invertible basis operations and keep both words positive in a,b.
For every coprime positive count vector (A,B), repeatedly subtract the
smaller coefficient in the current positive unimodular cone from the
larger. The coefficient sum strictly decreases. Equality means both
coefficients are one, so the target is the next mediant column. This
proves that the count vector of every positive primitive class occurs
as a generated component. Zero-count cases are the initial letters.

The word associated with that component is primitive. Nielsen's rank-two
uniqueness theorem then makes every primitive cyclic word with the same
count vector a rotation of it. The four signed alphabets supply every
sign pattern. A primary proof of the uniqueness fact is
[Clay--Conant--Ramasubramanian, Corollary 4.4](https://msp.org/involve/2014/7-4/involve-v7-n4-p01-p.pdf).

If a word labels a closed walk at some vertex, every cyclic rotation
does as well, by changing the starting point along that walk. Therefore
the fixed-point tests for generated component words cover all primitive
cyclic representatives, not just one special Christoffel spelling.
Conversely, every tested component word is primitive, so an actual fixed
vertex provides a real positive witness rather than a pattern heuristic.

For a finite folded graph, compose partial maps in **word-reading
order**: T_U*T_V means follow U and then V. Equal ordered map pairs
have exactly the same two successors and the same fixed-point tests.
Memoizing them therefore loses no future test. Missing values remain
undefined under composition. The implementation's finite map-pair
closure is consequently an exact decision, provided exhaustion occurs;
an explicitly stopped run cannot be treated as negative.

## 3. Q's graph quotient and sufficient filter are sound

Q stores full adjacency encodings in its set, rather than only a
machine-sized hash. Hash collisions therefore cannot identify different
graphs. A connected rank-two core has either one degree-four branch
vertex or two degree-three branch vertices, because the sum of degree
minus two is two. Taking the least fixed-letter-order BFS encoding over
these branch vertices gives an exact unbased **label-preserving** key.
It is not an ambient generator automorphism.

Forgetting the basepoint is valid for primitive-loop existence: a path
from the original base to a primitive closed loop and its inverse gives
a conjugate primitive in the based subgroup. Q retains the original
base index, and R/S independently check the actual rotated words there.

The direct signed-DAG filter is only sufficient. A primitive cyclic word
in rank two has one signed alphabet and avoids at least one of its two
doubled-letter patterns. Its closed edge walk would give a directed
cycle in the corresponding filtered edge-state graph. Thus acyclicity
excludes it. Filter failures are correctly passed to R's complete
decision, never declared primitive merely because the filter failed.

## 4. Independent replay of every exceptional negative certificate

The new `s_review_rotation_maps.py` performs these checks afresh:

1. Decode and hash-check N's cap-22 source, Q's candidate file and R's
   split proof manifest, including every compressed part.
2. Check every candidate's source index and actual two independent
   rotations against N's source words.
3. Refold every exceptional rotated pair with M's independent graph
   routine, and compare it to Q's graph rebased at its recorded base.
4. Reconstruct R's pooled partial maps as integer tuples, using -1 for
   missing values rather than R's byte sentinel. Verify all four initial
   signed map pairs occur, every stored component has no fixed vertex,
   and both positive-Nielsen successors of every pair remain in its set.
5. Check that every candidate appears exactly once and that all counts
   and dataset hashes match.

The replay passes for all **6,568** exceptional graphs and **735,712**
closed map-pair states. The maximum is **76** states for one graph and
signed alphabet. No assertion of discovery-search completeness or a
stored ABSENT flag substitutes for the checked closure conditions.

## 5. A precise consequence for a possible completion

Combining Q's exhaustive rotation scan and R's verified negative sets,
every tuple whose cyclic state belongs to C22 generates a primitive-free
subgroup under **every** independent choice of its conjugating tails.
The new replay supports that combined statement; it does not expand it
to arbitrary later cyclic states.

One useful formulation is that **the last subgroup-changing conjugation
before the first primitive coordinate in any proposed AK(3) completion
must have its source cyclic state outside C22**. To see this, suppose
that source were in C22. Its independently conjugated target subgroup
would still be primitive-free. After the last subgroup-changing
conjugation, Nielsen operations and subgroup-preserving conjugations
keep the subgroup fixed, so could never produce a primitive coordinate.
This is a contradiction. The conjugation's target has the same cyclic
state as its source, so is outside C22 as well.

This is a membership statement about the checked cyclic component,
**not** a numerical lower bound on the energy of that particular
conjugation. Another low-energy component may be reached after a
higher-energy excursion. The established overall cyclic peak bound
remains 23. No reduction of arbitrary Nielsen excursions is assumed.

The combined method also gives a finite decision procedure for this
restricted question for any two nonempty cyclic words: can independent
conjugates generate a subgroup containing an ambient primitive? Test
their finitely many rotation pairs with R's finite-map closure. That
restricted decidability does not decide unrestricted AC triviality.
