A new rank-three waypoint projects intrinsically to an energy-27 ordered pair whose sharp ordinary entry peak is 36. Its strict rank-three prefix reaches the chart at cyclic peak 24. ## Cap-24 products and projections

The source was the four-million-state rank-three prefix from [Round 06](round-06-stable-proof.md), seeded by stabilization of the cap-22 rank-two component. Its native search tracks departures from the fixed \(z\) pivot. Filtering out that pivot and deduplicating by signed ambient permutations gives 1,749 sources, each represented by an actual state from the prefix. A cyclic product replaces one relator using a conjugate of another or its inverse. The generator enumerates both orientations and all cyclic start points in the two relators. If their axes in the free-group tree are disjoint with shortest connecting word of length \(d>0\), the product length is the sum of the relator lengths plus \(2d\). This gives the finite positive-bridge bound used in the scan. Intersecting axes are covered by the zero-bridge cases. Each output retains its actual predecessor. The layer counts can overlap because each is an adjacency image of the previous layer. A second check finds 961 projected pairs for which an unchanged pivot was already present in the actual predecessor and the predecessor in that same chart is one of the 310 proven ordinary projections from Round 07. This covers 635 additional pairs. The union covers 1,252 pairs before the separate energy-27 check below. ## Two-row blocks in the first layer

The 1,237 layer-one triples without a primitive row contain 2,210 distinct unordered two-row blocks. None presents an infinite cyclic quotient. The primitive abelian quotient determined by the two rows gives a cyclic cover. A Fox determinant obstruction excludes 2,180 blocks. A single cyclic product with bridge length at most one exposes a literal deletion chart in 26 of the remaining blocks. Each corresponding rank-two one-relator quotient has nonprimitive relator. If the quotient were infinite cyclic, a free basis for its abelian marking would put its kernel equal to the normal closure of a basis letter, and Magnus's normal-closure theorem would force the relator to be primitive. [Block audit](../experiments/round08_stable_proof_block_screen.py) · [All surviving Fox cases and witnesses](../results/round08_stable_proof_first_blocks.json)

In each of the last four cases, one relator is a word \(r(x,y)\) with primitive exponent vector. The other is a cyclic rotation of \(zuZv\), where \(Z=z^{-1}\), and both \(u,v\) are words in \(x,y\). The nonzero cyclic height detects one of \(u,v^{-1}\) in \(G=\langle x,y\mid r\rangle\), and an explicit permutation marking of \(G\) in \(S_3\) or \(S_4\) detects the height-zero word. The recorded markings verify \(r=1\) and display the nonidentity image. Since a primitive exponent vector prevents \(r\) from being a proper power, the one-relator torsion theorem makes both detected elements infinite order. The HNN embedding theorem embeds \(G\) in the two-relator quotient by identifying the infinite cyclic groups generated by \(u\) and \(v^{-1}\). The homomorphism of that quotient onto \(\mathbb Z\) by exponent sum of \(z\) has \(G\) in its kernel, so the quotient cannot be cyclic. [Karrass–Magnus–Solitar torsion theorem](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160130107) · [Higman–Neumann–Neumann embedding theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-24.4.247) · [Permutation witnesses](../results/round08_stable_proof_first_blocks.json)

## Completed native cap-22 enclosure

The exact native search allowing deletion of any isolated letter exhausted **246,052,265 signed rank-three non-singleton orbits** from 48,096 literal seeds (3,008 signed seed orbits). It tested 267,125,415,576 cyclic products, including 97,463,328 positive-bridge candidates.
