# Unbounded middle bridges with actual Nielsen bases

2026-11-19. The third-phase exclusion now allows arbitrarily long middle bridges for actual simultaneous Nielsen-basis choices. First and second intrinsic basis masses at most four and three, with intersecting first axes, give 317 certified second-lobe pairs. They give 174,001 marked rotation and binary-alphabet templates in which every admissible bridge in that alphabet of sufficient length is certified. A strict ordinary AK(3) path with both bases changed reaches an example whose middle bridge has unread gap 74. [Counts and exact entry replay](../results/round07_ac_disproof_long_bridges.json).

## The two-copy criterion for arbitrary second lobes

Let a rank-two source have disjoint axes and folded barbell core $\Gamma$. Let its two marked cyclically reduced lobe words be $P,Q$, and let its shortest connecting path read $q$. Write $L=\max(|P|,|Q|)$ and assume $|q|\ge L$. Suppose that every signed rotation of $P$ or $Q$ has exactly one reading in $\Gamma$, counting open paths as well as loops. Also suppose that the subgroup generated by any two independently based rotations of these lobe words is ambient-primitive-free, allowing two rotations of the same lobe.

Under these hypotheses every join of two copies of $\Gamma$ rebased at arbitrary core vertices is primitive-free. Indeed, the component of a distinct vertex pair in the self-fiber product avoids the diagonal, by reversibility of the immersed labelled paths. A reduced closed path in a barbell contains a complete lobe circuit. A cycle in the product component, or a path showing failure of injectivity of either projection, would therefore give a second reading of a signed lobe rotation. Thus the component is a tree and both projections embed it. The fold of the two rooted copies is the pushout along this common tree: equal outgoing labels at an identified vertex give an edge in the same fiber component, so every forced identification is already present.

If neither tree image contains its whole bridge, delete an unshared bridge edge in each copy. Each chosen edge is separating in the pushout, since the tree image lies on just one side. If a tree image contains the whole bridge, the corresponding path in the other copy must also use a bridge edge. Otherwise that path reads the length-$|q|$ word entirely along one lobe and exhibits a complete signed lobe rotation on the first copy's bridge. This would give a second reading. Delete an edge shared by the two bridges. It is separating because the common tree splits across that edge and glues each side to its corresponding side.

After the cuts, each component contains at most one lobe per copy. Two disjoint lobes are joined by tree edges, and two intersecting lobes meet along a connected arc of the common tree. Any cyclic primitive word in rank two uses only one sign of each occurring generator, so its loop cannot traverse a separating edge in both directions. Its surviving component is therefore covered by one of the assumed lobe-rotation pair exclusions or by a single lobe. For coincident rebasing vertices, use the original barbell. If the two axes after the third conjugation are disjoint, their own separating bridge rules out a new primitive. If they intersect, the rebased join contains the subgroup generated by the placed pair. The conclusion allows any Nielsen changes before the third conjugation and any Nielsen suffix after it. This applies the separating-edge argument to the **actual** lobe pair chosen by the middle basis, whose words may differ from both original AK(3) relators. ## Exact compact guards on bridge labels

First verify that, on each bare lobe circle, its own signed rotations have their unique expected readings and no signed rotation of the other lobe can be read. Write $\mathcal R$ for the inverse-closed union of both signed rotation sets. If $|q|\ge L$, a reading of a word in $\mathcal R$ cannot use an edge of each lobe because it would have to traverse the entire bridge as well. A reduced reading can lie on a bare lobe, lie on the bridge, or cross exactly one attachment. A reading that starts on the bridge, runs into a lobe and then goes back out the same bridge is impossible: it would contain a complete signed lobe circuit. A rotation of its own length cannot contain that circuit and a nonempty tail at both ends, and a rotation of the other lobe containing it contradicts the bare-circle test. Reversing the signed word handles all readings that enter rather than leave a lobe.

Consequently the exact additional guard consists of the following two tests.

1. The bridge contains no word of $\mathcal R$ as a linear substring.
2. At each endpoint, take every split $r=uv$ of every $r\in\mathcal R$ with $u,v$ both nonempty. Forbid the outward tail prefix $v$ precisely when $u$ reads on the lobe ending at its marked vertex and $v$ starts in an available direction. Use $q$ for the left outward tail and $q^{-1}$ for the right.

This describes a regular language using the finite substring and prefix sets. For the binary-alphabet catalogue, choose one sign of $x$ and one sign of $y$. If the chosen alphabet contains none of $\mathcal R$, no bridge in it has a forbidden interior factor. If it contains none of the left forbidden prefixes and its inverse alphabet contains none of the right forbidden prefixes, every folded-admissible bridge in that alphabet of length at least $L$ qualifies. A second finite test uses just the pure outward endpoint windows to certify $q=c^L T d^L$ for every $T\in\{c,d\}^*$ in such an alphabet. This gives $2^m$ words of equal length $2L+m$ per padded template. Constant-letter templates use one letter and both pure windows.

## Actual basis catalogue and control

Generate the first placements from simultaneous primitive slope pairs of determinant $\pm1$ with the stated intrinsic mass bound, using intersecting axes or the indicated cap on the first geodesic gap. At each marking, generate simultaneous second slope pairs and substitute them in that marking. The two resulting cyclic words are the second lobes. All bare-unique lobe pairs in these six scopes pass all rotation tests, including joins from two rotations of a single lobe. The native check uses the signed double-free transition graph. An independent Python audit enumerates the small-mass bases as direct Nielsen words, reproduces the entire bare-unique census, and independently checks endpoint template counts for 51 sampled lobe pairs. This includes 15,848 independent folds and signed DAG checks for sampled rotation pairs. A first disjoint placement followed by a nontrivial second move of mass at most four cannot pass the bare uniqueness test in the same presentation of the core. At this mass one row has intrinsic norm one, and the changed lobe runs around the retained donor's entire circle in the folded first barbell. Its cyclic ambient word therefore contains a signed rotation of the retained lobe. This explains why allowing one-edge first bridges can enlarge the initial lobe-pair universe without increasing the number certified in the table.

This result concerns the indicated first placements and the classified middle bridges. Longer first bridges, lobe pairs with competing readings, and additional unrestricted conjugation phases remain open.

c++ -std=c++17 -O3 experiments/round07_ac_disproof_long_bridges.cpp -o "$work/scan"
for scope in 220 330 231 331 430 340; do
  first=${scope%??}
  second=${scope#?}
  second=${second%?}
  gap=${scope#??}
  "$work/scan" "$first" "$second" "$gap" "$work/$scope.jsonl" > "$work/$scope.json"
done
python3 -B experiments/round07_ac_disproof_long_bridges_audit.py "$work" \
