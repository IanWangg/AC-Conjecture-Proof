## 1. The needed alignment quotient is B3 times Z

Put C=F/[F,N]. In C the element z=b is central. Set a=x z^-1. Since the
exponent vector of b is (1,-1), direct central substitution gives

    b(a,y)=b(x z^-1,y)=b(x,y) z^-1=1.

Conversely, start with B3 on generators a,y and an independent central z;
put x=a z. Then b(x,y)=z. These inverse substitutions prove

    C = B3 times Z.

An element of C is determined by its image in B3 and its original free
x-exponent sum: the kernel is the central cyclic group generated by b,
and that homomorphism sends b to one. Thus exact equality in C is checked
by a B3 normal form and this one additional integer. Together with B3's
total exponent sum, this also determines the full F2 abelianization.

## 2. A 19-strict-move aligned prefix

Before E's braid program, perform `r0 <- r0 r1^-1` three times. This does
nothing in B3 because the second coordinate maps to 1. The resulting
13-instruction signed program therefore still ends at `(x,y)` in B3.

Its bare exponent matrix on the relator pair is

    [[-1,4],[-1,3]].

Multiplication by AK(3)'s initial matrix `[[3,-4],[1,-1]]` gives the
identity. Hence the same actual free endpoint has abelianization exactly
`(x,y)` and therefore equals `(x,y)` in C.

The three initial inverse-donor multiplications use only five strict
operations: invert the donor once, multiply three times, and restore it.
E's first two negative multiplications also share an unchanged donor, so
their inversions can be batched. The resulting complete prefix has
**19 strict elementary moves**, actual endpoint lengths **73 and 129**,
and raw peak **202**. `central_prefix.py` constructs the full ledger;
`aligned_prefix_19.json` records it exactly.

This already supplies I's augmentation-zero hypothesis. The 191-move
synchronized product prefix is unnecessary if the sole next objective is
equality in F/[N,N]. It remains necessary to distinguish the separate
product result from this coarser central quotient.

For comparison, exact radius-six instruction balls in C give distance
**12** in the fourteen-instruction alphabet. The complete ball sizes are
315,236 from the AK pair and 184,007 from the basis; the smallest sum of
intersection depths is 12. Every path of length at most 12 has a midpoint
in those balls. The stored 12-instruction witness expands to 16 strict
moves. It gives a worse correction below, so minimum quotient distance is
not the optimization objective used for the final lift.

## 3. A general lifting lemma behind the construction

Here is a useful formulation of I's explicit macros. Let G be generated
by a named tuple `(x1,...,xr)`, r>=2, and let A be an abelian normal
subgroup. If a tuple U can be AC-transformed to the named tuple in
G/[G,A], then it can be AC-transformed to that tuple in G itself.

To prove this, lift the quotient program. The endpoint has coordinates
`a_i x_i` with `a_i` in `[G,A]`. Write A additively. Every such error is a
finite sum

    a_i = sum_j (1-x_j) H_ij,  H_ij in A.

Indeed `[G,A]` is generated by `(1-g)H`, and right telescoping
`1-uv=(1-u)v+(1-v)` reduces every named-generator word g to those terms.

Conjugating coordinate i by an ambient representative of H adds
`(1-x_i)H` to its error. For j!=i the following six-operation macro adds
`(1-x_j)H`, while preserving every other coordinate:

    C_i(x_i), r_i<-r_i r_j, C_i(h),
    r_i<-r_i r_j^-1, C_i(h^-1), C_i(x_i^-1).

The intermediate error calculation is

    x_i K +(1-x_i x_j)H -(1-x_i)H
      = x_i K +x_i(1-x_j)H.

The last conjugation removes the left x_i action. Choosing the negatives
of the finite H_ij therefore kills one coordinate error at a time. Every
ambient h has a finite word in the named generators, so these are finite
ordinary AC programs. The converse follows by projection.

In particular, for any normal subgroup N of a free group F of rank at
least two, a given tuple reaches the free-generator images in F/[N,N]
**if and only if** it does so in F/[F,N]. This is a lifting statement
between two quotients, not a theorem about F itself. It neither erases
the remaining [N,N] errors nor supplies finite termination of deeper
lifting. It is recorded without a priority claim; the concrete macros
are those proved and checked in I.

## 4. A fully expanded 533-move first-order certificate

I's earlier representative routine expressed group elements using a
central C2*C3 normal form, producing long conjugating words. The new
constructor uses an exact B3 breadth-first dictionary through radius 12,
with an exact normal-form fallback, and orders conjugated braid cells
lexicographically by their chosen representative words. Free cancellation
between successive cells makes the ambient kernel words much shorter.
Every constructed word is checked by exact B3 evaluation and both Fox
derivatives; no shortest-word assertion is needed outside the dictionary.

Starting from the 19-move prefix, the initial error supports are 7 and 16
and their l1 norms are 14 and 28, with augmentations zero. Applying I's
four correction phases now gives:

| Quantity | Checked value |
|---|---:|
| Stored word operations | 33 |
| Fully expanded strict moves | **533** |
| Actual free endpoint lengths | **635 and 1,867** |
| Exact strict raw peak | **2,502** |
| Final Fox error supports over B3 | 0 and 0 |

Both the repository's strict elementary verifier and its independently
written free-word/Fox verifier pass. The full 533-step primitive ledger is
supplied, not merely its expansion count. The endpoint equals `(x,y)` in
F/[N,N], and emphatically **does not** equal it in F.

The selected cell order is a finite heuristic. No optimality of 533 moves,
2,502 peak length, or these representatives is asserted.

## 5. Testing the actual free remainder returns a loop

I next ran a certificate-producing greedy cyclic substitution on the
actual free endpoint. It finds long overlaps between a recipient and a
cyclic rotation of a donor or its inverse. A proposed substitution is
compiled into explicit relator inversions, donor/recipient rotations
(ordinary conjugations), multiplication, and donor restoration. Every
accepted change strictly decreases total cyclic relator length; this is
a search heuristic and not a complete test of AC connectivity.

For the selected certificate, eleven shortening steps reduce cyclic
total length through

    2158,1523,764,633,202,129,77,48,27,21,15,13.

The exact final pair is

    (xxYYYYx, YxyxYX).

Conjugating the first coordinate by x and the second by y restores
exactly `(xxxYYYY,xyxYXY)`. The entire construction followed by shortening
is therefore an **actual free-group AC loop**, independently replayed as
4,734 strict operations with peak 2,502. This directly verifies that the
quotient correction has not secretly supplied a trivialization.

Two prefix choices and eight correction order choices were tested: all
16 resulting free remainders return to the same initial cyclic/inverse
orbit under this specified greedy probe. The analogous probe on known
AC-trivial AK(2) also returns to AK(2)'s initial orbit. These controls show
why the failed completion heuristic is not evidence of non-AC-triviality.

## 6. The next central stage has a nonzero defect

There is a stronger, genuinely nonlinear diagnostic of the same actual
endpoint. Put N'=[N,N]. Its two residuals `r0 X` and `r1 Y` lie in N',
but they do **not** vanish in the next central quotient F/[F,N'].

The calculation uses a genuine Schreier free basis of N, not arbitrary
chosen conjugates of b used only as an abelian module basis. Choose the
shortlex spanning tree of the exact B3 Cayley graph. Its Schreier generator
for a positive labeled edge `g --c--> h` is `t_g c t_h^-1`. Tree edges give
the empty word. Each other positive edge gives a free basis generator;
negative edges traverse that generator inversely. Rewriting the two
residuals gives freely reduced Schreier words of lengths 166 and 472.
Multiplying their actual generator words reconstructs the original free
residuals exactly, and each rewritten residual has zero exponent sum in
every Schreier generator.

For a free group N, `N'/[N,N']` is the exterior square of `N_ab`.
Conjugation by N acts trivially there, so passing to `[F,N']` further
takes B3 coinvariants. I's explicit isomorphism `N_ab=Z[B3]` gives

    N'/[F,N'] = (exterior-square Z[B3])_B3.

This follows directly by collecting commutators in the free Schreier
basis: `[N,N']` kills third-order terms, and conjugating a commutator by F
translates both exterior factors simultaneously.

In the coinvariants, `e_g wedge e_h` is represented by
`e_1 wedge e_(g^-1 h)`; the inverse-relative pair has opposite sign. B3 is
torsion-free: b is not a proper power because its exponent vector is
primitive, so the one-relator torsion theorem applies (Putman, Section 4).
Thus no nonidentity relative element equals its inverse.
Choosing one of d,d^-1 therefore gives a free abelian basis of these
coinvariants. The script uses exact B3 normal forms for this choice.

The coefficient of each genuine Schreier generator in `N_ab=Z[B3]` is
obtained from both Fox derivatives of its actual free word. While scanning
the Schreier word, let P be its accumulated module coefficient and delta
the next signed generator coefficient. Accumulate `P wedge delta`. For
a word in N', the result is twice its commutator class; divisibility by
two is checked coefficientwise. For example `u v u^-1 v^-1` gives twice
`u_ab wedge v_ab`, fixing the sign and factor convention.

The resulting next-central classes are nonzero:

| Residual | Schreier length | Coinvariant support | l1 norm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| r0 X | 166 | 67 | 322 |
| r1 Y | 472 | 148 | 469 |

Only 105 exact B3 prefix states were needed. The shortlex dictionary
through depth 11 has 22,579 vertices and contains those states and their
tree paths. Thirty-four non-tree Schreier generators occur. This finite
dictionary is the needed initial portion of an infinite spanning tree;
it does not replace B3 by a finite quotient.

Thus this first-order certificate is not already aligned in F/[F,N'].
The general lifting lemma cannot be applied at N' without first finding a
new path in that central quotient. In particular the same own/cross
macros with conjugating words from N' act trivially on its central kernel
and cannot erase these nonzero classes. This is an obstruction to that
proposed continuation of this particular path, not an invariant under
arbitrary AC moves.

## Reproduction and files

All scripts use the Python standard library and import the previously
published `first_order_correction.py` and `braid_relation_module.py`.
Place them together in `experiments/`.

```sh
python3 experiments/central_search.py --depth 6 --budget 1000000 \
  --output results/central_depth6.json
python3 experiments/central_prefix.py --output certificates/aligned_prefix_19.json
python3 experiments/optimized_correction.py certificates/aligned_prefix_19.json \
  --output certificates/optimized_first_order.json.gz.b64 \

python3 experiments/expand_lift.py certificates/optimized_first_order.json.gz.b64 \
  --output certificates/optimized_first_order_strict.json
python3 experiments/elementary_verify.py certificates/optimized_first_order_strict.json
python3 experiments/verify_first_order.py certificates/optimized_first_order.json.gz.b64
python3 experiments/second_central_audit.py certificates/optimized_first_order.json.gz.b64 \
  --output results/second_central_results.json
python3 experiments/dehn_shorten.py certificates/optimized_first_order.json.gz.b64 \
  --close-to-start --output certificates/central_lift_closed_loop.json.gz.b64
```

The saved closed-loop word ledger is `certificates/central_lift_closed_loop.json.gz.b64`.
Expand it with `expand_lift.py` and replay with the same strict verifier.
`dehn_shorten.py` regenerates the shortening trace and restoring
conjugations with `--close-to-start`. To regenerate the sixteen probes,
use `central_prefix.py --search results/central_depth6.json` to save the
second prefix and pass both prefixes to `test_free_remainders.py`.
The compact result bundle records the
central search, 16 remainder probes, both strict replays, independent
Fox replay, and the next-central diagnostic. Large scratch variants and the expanded loop need not be
committed; the compressed exact ledger is sufficient to regenerate them.

## Consequence for the original objective

The correction is now small enough to inspect and search directly, and
the general lemma identifies precisely the central alignment information
needed for a first-order lift. But its actual free remainder still
returns to the known hard tuple under the tested shortening strategy.
The next derived-kernel stage would require a genuinely new path in
F/[F,N'], not merely repeating the same augmentation calculation. A
finite free-group completion or an unbounded invariant remains missing.
None of the user's four requested breakthrough objectives is achieved.
