## Reduction and phase scope

After a common conjugation, the first two output rows lie in $J_q=\langle H,qHq^{-1}\rangle$. A second phase with relative conjugator $q^k$ puts the two rows in
\[
L_{q,k}=\langle H,qHq^{-1},q^kHq^{-k},q^{k+1}Hq^{-k-1}\rangle.
\]
For $k=1$, this uses three copies of $H$. For $k=2,3$, it uses four. Conjugation by $q^k$ transports the exponent set for $-k$ to that for $k$. Reversing the spanning-tree pair replaces $q$ by $q^{-1}$, whose exponent set for any positive $k$ is transported to the same set by conjugation by $q^{k+1}$. The rows after the final phase and throughout its Nielsen suffix lie in the normal closure of $L_{q,k}$ in $K=\langle L_{q,k},r\rangle$ for an arbitrary relative word $r$. These deductions permit any choice of initial elements of $H$.

For a first relative conjugator in $HqH$, write it as $a q b$ with $a,b\in H$. Common conjugation by $a^{-1}$ carries its two-copy subgroup to $J_q$. In that marking the second relative word may be any element of $J_q q^k J_q$, for $1\le |k|\le3$. If the word is $j_1 q^k j_2$, conjugation by $j_1^{-1}$ puts the resulting rows in $L_{q,k}$. The third relative word remains arbitrary under these changes of marking. This covers all gapless first words outside $H$ for the stated condition on the normalized second word. The two-phase theorem covers the cases where the first relative word lies in $H$ or the normalized second word lies in $J_q$.

The subgroup $N_q=\langle H,q\rangle$ is proper for each $q$ by the single-word theorem. Its based core contains the image of the core of $L_{q,k}$. The latter group lies in the normal closure of $H$ in $N_q$. If $r\in L_{q,k}$, the Round 13 proper-overgroup theorem applies directly. If $r$ has an unread gap in the core of $L_{q,k}$, adjoining it adds a path with one new cycle. The signed count on a new chord kills $L_{q,k}$. A sign-pure cyclic primitive in the normal closure must avoid the chord and all remaining added bridge edges, so its loop would stay in the original core. If the reads of $r$ meet, its double coset is represented by two core vertex paths and adjoining it is a single vertex identification followed by folding. For each such fold, compute the same identification on the image of the two vertices in $N_q$. This gives the cumulative group $\langle H,q,r\rangle$. If it is proper, the normal closure in $K$ is contained in the primitive-free normal closure of $H$ in that cumulative group. [Single-word ranks and proper-overgroup theorem](round-13-ac-disproof.md), [gap criterion](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word).

## Disjoint copies and limited intersections

The same source has a separate exclusion for any finite family of at least two conjugated copies of $H$, and any finite family of extra words. Form the folded graph of the whole generated group $K$. Consider the connected components of the union of the marked source images. Suppose each component contains one source image or two embedded source images whose intersection has at most two connected components. Then the normal closure in $K$ of the conjugated copies contains no ambient primitive.

Choose a spanning tree extending trees in all those components. The signed count on each non-tree edge outside them kills all the conjugated sources and their normal closure. A sign-pure cyclic primitive loop in that normal closure therefore avoids every such edge. The remaining outside edges are bridges, so the loop lies in one component. Retraction to the corresponding free factor puts the putative primitive in the normal closure of its marked source rows. A single-copy component carries a proper ambient subgroup. If it carried $x$ and $y$, both single-edge loops would occur at its root and exhaust all four directions in the connected folded graph, leaving no other image. Round 13 excludes primitives in its source normal closure.

For a two-copy component, glue the abstract source copies at a pair of vertices mapping to one shared vertex. Their path difference $q$ transports the second copy to the first and gives the subgroup $J_q$. Folding the common edges propagates this identification along the entire connected component of that shared vertex in the intersection. If the intersection has another connected component, identifying the preimages of one vertex in it adjoins at most one word. The remaining folds identify the corresponding common edges and give the labelled union of the two images. The two-phase theorem excludes primitives from the normal closure of $J_q$ in this component, including when no additional word is needed. These conditions use the final intersections directly. The two-copy first-contact argument uses a rank-five bound and leaves larger connected components beyond this reduction. ## Literal AK(3) calibration

For literal AK(3), the schedule $(x,x,Y)$ gives the entire ambient rose on adjoining $Y$ to $\langle H,H^x,H^{x^2}\rangle$. Its rank-six core therefore loses four ranks in one final fold. The normal closure of the literal AK(3) rows in $F(x,y)$ already equals $F(x,y)$. Indeed the braid relation makes $x$ and $y$ conjugate, and $z=x^3=y^4$ is central. Conjugacy gives $y^3=z=y^4$, forcing $y=x=1$ in the presentation. This overgroup calculation leaves the actual three-phase exposure problem for literal AK(3) unresolved.
