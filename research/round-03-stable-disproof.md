# Round 03 · Stable Andrews–Curtis disproof

2026-11-19. Four crossing products can transfer at most one marked generator into an auxiliary block. A cut whose full crossing list is two incoming products followed by two outgoing products, or the reverse order, can always be projected away. When the endpoint markings of the block agree, a further projection criterion for exactly two incoming products is governed by the ordinary AC classes in a one-relator quotient.

## Four crossings at an auxiliary cut

Use the normalized ordinary section from the [peak-rank formula](round-01-stable-disproof.md). Absorb row permutations into the row indices, and let $T$ be a nonempty block of auxiliary rows. Write $A$ and $B$ for the sets of marked generators carried by $T$ at the initial padded tuple and at the terminal unordered basis. The sets have the same cardinality. Incoming products have their receiver in $T$. Outgoing products have the donor in $T$ and receiver outside it. Work with the strict signed-product metric from round one.

In degree-one abelianization over $\mathbb F_2$, the starting spans of both blocks are complementary coordinate subspaces $U,V$, with $U$ spanned by $A$. The rank of the projection of the current $T$ span onto $U$ starts at $\dim U$ and ends at $\dim U-s$, where $s=|A\setminus B|$. The matching projection for the outside block loses $s$ as well. Internal moves preserve the span of the block involved, and each crossing can decrease the matching rank of its receiver block by at most one. The first crossing cannot decrease either rank because its donor lies entirely in the opposite coordinate subspace. Thus if $s>0$ there are at least $2s+1$ crossings, with at least $s+1$ in the first direction and at least $s$ in the other. In particular, two incoming and two outgoing products permit at most one transferred generator.

Suppose the four products have directions in–in–out–out. During the two incoming products the outside span stays $V$, so the matching projection of $T$ stays full rank. After the second incoming product the $T$ span stays fixed. Its terminal coordinate span must therefore be $U$, which gives $A=B$. The same argument with the blocks exchanged applies to out–out–in–in. In either case every outgoing product occurs where the normal closure of the current $T$ rows is the endpoint normal closure of $A$. Set all generators of $A$ to the identity and discard the $T$ rows throughout. The outgoing products disappear, and the remaining steps give the desired path at lower padding rank with no increase in ordinary move count. This argument also works in the finite characteristic-two Magnus graphs, using their marked generator deletions. Consequently these two orders are excluded both in paths at the minimum feasible padding when that minimum is positive and in overall stable length minimizers. The other four orders permit zero or one transfer in the exact two-by-two mod-two model. This last fact concerns row spans only. [Round-two cut analysis](round-02-stable-disproof.md), [model results](../results/round03_stable_disproof_cuts.json).

## Eliminating a cut with two incoming products and unchanged marking

Work in the free group. Suppose $A=B$ and there are exactly two incoming products, with any number of outgoing products. Delete $A$ to obtain a marked free group $E$ of rank $r$ on the complement. Let $w$ be the first incoming donor's projection to $E$. Let $w'$ be the second incoming donor's projection just before its use. Outside rows are normally generating in $E$ at these two boundary tuples: before capture there is a projected ordinary path from the normally generating padded start, and after erasure there is one to the basis. Thus $w$ has a primitive exponent vector in $\mathbb Z^r$.

Before capture the $T$ rows vanish under projection. The first incoming product makes their normal closure in $E$ equal to $\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle$. Up to the second incoming product, the normal closure stays the same, because internal moves preserve it and outgoing products leave those rows alone. After the second incoming product the $T$ rows vanish again, by following their normal closure backward from the terminal basis. At that last incoming move all unchanged $T$ rows project to the identity, and the old receiver projects to the second donor or its inverse. Consequently

$$
\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle_E
=\langle\!\langle w'\rangle\!\rangle_E.
$$

By Magnus's normal-closure theorem, $w'$ is conjugate to $w$ or $w^{-1}$ in $E$. Normalize both donor rows to the same position with value $w$. Set $Q=E/\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle$. Let $\bar R,\bar S$ be the two tuples of the other $r-1$ exterior rows in $Q$. Each normally generates $Q$, and their abelianized tuples are bases of $\mathbb Z^{r-1}$. Every outgoing product in the capture interval vanishes in $Q$. Projecting that part of the path on the exterior rows therefore gives ordinary equivalence of $(1,\bar R)$ and $(1,\bar S)$ in $Q$.

**Splice criterion.** If $\bar R$ and $\bar S$ are ordinarily AC-equivalent in $Q$ as $(r-1)$-tuples, the entire original path compiles without the auxiliary block $T$. Lift their AC steps on the other exterior rows to $E$ with donor $w$ held fixed. The remaining coordinate differences lie in $\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle$. Clear them by multiplying by conjugates of the signed donor, restoring it after each use. Undo the terminal donor normalization and follow the projected tail. [Magnus's theorem](https://msp.org/gtm/2008/14/gtm-2008-14-004s.pdf).

For $r\ge3$, the criterion applies whenever $Q$ is solvable, by Guyot's theorem on normally generating tuples of length at least two. For $r=2$, it is enough that normally generating elements of $Q$ be conjugate up to inversion. In particular, if $Q$ is metabelian then its commutator subgroup $C$ is abelian and $Q/C$ is infinite cyclic. Take a lift $t$ of a generator and let $D$ be its conjugation action on $C$. Since $Q=\langle t,C\rangle$, the subgroup $(D-1)C$ is $[Q,Q]=C$. Thus every lift $c t$ of the generator is conjugate to $t$ by an element of $C$. Inversion handles lifts of the negative generator. This is the cyclic metabelian case of Guyot's Proposition 1.7. In the rank-two AK(3) application, $r=2$ means the removed block is all the padding. [Guyot, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 1.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06912).

When $A$ and $B$ differ, setting all of $A\cup B$ to the identity still makes the two boundary $T$ normal closures trivial. The same middle argument proves that the two incoming donors have equal normal closure, and hence are conjugate up to inversion, in that smaller marked free group. The splice criterion above requires the same deletion chart at both ends.

## Free-word control and limits

Take $w=xyx^{-1}y^{-2}$ and start with $(w,x,z)$. The program replays a 16-move excursion with order in–out–in–out and endpoint $(w,xw,z)$, then reaches the unordered basis in 25 total moves. During the capture interval it passes through $(wx,x,zw)$. Neither exterior row then projects to a conjugate of $w$ or its inverse, as already seen in exponent sums. The two boundary projections $(w,x)$ and $(w,xw)$ splice by one product.

Here $Q=\langle x,y\mid xyx^{-1}=y^2\rangle$ is nonabelian metabelian. The cyclic subgroups generated by $x^{-j}yx^j$ form an ascending chain for $j\ge0$. Their union is the normal closure of $y$ and is abelian, and the quotient by it is infinite cyclic. The maps $x(s)=2s$ and $y(s)=s+1$ on $\mathbb Q$ give a noncommuting image satisfying the relation. Thus $w$ is nonprimitive and the criterion is not limited to free deletion quotients.

The [frozen-donor witnesses](round-03-frozen-donors.md) expose the unresolved case at the saved 533-move AK(3) endpoint $(U,V)$. In $F_2/\langle\!\langle V\rangle\!\rangle$, both $U$ and $x$ normally generate but are nonconjugate even up to inversion. Normal generation of $U$ comes from the ordinary prefix, and killing $x$ projects $V$ to $y$ by its exponent vector. The matrix witness proves nonconjugacy. The permutation witness proves the analogous facts for $V,y$ after killing $U$. Hence the rank-one hypothesis of the splice criterion can fail at exactly these relevant deletion quotients. A cut passing through such nonconjugate classes, or a cut transferring a generator, requires a different argument. Stable AC remains unresolved.
