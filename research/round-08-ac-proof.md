# Ordinary projection with two or more changing retained donors

2026-11-19. A common free complement for all but one of the retained donors suffices to splice two marked endpoint projections by ordinary Andrews–Curtis moves. The quotient by the whole donor block can be nonfree. Both donor rows must receive products in five strict rank-three controls built from a torus-knot pair, $W_{23}$, and the three rank-two cap-31 waypoints. An exact rank-two criterion identifies when two primitive pivots admit a common complement. [Move ledgers and Whitehead certificates](../results/round08_ac_proof_two_donors.json).

## Comparing donor blocks

Let $F$ be free of rank $n$ with two ordered donor lists $D_0,D_1$ of length $s$, where $2\leq s\leq n$. Suppose their normal closures in $F$ coincide and denote the common subgroup by $N$. Assume each donor list can be taken by ordinary moves confined to that list to $(P_i,c_i)$, where $P_i=(p_{i1},\ldots,p_{i,s-1})$. Suppose a single free basis $X$ of a subgroup $H$ extends with either $P_i$ to a free basis of $F$. The conclusion is that $D_0$ and $D_1$ are ordinary AC-equivalent using just their donor slots. Individual conjugation and inversion of the $p_{ij}$ can be included in the preliminary moves.

Let $\rho_i:F\to H$ fix $H$ and kill $P_i$. Its kernel is the normal closure of $P_i$, and

$$
N=\rho_i^{-1}\big(\langle\!\langle\rho_i(c_i)\rangle\!\rangle_H\big),
\qquad
N\cap H=\langle\!\langle\rho_i(c_i)\rangle\!\rangle_H .
$$

Indeed, $N$ contains the kernel of $\rho_i$, and its image under $\rho_i$ is normally generated by $\rho_i(c_i)$. Multiply $c_i$ on the right by a product of conjugates of the signed $P_i$ rows to turn it into $\rho_i(c_i)$, restoring each donor after its use. By Magnus, the two resulting words are conjugate up to inversion within $H$, so they can both be aligned to the same row $t\in H$. This includes the case $t=1$. [Magnus's normal-closure theorem](https://msp.org/gtm/2008/14/gtm-2008-14-004s.pdf).

Set $u_j=\rho_0(p_{1j})$ and $p'_{1j}=p_{1j}u_j^{-1}$. Each $u_j$ lies in $N\cap H$ and each $p'_{1j}$ lies in $\ker\rho_0$. The list $(X,P'_1)$ is a basis because the right multipliers $u_j^{-1}$ are words in the fixed part $X$ of the basis $(X,P_1)$. In the ambient basis $(X,P_0)$, Piggott's theorem applies to $P'_1$. It gives an ordinary path $P_0\to P'_1$ using the $s-1$ indicated slots and conjugations by the words of $X$. If $X$ has one letter, apply the stated theorem in the free product $F*\langle e\rangle$ with $e$ adjoined to the fixed basis and then apply the homomorphism $e\mapsto1$ to every step. This yields a path in $F$ on the original slots. Conjugations by fixed words expand to single marked letters. [Piggott, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1515/JGT.2007.029).

The row $t$ remains at its aligned value. Since every $u_j$ belongs to $\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle_H$, products of conjugates of $t^{\pm1}$ send $p'_{1j}$ back to $p_{1j}$ for all $j$. Restore $c_1$ and undo the preliminary moves on the second endpoint. This proves the donor comparison.

## Endpoint test in the common quotient

Take two padded endpoint charts in a common ambient free group $G$. At each end, deleting $k$ designated relators gives a free group of rank $n$. A block of $k+s$ relators contains both sets of deleted slots, and the block has the same normal closure $K$ at the two endpoints. Suppose the two chart bases have common lifts to $G$. In the resulting common marking, the retained block projections $D_0,D_1$ have the same normal closure $N=\ker(F_n\to G/K)$, and the outside rows project to tuples $V_0,V_1$ in $F_n$. [Common lifted bases and the canonical pushout](round-05-ac-proof.md#common-lifting-and-the-one-donor-splice).

If the donor blocks meet the common complement hypothesis, there is an ordinary splice from $(D_0,V_0)$ to $(D_1,V_1)$ with no products from the outside into the block if and only if $V_0,V_1$ have AC-equivalent images in $Q=G/K$ using the outside rows alone. For necessity, the donor rows stay in $N$, so delete their updates and all uses of them in the projected sequence in $Q$. For sufficiency, lift the outside sequence using preimages of its conjugators, apply the donor comparison, then correct each outside row by a product of conjugates of the final donors. The corrections exist because both outside images agree in $Q$. The criterion depends on the endpoints of the padded path. It allows the padded path itself to import outside rows. [One-donor endpoint criterion](round-07-ac-proof.md#necessary-and-sufficient-endpoint-condition).

## Recognizing a common complement for two exposed primitive pivots

For $s=2$, let $p_0,p_1$ be the exposed primitive rows in the same marked $F_n$. Put $L_i=\langle\!\langle p_i\rangle\!\rangle_{F_n}$ and $P=F_n/(L_0L_1)$. The desired simultaneous complement exists up to individual conjugation and inversion exactly when there is one tuple $X$ of lifts of bases of both $F_n/L_i$ for which $H=\langle X\rangle$ is a corank-one free factor of $F_n$.

One direction follows by passing either basis $(X,p_i)$ to the quotient. In the other direction, each quotient restricts to an isomorphism on $H$, giving a retraction $\rho_i:F_n\to H$. Choose $z$ completing $X$ to a basis, and put $u_i=\rho_i(z)$. Then $\ker\rho_i=\langle\!\langle zu_i^{-1}\rangle\!\rangle_{F_n}$. Magnus identifies $p_i$ with $zu_i^{-1}$ up to conjugation and inversion. Both resulting rows extend the chosen $X$. The round-five pushout criterion gives unrestricted common lifts exactly when the two induced generating tuples in $P$ are Nielsen-equivalent. The free-factor condition on the lifts is the additional requirement in this argument. [Proof of common lifting](round-05-ac-proof.md#common-lifting-and-the-one-donor-splice).

For ambient rank two the common lifts can always be chosen to generate a free factor when unrestricted common lifts exist. Choose a marking $p_0=x$ and write $(u,v)$ for the exponent vector of $p_1$ in $(x,y)$. If $v=0$, then $u=\pm1$ and the primitive conjugacy classes of $p_0$ and $p_1$ agree up to inversion. If $v\ne0$, the pushout is cyclic of order $|v|$. On the left the image of a quotient generator is $\pm1$ in the marking by $y$. On the right a native positive generator has image $b$ with $ub=1\bmod |v|$, since $y$ maps in the right infinite cyclic quotient to $u$ times that generator. Thus common lifts exist exactly when $u\equiv\pm1\bmod |v|$.

A common primitive complement has exponent vector $(a,\pm1)$, and after inverting it if needed, the other determinant test is $|u-av|=1$. This is the same congruence. For sufficiency, choose $h=x^a y$ and lift the unimodular matrix with columns $(a,1),(u,v)$ to an automorphism of $F_2$. The resulting pair has first entry conjugate to $h$ and second entry conjugate to $p_1$, by uniqueness of the primitive conjugacy class of a given exponent vector. Conjugate the whole pair to put $h$ first. The marking $(x,h)$ is already a basis. The exact examples are $p_1=(xy)^4y$ with complement $xy$, and $p_1=(xy^2)^2y$ with no common generator lift or complement. Both pushouts have order five. [Osborne and Zieschang on primitive classes in rank two](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/IM_1981__63_142788/), [exact rank-two controls](../results/round08_ac_proof_two_donors.json).

## Rank-three controls with changing marked charts

For a rank-two pair $(t,w)$, take $G=F(x,y,z,a)$ and $H=F(x,y)$. Prepare the left projected donor pair $(z,t)$ and the right pair $(zt^{-1},t)$. To obtain the left endpoint donors $D_0$, conjugate the second row by $xz$, multiply the first row once by the second, then multiply the second twice by the first. To obtain the right donors $D_1$, perform the analogous recipe with conjugator $yzz$ and powers two and three. The common normal closure of the projected donors is $\langle\!\langle z,t\rangle\!\rangle$. The two prepared first rows each extend $(x,y)$ to a basis.

In the padded start $(a,D_0(z),w)$, undo the left preparation to get $(a,z,t,w)$. Multiply $a$ by $t^{-1}$ using the third row, then perform the right preparation on the first and third slots with $a$ in place of $z$. Multiply the outside row by the first row conjugated by $azx^{-1}$ and conjugate the result by $y$. The deleted slots switch from the first row $a$ to the second row $z$. The word tuple $(x,y,za)$ gives common lifted bases with charts $a=1$ and $z=1$, respectively. In the common quotient $Q=H/\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle_H$, the outside row changes by conjugation by $y$.

The ordinary path in rank three conjugates the outside row by $y$, undoes the left preparation, changes $z$ to $zt^{-1}$, performs the right preparation, and corrects the outside row by the final first donor conjugated by $yzx^{-1}$. Each complete padded and ordinary path replays in 30 strict operations. Both designated donor identities must receive a multiplication in any ordinary splice between these projected endpoints, since the sets of their individual Whitehead minimum lengths are disjoint and inversion and conjugation preserve those lengths. The pairs are balanced presentations of the trivial group. For the torus-knot seed, $w=xy^{-1}$ identifies $x=y$, after which $t=x^2y^{-3}$ kills $y$. For the waypoint seeds this follows by replay from AK(3). [Strict verification of the source waypoints](round-07-stable-proof.md#sharp-ordinary-entries-for-the-three-minima-and-one-marked-chart), [marked $W_{23}$ certificate](../certificates/round07_ak3_marked_waypoint30.json), [Whitehead's minimum-length test](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968618).

The common quotient $Q$ is nonfree in all five controls. The exponent vector of $t$ is primitive by the unimodular $2\times2$ exponent matrix of $(t,w)$, so $Q_{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb Z$. If $Q$ were free, it would be infinite cyclic. A surjection $F(x,y)\to\mathbb Z$ has kernel normally generated by one primitive word, by a change of basis in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. Magnus would make $t$ primitive as well. Its verified Whitehead minimum length is at least five.

In the original rank-two marking, the individual AK(3) minima are 7 and 5. The waypoint minima are $(10,11)$, $(13,13)$, $(17,10)$ and $(16,13)$, so the existing ordinary paths from AK(3) to these waypoints must likewise multiply into both rows. The new rank-three controls use the waypoints as seeds for adding an exposed primitive donor. For the original rank-two pairs the hypothesis would require a donor-only path exposing a primitive row, which for these balanced trivial presentations would itself give an ordinary trivialization. Neither that hypothesis nor a solution of ordinary AC is established here.
