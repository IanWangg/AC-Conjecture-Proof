# Five conjugations necessary for an ordinary AK(3) trivialization

2026-11-19. Every ordinary rank-two AC path from literal AK(3) to a free basis uses at least **five single-generator conjugations**. Round 18 excludes three or fewer. The eight four-conjugation schedules left by Round 19 all fail a joint test on the two primitive rows required immediately before the last conjugation. The test retains the two first source rows separately in an abelian quotient of an eight-vertex subgroup. It has no bound on the Nielsen intervals or the lengths of the first rows. [Exact replay](../results/round20_ac_disproof_primitive_incidence.json), [three-conjugation exclusion](round-18-ac-disproof.md), [eight schedules](round-19-ac-disproof.md).

## Keeping both source rows

Write $H=\langle p,b\rangle$ for the literal source subgroup, with $p=\texttt{xxxYYYY}$ and $b=\texttt{xyxYXY}$. For a group element $g$, write $w^g=gwg^{-1}$. The eight schedules have first two letters $(t,s)=(x,y)$ or $(y,x)$, followed by $t^{\pm1}$ and $s^{\pm1}$. Let $u$ be the stationary row at the first conjugation and $v$ its receiver. They are an ordered basis of $H$, with exponent rows $U=(\alpha,\beta)$ and $V=(\gamma,\delta)$ in the marked basis $(p,b)$. Thus $\det(U,V)=\pm1$.

After the first conjugation both rows lie in $\langle u,v^t\rangle$. After the second they lie in
\[
\langle u,v^t,u^s,v^{st}\rangle .
\]
The group
\[
K=\langle H,H^s,H^{st},t\rangle
\]
contains all four elements. The third conjugation uses $t$ or $t^{-1}$, so the two resulting rows and every row in the following Nielsen interval lie in the normal closure of $\langle H,u^s,v^{st}\rangle$ inside $K$.

The core of $K$ has eight vertices and rank six in each case. The six ordered words $(p,b,p^s,b^s,p^{st},b^{st})$ give an integral basis for its abelianization, as checked by a spanning-tree coordinate matrix and its integer inverse. Quotient by the first two basis vectors to obtain $\pi:K\to\mathbb Z^4$ in coordinates $(p^s,b^s,p^{st},b^{st})$. The image of every actual row after the third conjugation and before the fourth belongs to the lattice
\[
\{(j\alpha,j\beta,k\gamma,k\delta):j,k\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Conjugations by elements of $K$ act trivially in this quotient. The lattice retains one complementary source direction in each of its two coordinate blocks. [Coordinate matrices and inverse markings](../results/round20_ac_disproof_primitive_incidence.json).

## All relevant primitive classes in the prefix cores

If the path ends at a basis after four conjugations, the pair immediately after the fourth is already a basis, since a Nielsen suffix preserves its subgroup. Both rows immediately before that conjugation are therefore individually primitive. Their ambient exponent determinant must be $\pm1$, since conjugations preserve exponent vectors.

Every cyclically reduced rank-two primitive is sign-pure, and each cyclic primitive class on a signed alphabet has a representative generated by the positive two-coordinate basis operations $(A,B)\mapsto(A,AB)$ and $(A,B)\mapsto(AB,B)$. To exhaust primitives using at least two letters of each generator, the replayer stores pairs of partial vertex maps with letter counts saturated at $(6,4)$. Composition updates both maps and saturated counts exactly. The replayer checks closure of all 2,180 states under both operations across the two cores and four signed alphabets. The only accepted classes with both counts at least two have ambient exponents $(5,3)$ for $t=x$ and $(2,3)$ for $t=y$, up to simultaneous inversion. The caps distinguish both vectors from every larger count. A cyclic rotation changes the start vertex of an accepted loop and is therefore covered by the test at all vertices. [Partial-map method](research-r.md), [primitive count receipts](../results/round20_ac_disproof_primitive_incidence.json).

A primitive with a singleton generator, after inversion and cyclic rotation if necessary, has a representative of the form $xy^k$ or $yx^k$. A pure primitive is included when $k=0$. From each vertex the replayer follows the singleton and then traces iterations of the other letter with either sign. A partial deterministic map terminates at a missing arc or repeats a vertex. The first return and any repeating segment give exact conditions on every exponent $k$ and an integer increment of $\pi$. Traversing a conjugating prefix and its inverse adds zero total voltage, so these values also give $\pi$ for based representatives of the cyclic loops.

| First two letters | Ambient exponents | Values of $\pi$ up to simultaneous inversion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $xy$ | $(1,4n)$ | $(1+3n,0,-1-3n,0)$, plus $(1,0,-1,1)$ when $n=0$ |
| $xy$ | $(3m,1)$ | $(1+4m,0,-1-3m,0)$ |
| $yx$ | $(1,4n)$ | $(1+3n,0,-1-4n,0)$ |
| $yx$ | $(3m,1)$ | $(1+4m,0,-1-4m,0)$, plus $(1,0,-1,-1)$ when $m=0$ |

Here $m,n$ are arbitrary integers. Every return profile, including the additional singleton loops, is independently read from the specified core and the spanning-tree chord voltages.

Two classes of type $(1,4n)$ have determinant divisible by four. Two of type $(3m,1)$ have determinant divisible by three. If $a\in\{5,2\}$, the exceptional vector $(a,3)$ has determinant $4an-3$ against $(1,4n)$ and $a-9m$ against $(3m,1)$. Neither expression can be $\pm1$. Two exceptional classes also have determinant zero. Consequently a pre-last pair of primitives with determinant of absolute value one must use one class of each of the two periodic types.

For $t=x$, the required $(3m,1)$ primitive has nonzero first and second coordinate blocks, both along the direction $(1,0)$. The integers $1+4m$ and $-1-3m$ never vanish. The lattice condition would force both $U$ and $V$ into the same direction. For $t=y$, the required $(1,4n)$ primitive likewise forces both source rows into that direction, since $1+3n$ and $-1-4n$ never vanish. Both contradict $\det(U,V)=\pm1$. This excludes all eight remaining four-letter schedules.

## A necessary condition with auxiliary rows

Consider the normalized stable fixed-rank section after killing all added generators and retaining their rows. With exactly three conjugations by original letters, Round 11 forces $x,x,Y$. Just before the first $x$, the projected rows lie in $H$. If the stationary projected rows at this move generate a cyclic subgroup, take a generator $u\in H$, including $u=1$ in the trivial case. All rows after the first conjugation lie in $\langle u,H^x\rangle$. After the second they lie in $\langle u,H^x,H^{x^2}\rangle$. The last conjugation and its Nielsen suffix remain in the normal closure in $\langle u,H^x,H^{x^2},Y\rangle$ excluded by the Round 18 fibre calculation. That calculation holds for any $u\in H$ and places no bound on the number of rows contained in its kernel.

Thus a three-original-letter stable path needs two noncommuting stationary projected rows at the first conjugation. Its moving projected row must also be nontrivial. Otherwise the first conjugation can be omitted from the projected path, violating the Round 06 two-conjugation exclusion. At least three projected rows must already be nontrivial before that first move, requiring a multiplication before it from the padded initial tuple. The argument leaves paths with noncyclic stationary projected subgroup open.
