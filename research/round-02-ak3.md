# Round 02 · Ordinary AC trivialization of AK(3)

Two rank-two subgroups can match after arbitrary Nielsen moves on both pairs and independent conjugation of the coordinates if they have two common ambient conjugacy classes which can form a basis on both sides. This round uses Stallings fiber products to detect such joins. For the tested AK(3) cores it also exhausts arbitrary preliminary independent conjugations on the known-trivial side. The scan found no join in that scope. AK(3) remains unresolved.

## From common loops to a strict join

Let \(A,B\le F(x,y)\) have rank two. In their labelled Stallings cores, a fiber-product vertex is a pair of vertices, and a labelled edge is present if both coordinates can follow the corresponding edge. An ambient conjugacy class represented in both subgroups has a cyclically reduced representative which reads a loop in the product. Conversely every product loop projects to loops in the two cores. Paths from the subgroup basepoints to the projected loops give representatives in \(A\) and \(B\). A different choice of such a path changes the representative by conjugation within the corresponding subgroup. [Stallings, Theorem 5.5](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993).

A prospective ordinary join requires two classes whose projections are primitive **in their respective subgroups**. Calculate the projected words in a spanning-tree basis of each rank-two group. The two projected conjugacy classes contain a basis if and only if both are primitive and their two abelianization columns have determinant of absolute value one. Necessity follows from abelianization. For sufficiency, lift the unimodular matrix to a free-group automorphism by Nielsen moves. Its two images have the desired vectors and are primitive. In rank two a primitive conjugacy class is uniquely determined by its signed exponent vector, so the images represent the required classes. The same argument applies independently on both sides. This gives a sequence of Nielsen moves in each subgroup followed by individual ambient conjugations to a common pair. Reversing one sequence gives a strict ordinary join. [Osborne and Zieschang, Theorem 1.2](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/IM_1981__63_142788/).

If a product component has rank one, a loop in it is a power of its core cycle. A proper power cannot project to a primitive on either side, since the projection of the cycle is nontrivial by immersion. Thus each rank-one component contributes at most one candidate class up to sign. Two basis classes cannot come from the same rank-one component. If every component has rank zero or one, the primitive tests and determinant test decide the join exactly.

A product of two rank-two cores has at most one rank-two component and no higher-rank component, by the strengthened Hanna Neumann inequality. The implementation tests necessary determinant conditions on the two inclusion matrices when this case occurs. A surviving rank-two case is recorded as inconclusive unless the right subgroup equals \(F(x,y)\) and the left core has a primitive-absence certificate. In that event the forward subgroup would need to contain ambient primitives, already excluded. All the observed rank-two products were of this last type. [Mineyev, Theorem 25](https://mineyev.web.illinois.edu/art/submult-shnc.pdf), [signed-DAG primitive-absence test](research-r.md).

## Arbitrary target conjugation tails

For each tested fixed forward core, the off-diagonal self-product on unordered pairs of distinct vertices is a forest. A nonempty reduced word readable from two distinct vertices labels a nonbacktracking path in this forest. Therefore it is one of the finitely many oriented path labels obtained by traversing the forest. This verifies malnormality and gives an explicit list of all words admitting two or more distinct readable lifts. The four waypoint cores have respective lists of 38, 40, 34 and 94 words, with maximum lengths 4, 5, 5 and 9.

Independently conjugating the two target words has two possibilities. If their axes intersect, the resulting subgroup is a global conjugate of one generated by independent rotations of the two words. If the axes are disjoint, its core consists of the two cyclic lobes joined by a shortest labelled bridge. The first bridge direction and the inverse last bridge direction must avoid the two lobe directions at their respective endpoints. [Round 01 axis and barbell reduction](round-01-ak3.md#exact-subgroup-matching-for-a-single-independent-conjugation-phase-on-each-side).

The product with a target barbell starts with the disjoint union of the two products with the individual lobes. Every complete lift of the bridge glues a path between these lobe products. Partial bridge lifts are trees attached to at most one endpoint or disjoint from the lobe products. They contribute no cycle. This follows because over the interior of a bridge a vertex has at most one edge in each direction, and inverse transitions are injective. Contracting each connected component of each lobe product to a vertex preserves the contribution of the complete lifted bridges to the first Betti number. In the computation, the rank contribution is counted by joining the endpoint components for each complete lift and incrementing the rank only on a repeated connection.

Across every tested source and target there is at most one intrinsic cycle in the two lobe products before attaching the bridge paths. Thus a bridge with at most one complete lift leaves total product cycle rank at most one. Every bridge with at least two lifts must be in the finite off-diagonal list. Each such label is tested at all pairs of lobe attachment positions that satisfy the direction restrictions. Every test in this branch also gives total product cycle rank at most one. This covers all lengths of target conjugating tails. The rank bound excludes two independent basis classes even when the allowed Nielsen prelude on the target barbell has unbounded length.

An independent Python audit extends the bridge words by their partial maps, instead of traversing the C++ off-diagonal forests. It reproduces all 25 list sizes and maximum word lengths. It checks the single-intrinsic-lobe condition for all 262,169 source and target comparison pairs, and uses explicit folding and product graphs for 8,788 sampled barbells and 32,778 sampled rotation comparisons.

The comparison allows arbitrary Nielsen moves within each fixed forward core, arbitrary independent conjugations of the two target archive coordinates before their Nielsen moves, and independent ambient conjugation to match the resulting bases. On the forward side the 21 classes also cover arbitrary initial independent conjugations whose axes intersect. Further arbitrary subgroup-changing phases between the fixed forward core and the matching basis are outside this conclusion.

For a positive control, use the tuples **(xyxyXY, yxyXY)** and **(x, yXyxYx)**. Each has a barbell core. Their product has the two cyclic components with classes \(x,y\), each primitive in both marked cores with unimodular columns. The round 01 all-tail test cannot join the two *displayed* basis pairs through independent conjugations followed by subgroup equality. The new algorithm finds the join by changing bases first. Its compiler reconstructs suitable bases in both groups and transports explicit free-group moves through the spanning-tree markings. The saved join replays in 22 elementary moves with raw peak 11. [Strict positive control](../certificates/round02_ak3_fiber_control.json).

## Reproduction

Run the round 01 archive replay with cap 44 and pass the resulting reverse catalogue to the following commands. The cap-32 catalogue is an exact filter of the cap-44 catalogue. Initial rotation cores are deduplicated up to unbased labelled graph isomorphism by independent Python folding. All binaries and temporary catalogues can stay outside the repository.

```sh
mkdir -p /tmp/ac-r02-fiber
python3 -B experiments/round02_ak3_fiber_replay.py prepare \
  /tmp/ac-r02-fiber /tmp/ac-r01-output/reverse.tsv
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 experiments/round02_ak3_fiber_join.cpp -o /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/join
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 experiments/round01_ak3_all_tails.cpp -o /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/prior
/tmp/ac-r02-fiber/join /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/sources.tsv /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/reverse44.tsv \
  all_tails /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/fiber_tails44.json
/tmp/ac-r02-fiber/join /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/sources_initial_rotations.tsv /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/reverse32.tsv \
  all_tails /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/fiber_initial_rotations.json
/tmp/ac-r02-fiber/join /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/ctlA.tsv /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/ctlB.tsv \
  literal /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/control_fiber.json
/tmp/ac-r02-fiber/prior /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/ctlA_canonical.tsv /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/ctlB_canonical.tsv \
  /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/control_older.json
python3 -B experiments/round02_ak3_fiber_replay.py compile \
  /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/control_fiber.json /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/control.json
diff certificates/round02_ak3_fiber_control.json /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/control.json
python3 -B experiments/round02_ak3_fiber_audit.py /tmp/ac-r02-fiber \
  > /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/fiber_direct_audit.json
python3 -B experiments/round02_ak3_fiber_replay.py summary \
  /tmp/ac-r02-fiber /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/summary.json
diff results/round02_ak3_fiber_join.json /tmp/ac-r02-fiber/summary.json
```

For the next search, forward cores reached after additional alternating phases can be passed directly to this solver. An off-diagonal product with cycles or a proper target with a rank-two fiber component should be retained as an explicit new case.
