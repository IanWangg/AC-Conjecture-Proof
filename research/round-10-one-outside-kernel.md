# Unbounded primitive exclusion with one outside conjugator

2026-11-19. Let $H$ be the subgroup of $F(x,y)$ generated by either the marked AK(3) relators or the new energy-17 pair `(xxyXYXy, xxYXYxYYXY)`. For every reduced word $q$, the normal closure of $H$ in $\langle H,q\rangle$ contains no ambient primitive. Consequently an ordinary AC path from either marked pair cannot expose a primitive row if all of its conjugators belong to a single fixed $\langle H,q\rangle$. The number of instructions, word lengths and alternations of products with conjugations are unrestricted within this hypothesis. [Exact finite audits](../results/round10_one_outside_kernel.json).

## Outside edges of an immersed core

Let $L\le G\le F(x,y)$ have finite based immersed core graphs, and suppose the core of $L$ embeds as a subgraph of the core of $G$. Choose a spanning tree of $L$ and extend it to one of $G$. For every unoriented edge of $G$ outside both $L$ and the extended tree, choose one direction. The signed traversal count of that edge gives a character of $G$ killing $L$. The cyclic portion of any loop in the normal closure of $L$ has zero count on every such edge, because moving its basepoint by conjugation preserves each character value.

Every cyclically reduced ambient primitive uses only one sign of each generator that it uses. For a particular outside edge it can therefore traverse that edge in at most one direction. Its zero signed count forces it to avoid the edge entirely. Deleting all those outside edges leaves $L$ with trees attached, so any remaining cyclic primitive would be conjugate to a word in $L$. If $L$ is primitive-free, the normal closure of $L$ in $G$ is primitive-free as well. The same argument applies to any subgroup of $G$ whose loops have zero signed counts on these edges and whose surviving loops in $L$ are known to lie in a primitive-free subgroup up to conjugacy.

## One outside word with a positive unread gap

Take $G=\langle H,q\rangle$. As in the [overgroup census](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word), read the maximal prefix of $q$ and the maximal inverse suffix at the original basepoint. If a positive gap remains, attach that gap between the two ends. Its unused label directions prevent folds with the original core. A new loop can fold at its own base into a stem and nonempty circuit. In both cases the new graph contains the original core as a subgraph, so the preceding result applies with $L=H$. Equivalently, all cyclic primitive loops of voltage zero must avoid one chosen edge of the new cycle, chosen to give $q$ voltage one. [Certified primitive absence in the two original cores](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md).

This embedded-core argument applies to any number of outside words whenever the original core still embeds in their joined core.

## One outside word with no unread gap

Otherwise write $q=\alpha\beta$ with $\alpha$ and $\beta^{-1}$ readable to vertices $a,b$. For fixed tree paths $p_a,p_b$, the identity $q\in H(p_ap_b^{-1})H$ gives $\langle H,q\rangle=\langle H,p_ap_b^{-1}\rangle$. If $a=b$, then $q\in H$ and the result holds directly. If $a\ne b$, the group is precisely the folded identification of those vertices. The old AK(3) data cover all 45 such groups. Each has a character killing $H$ and sending the indicated generator $p_ap_b^{-1}$ to one, and each character kernel is primitive-free by the finite signed-double-free cycle inequalities. [Original 45 certificates and exact reasoning](research-v-unbounded.md).

For the energy-17 pair, the same independent procedure checks **all 91** vertex identifications. All 91 groups have rank three. The audit verifies their exact folds independently, reconstructs 728 signed-double-free transition graphs, recognizes 342 cyclic components, and checks 636 integer potential inequalities. These calculations prove the absence of any nonempty zero-voltage cyclic primitive word at all lengths. The normal closure of $H$ lies in the certified kernel. In fact it equals that kernel, as both groups are the free product of $H$ with the new infinite cyclic factor by the [single-adjunction result](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md#adjoining-a-single-outside-word). [Energy-17 finite certificates](../certificates/round10_one_outside_kernel_e17.json), [independent replayer](../experiments/verify_vertex_voltage.py).

Every product, inversion or conjugation in $G$ keeps the current relators in the normal closure of $H$ there. This proves the ordinary-path restriction. A path can use conjugators that collectively escape every such fixed group, so the conjecture remains open.

## Every proper overgroup of rank at most three

For both marked pairs, the normal closure of $H$ in **every proper subgroup $Gleq F(x,y)$ of rank at most three containing $H$** is primitive-free. This allows all elements of $G$ as conjugators, however many generators are needed to describe $G$ relative to $H$.

Inside the based core of $G$, the image of the based core of $H$ is a connected subgraph and represents a free factor $L$ of $G$. This follows by extending a spanning tree of the image to the whole graph. The image has no dangling edges other than a possible based tail, since every image edge is traversed by an immersed based loop from $H$. Its rank is at least two because $H$ surjects on the ambient abelianization. If the rank is two, [the overgroup census](round-09-rank-two-overgroups.md) gives $L=H$ or $L=F(x,y)$. Properness excludes the second case, so the core of $H$ embeds and the outside-edge argument applies. If the rank is three, $L=G$ and the whole core of $G$ is a principal quotient of the core of $H$. The principal quotients are enumerated by successive vertex identifications and folds.

For the energy-17 pair, the 91 single-vertex-pair identifications already cover all 42 distinct rank-three principal cores. For AK(3), the 45 single-vertex-pair identifications cover 20 of the 21 rank-three cores. The additional core has basis `(x, YY, yxyXY)`. The coordinates of the original relators in its abelianization are $(3,2,0)$ and $(1,0,-1)$. The character with values $(-2,3,-2)$ on the basis kills both. An independent reachability and potential replay checks all 24 inequalities for this additional character. Thus its kernel is primitive-free, and it contains the normal closure of $H$.
