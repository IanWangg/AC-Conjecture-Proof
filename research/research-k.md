# Research K: a finite obstruction to a primitive replacement after the first hump

Date: 2026-11-02. Status: an exact, unbounded **one-coordinate** obstruction at a specific AC-equivalent AK(3) tuple. **This is not a global AC invariant and does not solve AK(3).** No novelty claim is made.

After the coordinating agent identified a free-by-cyclic model for the length-eight braid replacement below, I independently chose a finite-characteristic-quotient test of its twisted conjugacy problem. This complements a search for positive conjugators: an exact finite quotient can exclude every integer parameter simultaneously, rather than failing to find a bounded conjugator.

## Statement

Use X=x^-1, Y=y^-1, and put

    p=xxxYYYY,   b=xyxYXY,   r=XXyxYxxY.

Research F observes that r=b in the torus quotient <x,y | p=1>, so replacing b by r is an allowed quotient-conjugacy move. Its total cyclic length is 7+8=15.

**Theorem.** No primitive element of F(x,y) has image conjugate to p or p^-1 in

    G=<x,y | r=1>.

Thus, even allowing arbitrary word lengths, a single quotient-conjugacy replacement of p while holding r fixed cannot produce a primitive relator. Research J also excludes a primitive replacement of r while p is fixed, since r=b in the quotient by p. Consequently **neither coordinate of (p,r) can be replaced by a primitive in one ACM move**.

This extends the initial primitive-replacement exclusion to a first length-15 departure. A path that changes both coordinates again is unrestricted by this theorem.

## 1. Exact free-by-cyclic model

Define fiber generators a,d and a stable letter t by

    t=x,   a=X^2 y x,   d=X y.

The group G is isomorphic to

    <a,d,t | t a t^-1=d, t d t^-1=a d^-1>
      = F(a,d) semidirect_alpha Z,

where

    alpha(a)=d,   alpha(d)=a d^-1.

The inverse automorphism is alpha^-1(a)=d a and alpha^-1(d)=a. Thus this really is a free-by-cyclic normal form, with no extra relation on the fiber.

To verify the presentation change directly, the first stable-letter relation follows from the definitions. The second becomes

    yX = X^2 y x Y x,

which is equivalent to r=1. Conversely x=t and y=t d reconstruct the original generators and r follows from the two displayed relations. The two maps are inverse: in particular t^-1 d t=a.

In normal form, with coefficients on the left,

    y=(a d^-1)t,   Y=d^-1 t^-1,
    c=xY=d a^-1,
    p^-1=W t,   W=a d^2 a^-1 d a^-1.

For a free check of the last formula, put u=a d^-1. Expanding y^4 x^-3 gives u alpha(u) alpha^2(u) alpha^3(u) t. The four coefficients are

    aD, d^2A, aDaD^2, d^2Ad^2AdA,

where A=a^-1 and D=d^-1. Their freely reduced product is a d^2 A d A, exactly W. The implementation also verifies the images of these original x,y words directly in the finite semidirect quotient below.

## 2. All primitive candidates reduce to one integer family

The homomorphism chi:G->Z sends x and y to 1 and t to 1; its kernel is the free fiber F(a,d). The image of p^-1 has chi-value one. Inverting a proposed primitive word if needed reduces the problem to primitives of total exponent sum one.

Their free abelianization vectors are (k+1,-k), k in Z. The words

    v_k=(xY)^k x=c^k x

are primitive: (c,x) is a free basis because y=c^-1 x, and replacing x by c^k x preserves that basis. Nielsen's uniqueness theorem says every primitive word with the same exponent vector is freely conjugate to v_k. Therefore this family covers every possible primitive replacement, not merely short examples.

In G the candidates have normal form C^k t, with C=d a^-1. Conjugacy to Wt may be tested using only fiber conjugators. Indeed, for any conjugator g and any u of chi-value one, let q=g u^-chi(g). Then chi(q)=0 and q u q^-1=g u g^-1. Conjugating Wt by a fiber word q changes its coefficient to

    q W alpha(q)^-1.

It remains to exclude C^k from this twisted orbit for every integer k.

## 3. Explicit finite characteristic quotient

Let

    R=F_2< U,V > / (all monomials of degree at least six).

This is a finite noncommutative algebra: its basis consists of the empty monomial and all binary words of lengths one through five, 63 basis elements in total. Let H be the subgroup of its units generated by

    a -> 1+U,   d -> 1+V.

Each unit 1+z has inverse 1+z+...+z^5 because z is in the nilpotent augmentation ideal. No commutativity is imposed on U,V.

Every free-group automorphism preserves the kernel of this truncated mod-two Magnus map. Indeed, substituting arbitrary augmentation elements for U,V preserves the degree-six ideal; applying the inverse free-group automorphism supplies the inverse substitution. In particular alpha induces an automorphism of H, with generator images d and a d^-1.

Exact finite closure gives

    |H|=524,288,    order(alpha on H)=24.

Hence the explicit finite group Q=H semidirect_alpha C_24 has order **12,582,912**, and G surjects onto Q. The generator formulas above define the surjection. The code checks r=1, a=X^2 yx, d=Xy, p^-1=Wt, and c=C directly in Q.

The finite twisted orbit is generated by four operations

    z -> g z alpha(g)^-1,   g in {a,d,a^-1,d^-1}.

Starting from W, breadth-first closure has exactly **65,536** elements. It is complete, with no word-length cutoff or heuristic pruning. The element C has exact order eight in H. None of the eight powers

    1,C,C^2,...,C^7

belongs to the completed orbit. This excludes every k in Z at once and proves the theorem.

The same computation also describes full conjugacy in Q for this stable-letter coset: the preceding conjugator adjustment works using the projection Q->C_24 and u=Wt. Thus this is an explicit finite conjugacy-separation certificate, not an assumption that bounded failure reflects an infinite orbit.

It does not conflict with the finite-group AC connectivity theorem reviewed in B. We exclude a single-coordinate conjugacy replacement, not a path using all AC moves and changing both coordinates.

## 4. Independent computation and controls

`experiments/twisted_magnus.py` represents a polynomial by its coefficient bits. It precomputes the four affine transformations as linear columns in the 63-dimensional coefficient space, then exhausts the orbit. It also independently enumerates H and computes the order of alpha on its two generators. The stored SHA-256 of the sorted orbit bytes is

    7a4eccec658ad9c31c5121ae71c53a3d7c1ddcd1515c130737058fb774862011.

`experiments/twisted_magnus_independent.cpp` was written separately. It builds its multiplication table from **literal monomial strings** and their concatenations, and applies direct left and right polynomial products for every orbit edge. It uses neither the Python concatenation-index formula nor the precomputed transformation columns. It independently reproduces 65,536 states, candidate order eight, and zero hits.

A less discriminating degree-four quotient is a useful positive comparison. Both implementations give 2,048 orbit states and candidate residues k=0,7 modulo eight. Passing to degree five removes those survivors. This catches an implementation that merely rejects every candidate regardless of input.

There are also exact inverse-substitution controls and direct original-word checks. A state-budget stop is explicitly marked incomplete and cannot support the theorem. The recorded degree-five run is complete.

An earlier class-two calculation gives another consistency check. In coordinates a^s d^t [a,d]^z, with [a,d]=a^-1 d^-1 a d, W has coordinates (-1,3,5) and C^k has (-k,k,k(k+1)/2). A conjugator must have (s,t)=(-k-1,-2), and the remaining central equation is

    2z+2=k(k+1)/2.

Thus class two only restricts k to 0 or 3 modulo four; the degree-four residues 0,7 modulo eight satisfy this condition. Neither weaker calculation by itself proves the degree-five exclusion.

## 5. Exact entry identity and a symmetry corollary

For a separate direct free-group check, r b^-1 is a product of four conjugates of p or p^-1. The (sign,conjugator) pairs are

    (-1,xYYYY), (+1,xYYY), (-1,xyxYYYY), (+1,xyxYYYYY).

Multiplying the four conjugates and freely reducing gives r b^-1 exactly. This provides a finite ordinary-AC expansion of the entry replacement while retaining p; it is an upper bound of four relative cells, not a minimal-area assertion. The coordinating agent has independently obtained another four-cell identity.

The second explicit length-eight word s=xyXXYxxY inherits the same primitive obstruction. Apply global inversion sigma(x)=X, sigma(y)=Y. The word s is rotation by four of sigma(r)^-1, while sigma(p) is a rotation of p^-1. Free automorphisms preserve primitivity and transport quotient conjugacy, so the theorem for r transfers to s. This corollary does not claim that r and s classify all short departures; any such classification is separate work.
