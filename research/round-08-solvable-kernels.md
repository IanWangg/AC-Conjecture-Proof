# Ordinary AC lifting through solvable normal kernels in every rank

2026-11-19. Let $G$ be generated by $B=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$, and let $D\lhd G$ be solvable with $D\subseteq[G,G]$. For any $n$-tuple $A$, its ordinary AC-equivalence to $B$ in $G/D$ lifts to ordinary AC-equivalence in $G$. The ambient group may be infinite and nonsolvable. This extends the [round-five rank-two solvable result](round-05-finite-kernel.md) to arbitrary finite rank. The proof uses a cyclic quotient determined by the frozen rows at each correction.

## Abelian kernel

Suppose first that $D$ is abelian. Write $A=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, where all but at most one $a_i$ agree with $b_i$ modulo $D$, and every $a_i$ agrees with $b_i$ in the abelianization. If there is an exceptional row, correct it first.

To correct row $i$, freeze all other current rows and write $K=\langle\!\langle a_j:j\ne i\rangle\!\rangle_G$. In $Q=G/K$, each image of $b_j$ with $j\ne i$ lies in $E=\operatorname{im}(D\to Q)$ because $a_j$ agrees with $b_j$ modulo $D$. Therefore $Q=\langle E,h\rangle$ with $h=\bar b_i$. The subgroup $E$ is abelian and normal. The map

$$
E\longrightarrow E,\qquad e\longmapsto h^{-1} e h e^{-1}
$$

is a homomorphism. Its image is normal in $Q$, since conjugation by $h$ preserves it and $E$ centralizes it. The quotient by this image is abelian, so the image is exactly $[Q,Q]$.

The difference $\bar b_i^{-1}\bar a_i$ belongs to $[Q,Q]$ by the abelianization hypothesis. Therefore $\bar a_i=e h e^{-1}$ for some $e\in E$. Conjugate the actual row $a_i$ by the inverse of any lift of $e$. The adjusted row now agrees with $b_i$ modulo $K$. Their exact difference is a product of conjugates of the frozen rows and their inverses. Multiply the adjusted row by those factors, restoring each donor after use. This gives $b_i$ exactly. At the next correction all other rows still agree with their targets modulo $D$, so the procedure continues.

This argument even gives a two-stage correction for the exceptional row while every other row is frozen. It uses a conjugation and multiplication by factors from the normal closure of the frozen donors.

## Solvable kernel

Lift a given quotient path from $G/D$ to $G$, choosing lifts of its conjugators. Its endpoint agrees with $B$ modulo $D$ and in the abelianization because $D\subseteq[G,G]$. Induct on the derived length of $D$. For $D'=[D,D]$, use the abelian argument in $G/D'$ with kernel $D/D'$. Lift those moves, leaving a tuple that agrees with $B$ modulo $D'$. Now invoke induction on $D'$. Projection proves the converse implication.

The same induction works for an initial tuple with one exceptional row that agrees only in the abelianization. For a solvable kernel outside $[G,G]$, apply the theorem to $D\cap[G,G]$. In this form the quotient test is the joint image of $G/D$ and the abelianization of $G$. The proof also combines with the earlier noncentral minimal normal-factor argument along an ascending $G$-normal series with abelian or minimal normal factors. At a limit term the finitely many coordinate errors lie in an earlier term. [Finite-kernel and minimal-normal results](round-05-finite-kernel.md).

Guyot's theorem proves ordinary AC-equivalence for two normally generating tuples in a finitely generated solvable group when their abelianized tuples are Nielsen equivalent. The relative criterion here works with a generating target and a solvable kernel inside an arbitrary ambient group. [Guyot, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06912).

## Exact rank-three control

In $A_5\times\mathrm{BS}(1,2)\times\mathbb Z$ use the [round-five marks](round-05-finite-kernel.md#exact-control-with-nonsolvable-quotient-and-infinite-kernel) for $x,y$ and take $z$ in the last factor. The dyadic translation group $D=\mathbb Z[1/2]$ is an infinite abelian normal subgroup. The word $v=[x,y]^6 y^{-5}$ has trivial permutation and translates by one. Start from

$$
(xv,\ yv^{-1},\ z[xv,yv^{-1}]).
$$

The last row has a nontrivial error in $A_5$, outside $D$. It is corrected using the other two rows. At this point those two frozen donors for the subsequent $x$ correction project to the identity in $\mathrm{BS}(1,2)$, while the first row projects to the dilation with translation two. Thus donor multiplication alone cannot correct that row in this projection. Conjugation by $v^2$ does so. A final donor correction then fixes the second row.
