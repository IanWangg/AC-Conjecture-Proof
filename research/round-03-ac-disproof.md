# Round 03 · Ordinary Andrews–Curtis disproof

2026-11-19. The two-copy method now covers **every admissible all-positive or all-negative shortest first bridge**, with any lobe rotations and no length bound. A regular-language test certifies many additional signed bridges. A separate family of rank-three overgroups proves the same exclusion for bridge words with repeated lobe readings.

Put $p=x^3y^{-4}$ and $b=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}$. Let $\Gamma$ be the folded barbell of two independently conjugated initial AK(3) relators with disjoint axes. Its lobes carry rotations $P,B$ of $p,b$ and its bridge reads $q$. The conclusion for each certified $\Gamma$ is that any two elements of its subgroup, independently conjugated in $F(x,y)$, generate an ambient-primitive-free subgroup. Thus an arbitrary Nielsen prelude to this second conjugation phase and a Nielsen suffix cannot produce a primitive. The result concerns this two-phase pattern. [Round 02 two-copy reduction](round-02-ac-disproof.md#finite-join-in-place-of-the-infinite-voltage-kernel), [Kapovich and Myasnikov on folding and product graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202285).

## Allowing repeated full-bridge readings

Assume $|q|\ge7$ and that each signed rotation of either lobe reads from exactly one vertex of $\Gamma$. Every subgroup generated by two rotations of lobe words is already certified primitive-free, allowing two choices from the same lobe. The 127 exact tests in round 02 cover this fact. Inversions change neither generated subgroup.

For distinct vertices $a,b$, form the component $C$ of $(a,b)$ in the labelled fibre product of $\Gamma$ with itself. It misses the diagonal, since paths can be read backward in the folded graph. Each projection is an immersion. A nontrivial reduced closed path in a barbell contains a contiguous simple lobe circuit. Therefore a cycle of $C$, or a reduced path between distinct vertices with the same projection, would give a signed lobe rotation readable from two distinct vertices of $\Gamma$. Both are impossible. Thus $C$ is a tree and both projections embed it. The folded join of the two rebased copies is their pushout along this tree. Indeed, every identified pair is forced by equal-label paths from $(a,b)$. After making exactly these identifications, common outgoing labels already agree by the defining property of $C$, so no further folds remain.

If neither tree image contains a complete bridge, choose an edge of the bridge outside that image in each copy. Each chosen edge remains separating in the pushout because the copy is glued to the other only on one side of the deleted edge. Now suppose a tree image contains a complete bridge. The corresponding path in the other copy reads $q$. If it used no bridge edge, it would lie in one lobe. Its length at least seven would then exhibit a signed lobe rotation as a substring of $q$, giving a second reading of that rotation in the actual first bridge. Thus at least one edge of the tree maps to a bridge edge in both copies. That shared edge is also separating. Removing it splits each copy between its two lobes, and each side of the common tree is glued entirely within its respective pair of sides.

In either case, each component left after the separating cuts has at most one lobe from each copy. Two disjoint lobes are connected by a tree path. Two intersecting lobes meet in a connected arc, possibly a single vertex. An embedded common tree meets a lobe in a connected set, because leaving a lobe and returning would revisit its only attachment vertex. The intersection of the resulting subtrees is connected. No additional cycles are introduced by the tree gluing. A cyclic primitive cannot use a separating edge because it would read both a letter and its inverse. This uses the signed monotonicity of rank-two primitive conjugacy classes. The only remaining two-lobe case has intersecting axes and is one of the rotation-pair tests. A single lobe passes the same test using a repeated choice. Therefore the folded join is primitive-free for all $a,b$. The case $a=b$ uses the original barbell and the same separating-edge argument. [Primary primitive-class theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.6574), [exact lobe-pair checks](../results/round02_ac_disproof_two_copies.json).

The argument uses a shared separating edge in precisely the case that required a unique full-bridge reading in round 02. For example, the bridge $x^{18}$ with lobes **YYYYxxx** and **xyxYXY** has five distinct full readings. A direct finite-map audit of every rebased join supports the new conclusion.

## Exact regular-language condition

Use uppercase letters for inverses. There are 26 signed lobe rotations, of lengths six and seven. For $|q|\ge7$, each has a unique reading if and only if:

1. No signed lobe rotation occurs as a contiguous subword of $q$. This also excludes occurrences in $q^{-1}$ because the rotation set is inverse-closed.
2. The two endpoint graphs pass an exact test. Use the first seven letters of $q$ as the outward tail at $P$ and the inverse of the last seven letters as the outward tail at $B$. In each graph consisting of its lobe and this tail, each signed rotation of that lobe must read from exactly one vertex and each signed rotation of the other lobe must have zero readings.

Indeed, a path reading a rotation either stays within the bridge or uses a lobe edge. In the latter case its length prevents it from using the other lobe and confines it to the corresponding endpoint graph. The expected readings already exist on their own lobe circles.

The check needs just a finite table of endpoint tails and a suffix automaton that forbids the 26 rotations. For the table, enumerate all $4\cdot3^6$ reduced seven-letter words and the seven power and six braid rotations, with the folded endpoint directions enforced. Every admissible outward tail in **xy** or in **XY** passes. For each of those two alphabets, the table checks 448 power endpoints and 384 braid endpoints. The signed lobe rotations all use both a positive and a negative letter, so the interior test holds automatically on either same-sign alphabet. Lengths below seven are within the complete round-two cap. Thus every admissible same-sign bridge of every length is covered.

For general signed inputs, start with each admissible guarded power prefix and advance through the six- and seven-letter forbidden subwords while retaining the last seven letters. Weight each final suffix by the number of guarded braid attachments. This counts literal triples $(P,q,B)$, including possible isomorphic source cores. Examples follow. The counts use the proved language condition at arbitrary length, without requiring a finite search over joins at those lengths.

## An overgroup certificate for repeated lobe readings

There is a separate certificate when the lobe-rotation condition fails. Make a chain of three circles. The outer labels are $P,B$ and the middle one is a signed rotation $R$ of $p$ or $b$. The connecting edges have single signed labels $c,d$, read from left to right. Require that the four incident labels $c^{-1},d$, the first letter of $R$, and the inverse of its last letter are pairwise distinct. At $P$, require $c$ to differ from both outgoing lobe labels. At $B$, require the same of $d^{-1}$. The resulting graph is already folded. At its left marked vertex the subgroup is

$$
H_{P,R,B,c,d}=\langle P,\ cRc^{-1},\ cdB(cd)^{-1}\rangle .
$$

All 552 literal folded chains pass the two-copy test. The native check tests all 100,092 unordered pairs of distinct vertices. Each join passes the signed double-free DAG certificate from round 02. There are 3,880 rank-five and 96,212 rank-six joins. An independent Python check constructs subgroups from the rebased six words and exactly audits all 1,596 joins of nine selected chains, including the explicit one below.

Now let $q$ be any reduced word labelling a path from the left marked vertex to the right marked vertex in one of these chains, subject to the outer endpoint guards. The subgroup $\langle P,qBq^{-1}\rangle$ is contained in the left-based chain subgroup. The conclusion about arbitrary pairs and independent conjugations is inherited by subgroups. In particular all $q=cR^m d$ for $m\in\mathbb Z$ are certified. For $|m|\ge2$ these bridges violate the interior regular-language test.

For an explicit example take $P=$ **xxxYYYY**, $B=$ **xyxYXY**, $R=$ **YXYxyx**, $c=$ **X**, and $d=$ **y**. The saved control independently folds and checks the whole overgroup, and verifies the free-word identity expressing the second relator for $m=3$ as a conjugate of the third generator by the cube of the second. The readable chains yield exponentially many witnesses beyond isolated powers. For each of $s$ positions independently choose the central loop $RR$ or $R d B d^{-1}$, then append $R$ before following $d$ to the right lobe. Starting with $c$, the paths are reduced by the incidence guards. Their labels are distinct because the graph is folded and the choices traverse different edges immediately after the first $R$. For $s\ge1$ every bridge contains a full signed lobe word and fails the interior language test.

This is a finite overgroup method for a restricted bridge language. It does not supply a finite cover for arbitrary signed bridges or control arbitrary alternating phases. [Round 02 finite-cover limitation](round-02-barbell-cover.md).
