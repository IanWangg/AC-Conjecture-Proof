# Round 09 stable AC proof track

One stabilization makes the ordinary Andrews–Curtis component invariant under every automorphism of the stabilized free group. Compiling this fact reduces the earlier chart paths from millions of moves to thousands and keeps every path at rank at most three. A new 544-move path enters a marked chart at cyclic peak 22. Its energy-20 projection has been independently replayed in 1,871 strict stable moves. These results give stable waypoints for AK(3).

## Ambient automorphisms after one stabilization

Let \(R\) be an \(n\)-tuple normally generating \(F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), and let \(S=(R,z)\). The automorphisms \(\alpha\) with \(\alpha(S)\sim_{\mathrm{AC}}S\) form a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n+1})\). Indeed, applying an automorphism to an ordinary proof preserves multiplication and inversion and sends a conjugation to a conjugation by the image word. Single-letter conjugations expand the latter when needed.

For a Nielsen shear \(x_i\mapsto x_i z^{\pm1}\) or \(x_i\mapsto z^{\pm1}x_i\), keep the last relator \(z\) and substitute each occurrence using a conjugate of that donor. For example, if one occurrence in the row \(u x_i v\) becomes \(x_i z\), right multiplication by \(v^{-1}zv\) gives the prescribed replacement. The left and inverse-letter versions insert the conjugate at the corresponding position. For \(z\mapsto z x_i^{\pm1}\), use a normal-generation expression for \(x_i^{\pm1}\) over \(R\) and multiply its factors into the singleton. For \(z\mapsto x_i^{\pm1}z\), use the conjugate of this expression by \(z^{-1}\). The map \(z\mapsto z^{-1}\) is a single inversion of the last relator. Every one of these automorphisms lies in the subgroup.

Write \(E:x_i\mapsto x_i z\), \(E':x_i\mapsto z x_i\), \(F:z\mapsto x_i^{-1}z\), and \(I_z:z\mapsto z^{-1}\), with all unspecified letters fixed. The composition \(E'FEI_z\), read right to left, exchanges \(x_i\) and \(z\). Conjugating the already available shears and inversions by these exchanges gives the remaining elementary Nielsen generators. Thus the ordinary component of \(S\) is invariant under all ambient automorphisms. The statement applies in every rank to the once-stabilized tuple. Panteleev and Ushakov prove the corresponding ordinary invariance for the unstabilized AK family in \(F_2\). [Panteleev–Ushakov, Proposition 3.7 and Corollary 3.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.00325)

If an ordinary rank-three prefix connects \(S\) to a triple \(T\) with a primitive row, choose an automorphism \(\alpha\) which sends that row to a generator up to cyclic normalization. First compile the path from \(S\) to \(\alpha(S)\), then replay the original prefix after applying \(\alpha\) to every conjugator. At \(\alpha(T)\), normalize the selected row, clear its letter from the other rows, and delete it. This gives a strict stable path at maximum rank three to the corresponding projection. It uses normal-generation expressions at the initial tuple. A size bound for the resulting cyclic peak need not match the original prefix peak.

### Endomorphisms preserving the component

An endomorphism also transports an ordinary ledger. Suppose it fixes \(z\) and induces an automorphism of \(F_n\) after killing \(z\). Apply the ambient lift of that automorphism first. Each of the other rows then agrees with the desired row modulo the normal closure of \(z\), and the fixed \(z\) donor allows the correcting products. In a second case, suppose the endomorphism fixes all the old generators and sends \(z\) to a word with \(z\)-exponent \(e=\pm1\). The normal closure of \(R\) in \(F_{n+1}\) is the kernel of the map onto \(F(z)\) that kills the old generators. Invert the singleton when \(e=-1\) and multiply its row by the normal product for \(z^{-e}\phi(z)\). This reaches \(\phi(S)\) by ordinary moves. Thus both families preserve the ordinary component of \(S\), as does the monoid they generate. Conjugating the second family by signed ambient permutations permits the replacement of any one generator by a word with self-exponent \(\pm1\), while holding the other generators fixed.

Unimodular endomorphisms cannot create a primitive projection. In fact, let \(\phi:F_n\to F_n\) induce an automorphism on abelianization. Its image subgroup \(H\) is generated by \(n\) elements and \(H_{\rm ab}\) surjects onto \(\mathbb Z^n\), so \(H\) is free of rank \(n\). Folding the generating loops of \(H\) from a subdivided rank-\(n\) rose to its core uses only rank-preserving folds, each a homotopy equivalence. Hence \(\phi\) identifies \(F_n\) with \(H\). A primitive element \(p'\) of \(F_n\) lying in \(H\) is primitive in \(H\). In the subgroup cover of a rose marked with \(p'\) as a basis letter, \(p'\) forms a loop at the base vertex, and the basis from a spanning tree contains this loop. [Stallings, Topology of Finite Graphs](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02095993)

If \(\phi(p)\) is primitive, the subgroup argument shows that \(p\) is primitive. The induced map \(F_n/\langle\!\langle p\rangle\!\rangle\to F_n/\langle\!\langle\phi(p)\rangle\!\rangle\) has free domain and target of rank \(n-1\). Its abelianization is unimodular because the original abelian isomorphism sends the direct summand generated by \([p]\) to that generated by \([\phi(p)]\). Thus the induced map is injective by the same argument. If a retained row projects to a primitive element in the target quotient, that element is also primitive in the image subgroup, and so the row already projected to a primitive in the original quotient. The handlebody-cover argument for geometric multiwords and the finite substitution screen are in the [companion substitution note](round-09-stable-substitutions.md).

## Reproduction

Regenerate the cap-22 states and seeds by the [Round 06 instructions](round-06-stable-proof.md), and the verified ambient anchors from the ordinary cap-29 and cap-30 runs. For the low-energy geometry inputs, retain rows whose three lengths sum to at most the stated threshold and duplicate the three columns to supply the predecessor columns expected by the chart screen. The low-energy entries came directly from the full orbit catalogue. The predecessor display for this reuse of the chart screen records the same source triple.

~~~sh
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 experiments/round06_stable_proof_strict_verify.cpp -o "$work/strict-verify"
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 -pthread experiments/round09_stable_proof_excursion_path.cpp -o "$work/excursion-path"
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 -pthread experiments/round09_stable_proof_primitive_catalogue.cpp -o "$work/primitive-catalogue"
c++ -std=c++17 -O3 experiments/round03_ak3_escape_path.cpp -o "$work/pair-route"
"$work/excursion-path" 22 4000000 "$seed22" XXXYY XYXZy XzyZZ > "$work/e20.orbit.tsv"
printf '%s\t%s\n' XXXXYYY XYxYXy > "$work/e20.pair.tsv"
"$work/pair-route" 21 "$work/e20.pair.tsv" "$work/e20.pair.json"
python3 -B experiments/round09_stable_proof_lift_excursion.py "$work/e20.pair.json" "$work/e20.orbit.tsv" "$work/e20.rank3.json"
python3 -B experiments/round09_stable_proof_rank_three_chart.py "$work/e20.rank3.json" 2 "$work/e20.strict.jsonl" --literal y
"$work/strict-verify" "$work/e20.strict.jsonl"
"$work/primitive-catalogue" "$states22" 22
"$work/primitive-catalogue" "$states22" 22 "$work/chartless22.tsv"
python3 -B experiments/round09_stable_proof_chartless_geometry.py "$work/chartless22.tsv"
~~~

Pass the Round 06 intrinsic prefix with pivot slot 0 or the Round 08 intrinsic prefix with slot 1 to the chart compiler. Use `--use-swaps-for-shears` for the swap variants. For the Round 07 literal charts pass each published prefix with its pivot slot and literal letter, respectively `2 x`, `1 y`, and `2 x`. The independent cap-22 enclosure checker remains underway.
