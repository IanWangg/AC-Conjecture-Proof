# Lifting AC paths through derived subgroups of nilpotent normal kernels

2026-11-19. The existing rank-two certificate for AK(3) in the braid relation-module quotient implies AC triviality in every lower-central quotient of the braid kernel. These ambient quotients are infinite and nonsolvable. This rules out component separation in that family and narrows the quotient approach to a distance problem.

## A lifting lemma

Let $P\triangleleft G$ be nilpotent, and put $P'=[P,P]$. Suppose two equal-length tuples $A,B$ have AC-equivalent images in $G/P'$ and that $B$ generates $G$ as a group. Then $A$ and $B$ are AC equivalent in $G$.

First, if a subgroup $J\le P$ satisfies $JP'=P$, it equals $P$. Induct on the nilpotency class, using the projection to $P/Z(P)$. Induction gives $JZ(P)=P$. Central factors drop out of commutators, so $P'=[J,J]\subseteq J$ and $J=P$. The abelian case is immediate. This argument needs no finite generation of $P$. If $H\le G$ surjects onto $G/P'$, then $HP'=G$ and $(H\cap P)P'=P$. Hence $H\cap P=P$ and $H=G$.

Lift the AC path from $G/P'$ to $G$, lifting each conjugator arbitrarily. Its endpoint $C$ agrees with $B$ modulo $P'$. Every hybrid tuple obtained by taking some coordinates of $B$ and the remaining coordinates of $C$ therefore generates $G$ by the preceding paragraph. To change a coordinate $C_i$ to $B_i$, freeze the other current coordinates. The quotient of $G$ by their normal closure is cyclic, since it is generated by the remaining coordinate. The difference $C_i^{-1}B_i$ lies in $P'\subseteq [G,G]$ and is consequently a product of conjugates of the frozen coordinates and their inverses. Multiplying in these factors one at a time gives ordinary AC moves. Each conjugated donor is restored after use. Repeat over the coordinates. This proves the lemma. The single-row updates are the usual M-transformations compiled into ordinary moves. [Guyot, §1 and §2](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06912).

The group-generation assumption on $B$ is used in every hybrid. Nilpotence cannot be replaced by solubility in the subgroup argument. For example, a transposition subgroup of $S_3$ surjects onto its abelianization and remains proper.

## Application to the braid kernel

Let $F=F(x,y)$, let $b=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}$, and let $N=\langle\!\langle b\rangle\!\rangle_F$. For $m\ge 1$, put

$$
G_m=F/\gamma_{m+1}(N),
\qquad P_m=N/\gamma_{m+1}(N),
$$

where $\gamma_1(N)=N$ and $\gamma_{j+1}(N)=[N,\gamma_j(N)]$. Then $P_m$ is nilpotent and $G_m/P_m'=F/[N,N]$. The existing 533-move certificate sends AK(3) to the marked standard basis in $F/[N,N]$. Apply the lemma with that same lifted path and the target basis in each $G_m$. Thus AK(3) and $(x,y)$ lie in the same ordinary AC component in every $G_m$. All $G_m$ map onto $B_3=F/N$, which in turn maps onto the nonsolvable group $C_2*C_3$. [First-order lift and certificate](research-l.md), [independent second-central audit](research-m-second-central-review.md).

The earlier nonzero second-central errors distinguish the saved endpoint from the basis. They do not prevent further donor multiplications. In particular $G_2$ retains the full $\gamma_2(N)/\gamma_3(N)$ layer, and its quotient $F/[F,N']$ retains the previously computed coinvariants. Both admit a completion by the lifting lemma.

## Constructive replacements in each layer

The proof gives a direct compiler for the marked free basis case. Let a hybrid tuple define a substitution $\sigma$ on $F$ taking each marked basis element to the corresponding hybrid relator. It is the identity modulo $N'$, so it preserves $N$ and induces the identity on $N/N'$. Commutator collection shows that it induces the identity on each $\gamma_j(N)/\gamma_{j+1}(N)$, since that layer is generated by iterated commutators of $j$ elements of $N$ and their classes depend only on the abelianizations of the entries.

For a marked basis element $x_i$, start with the formal word $p_2=x_i$ on the basis symbols. Given $\sigma(p_j)\equiv x_i\bmod \gamma_j(N)$, take the freely reduced error word $e_j=\sigma(p_j)^{-1}x_i$ and use $p_{j+1}=p_j e_j$. Then

$$
\sigma(p_{j+1})=\sigma(p_j)\sigma(e_j)
 \equiv \sigma(p_j)e_j=x_i\pmod{\gamma_{j+1}(N)}.
$$

At the desired level, the correction word $x_i^{-1}p_{m+1}$ has $x_i$ exponent sum zero. Reading it from left to right gives a normal-closure factorization by the other basis symbols. An occurrence of $x_j^{\pm1}$ for $j\ne i$ contributes that signed donor conjugated by $x_i$ raised to the accumulated $x_i$ exponent. The final remaining power of $x_i$ is zero. Evaluating these factors in the hybrid tuple and using frozen donors gives the required AC path. Thus the proof does not require an unbounded search for normal-closure witnesses at each finite level.

For $G_2$ the compiler follows the 533-move prefix with 316 conjugates of the second donor and 942 of the first. The checker factors the two correction words, evaluates them, and computes the residuals using a Schreier basis for the braid kernel. It uses first-visit edges of the actual braid Cayley paths as a tree and ignores precisely those edges when reading Schreier letters. This finite tree extends to a spanning tree. In a free group the vanishing of degrees one and two detects membership in its third lower-central subgroup: degree one gives the abelianization, and the basic commutators have independent antisymmetric degree-two tensors. Both corrected residuals pass these tests. The verifier uses the quotient equality with $x$ from the first correction when evaluating the second. ## Distance and scope

Use single-letter conjugations and the other finite instructions of the [round-one distance metric](round-01-magnus-distance.md). For AK(3), let $D_m$ be the distance between its marked image and the basis in $G_m$. Each $D_m$ is finite and they are nondecreasing. For any fixed move bound $L$, all the reachable word differences are separated by sufficiently high $m$: $\gamma_{m+1}(N)\subseteq \gamma_{m+1}(F)$, and $\gamma_{m+1}(F)$ maps to the identity under the degree-$m$ Magnus expansion. The explicit free-word separation and bounded lifting argument of round one apply. Thus the free distance is $\sup_m D_m$. The same conclusion follows from the finite instruction alphabet and $\bigcap_m\gamma_{m+1}(N)=1$. The individual completions in the $G_m$ can depend on $m$. A uniform bound on their strict move counts would imply an actual free-group path.
