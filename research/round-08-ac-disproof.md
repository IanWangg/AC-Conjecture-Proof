# Four-letter middle bridges and nested basis words

2026-11-19. The third-phase exclusion now covers bridges over the full reduced alphabet. Every one of the 317 previously certified actual second-lobe classes has a marked family with at least 184,184 independent choices for each 16-letter bridge block, each containing both signs of both ambient generators. An explicit mass-$(4,3)$ path reaches an 82-edge entirely unread bridge in such a family. A separate theorem shows that, after a disjoint placement, every non-coordinate intrinsic basis has nested ambient cyclic words. This forces a second marker reading after any subsequent disjoint placement of that basis, at any intrinsic mass. ## Exact bridge language at every positive length

Let $P,Q$ be marked cyclically reduced labels on the two circles of an immersed barbell, and let the nonempty bridge read $q$ from $P$ to $Q$. Put $L=\max(|P|,|Q|)$ and let $\mathcal R$ be the union of the signed rotations of $P,Q$. Assume the bare circle of each lobe has exactly one reading of every own signed rotation and no reading of an opposite-lobe rotation. The reads here include open paths. Impose the following conditions on the bridge.

1. The word $q$ is reduced and its outward initial directions at both marked vertices are unoccupied by the corresponding circles.
2. No $r\in\mathcal R$ occurs inside $q$.
3. At each endpoint, for every split $r=uv$ with nonempty $u,v$, forbid outward prefix $v$ if $u$ reads on that circle ending at the attachment. The outward words at the two endpoints are $q$ and $q^{-1}$.
4. For every split $r=u w v$ into three nonempty pieces, forbid $q=w$ if $u$ reads on the left circle ending at its attachment and $v$ reads on the right circle beginning at its attachment.

These conditions characterize immersed nonempty bridges for which every marker has its unique reading. To see this, a reading using bridge edges and lobe edges cannot contain an entire circuit of either lobe as a proper subpath. That would place a rotation of the shorter lobe inside a rotation of the longer one, contrary to the bare-circle condition. A return to a bridge attachment after following lobe edges requires such a full circuit. Therefore every additional reading is internal to the bridge, crosses exactly one attachment, or traverses the whole bridge and uses edges of both lobes. Inverse closure of $\mathcal R$ lets the one-attachment tests run outward and the two-attachment tests run left to right. Condition 4 has support in lengths at most $L-2$.

The unique-reading condition is also equivalent to all off-diagonal self-fiber-product components being trees that embed under both projections. For the forward direction, a cycle or failure of projection injectivity would give a nonempty reduced path that is closed under one projection. This path includes a full lobe circuit, whose simultaneous reading starts at a different vertex in the other projection. For the converse, pair the expected closed marker path with its second reading. If the second reading closes, the off-diagonal component contains a cycle. If it ends at a different vertex, the first projection identifies the two distinct endpoints of the product path. Thus the language is exact for the common-tree step of the two-copy argument. Every distinct-vertex join in this language is a rank-four pushout along a tree and carries the free-product marking used by the fixed-witness criterion. [Rank-four criterion](round-06-ac-disproof.md).

For primitive exclusion, retain the rotation-pair hypothesis of Round 07. Its separating cuts apply whenever $q$ cannot be read along either bare circle. This includes every $|q|\ge L$ that passes the guard. In the case requiring a shared cut, one tree projection contains the entire bridge. Traverse it from the left attachment to the right. If the other projection uses a bridge edge on the matched path, deleting that shared edge in the pushout separates the two sides of the common tree. The remaining case aligns the complete bridge with a path contained in a bare circle. Thus each candidate bridge is a proper prefix of a signed rotation. Every candidate fiber component contains a pair of the left attachment and a vertex reading $q$ inside its lobe, up to swapping the copies. Testing these pairs suffices because a product path conjugates both based loop groups by its common label.

## Exact counting and four-sign families

Use a deterministic automaton whose state is the longest suffix of the current word that is a prefix of a forbidden internal word or a forbidden terminal suffix. Reject transitions that complete a marker or an adjacent inverse pair. Test right endpoint guards at termination, and add a finite initial trie to enforce the left endpoint guards. If $A$ is the resulting edge-count matrix, $s$ the initial state vector and $t$ the indicator of terminal states, then the number of accepted bridges of positive length $n$ is $s^{\mathsf T} A^n t-\epsilon_n$, where $\epsilon_n$ counts the words accepted by the automaton but forbidden because they cross both lobes. Only finitely many $\epsilon_n$ are nonzero. The length generating function is rational.

Each of the 317 certified lobe classes has a stored marking with an admissible padded bridge $c^L d^L$. For each such marking, use the automaton for internal factors only and let $v$ be the state after reading $c^L$. Every sequence of 16-letter return walks at $v$, followed by $d^L$, gives a bridge in the exact language. The padding shields both endpoint tests, while the return states certify the bridge interior across all block boundaries. Determinism makes the return labels distinct. The weakest marking has 244,963 return labels, including 184,184 labels containing all four signs. Every marked family therefore has at least $184184^m$ bridges of length $2L+16m$ with all four signs in every block, a growth rate of at least $184184^{1/16}>2.13$ on those lengths.

The detailed control uses $P=\texttt{XXXXYxxxYYYYxxxYYYYxyxyyy}$, $Q=\texttt{XXyxyXYXyyyyXXXyyyyX}$ and padding $x^{25},y^{25}$. It has 265,721 length-16 return labels, including 204,942 that contain all four signed letters by exact inclusion–exclusion. At length 128 it has 72778868912256580976599800190582646061674929674477357862615 such four-sign returns. Using only the four-sign labels as independent blocks gives a growth rate above $2.883$ along lengths $50+128m$. The 82-edge bridge $x^{25}(\texttt{YXXyyyyXXXyxxxxx})^2y^{25}$ gives a 148-move strict ordinary entry from AK(3). It has zero readable prefix or suffix at the selected lifts in the first source. Forty full rebased joins were folded and independently excluded by 25,980 exact partial-map states. [Bridge counts and strict entry](../results/round08_ac_disproof_bridge_language.json).

Direct folds of 4,365 immersed short barbells verify the automaton counts and marker tests for two markings. With $P=\texttt{XXXyyyy}$ and $Q=\texttt{XYXyxy}$, the bridge $q=\texttt{xyX}$ passes the immersion, internal and one-ended guards. It creates second readings of `xyxYXY` and `yxyXYX` across both lobes. This checks that the two-ended test is required at short lengths. In the baseline and mass-$(4,3)$ markings, 14 and 338 guarded bridges can also be read in a bare circle. Their 497 potentially exceptional alignment joins all pass the independent signed double-free graph test. Thus both markings have the third-phase exclusion for every nonempty bridge in their exact languages.

## Why a nontrivial basis change breaks unique lobe readings

**Theorem.** Suppose a source rank-two free basis is given, up to simultaneous conjugation, by the two lobe loops of an immersed barbell connected by a bridge. For any new simultaneous intrinsic Nielsen basis other than the original coordinate slopes up to signs and order, one ambient cyclic word has a signed rotation that occurs as a proper linear subword of a signed rotation of the other. Consequently independently conjugating the new basis to a disjoint placement always gives two bare circles that fail the marker test.

Write the new basis slopes as two unimodular columns in the old basis. By choosing the orientations of the old generators and inverting output rows, both columns can be made nonnegative. If both columns have both coordinates nonzero, differing relative signs would make the determinant have absolute value at least two. If one column lies on an axis, orient it to agree with the other column there. Two nonnegative unimodular columns are componentwise comparable except at the original coordinate pair. In fact if $a=c+s$ and $d=b+t$ with $s,t>0$, then the crossed columns $(a,b),(c,d)$ have determinant $ct+sb+st$. Its value can be one only when $c=b=0$ and $s=t=1$. Repeated subtraction of the smaller column from the larger therefore reduces the pair to the coordinate columns, with strict decrease of total mass. Reversing those subtractions realizes the slopes by positive Nielsen additions.

At the first addition, take the common basepoint on the retained lobe. Represent the retained row by that lobe circuit and the changed row by the retained circuit followed by a bridge crossing, the other circuit, and the reverse bridge crossing. These are cyclically immersed words with the same first edge. At every later addition, either order of their positive concatenation is reduced and cyclically reduced because the two first edges agree. The resulting pair still has the same first edge. The final addition displays the entire retained word inside the changed word. Rank-two primitive conjugacy classes are determined by their intrinsic slopes, so this gives the ambient cyclic classes of the requested basis. [Osborne and Zieschang, Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389191).

The same positive-concatenation proof applies if the original axes meet only in a vertex. Reduced representatives based at that vertex have four distinct outgoing directions and the first addition leaves two cyclic words with a common first edge. If the original axes share an edge, the proof applies to the quadrant given by orienting both axes to traverse that edge in the same direction. In particular, for an initial intersection to produce genuinely new bare-unique lobes, the next non-coordinate basis must use a signed quadrant in which the shared edge has opposite orientations. When a first placement is disjoint the theorem applies to every intrinsic basis mass, extending the mass-four obstruction of Round 07.

The 82-edge control gives a strict example in which both rows change, with intrinsic slopes $(2,1)$ and $(3,2)$. Three positive Nielsen additions extend the ledger to 163 elementary moves, yielding a literal occurrence of the 234-letter row at the start of the 443-letter row. Independent Euclidean slope representatives check the predicted ambient cyclic classes and nesting in 504 cases, covering all non-coordinate positive unimodular pairs up to intrinsic mass 20 up to output order, with every orientation of the input barbell lobes. [Basis checks and strict replay](../results/round08_ac_disproof_bridge_language.json).

The bridge result excludes primitives after the third independent conjugation and later Nielsen operations for the stated actual placements. The nesting theorem identifies why the same marker condition cannot inductively cover a further disjoint placement after a non-coordinate basis change. Controlling the resulting nested overlaps is a separate requirement for an unrestricted AC obstruction.
